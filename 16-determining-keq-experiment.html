Practical Determination of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ via Colourimetry

Master the mandatory NESA Module 5 practical investigation. Learn how to experimentally determine the equilibrium constant ($K_{\mathrm{eq}}$) for the iron(III) thiocyanate system ($\mathrm{Fe^{3+}_{(aq)} + SCN^-_{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons FeSCN^{2+}_{(aq)}}$) using Beer-Lambert colourimetry, Le Chatelier calibration standards, dilution algebra, risk assessments, and error reduction strategies.

Syllabus Outcomes Addressed (Outcome CH-12-01 & CH11/12-2, 3, 4, 5):

  • Perform a practical investigation to determine the value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ for an equilibrium system using colourimetric or spectrophotometric techniques.

1. The Model System: Iron(III) Thiocyanate Complexation

Practical Foundation

Determining $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ experimentally requires an equilibrium system where at least one species possesses an intense, distinct optical property (such as visible light absorption) that allows its equilibrium concentration to be measured non-destructively without disturbing the chemical equilibrium.

The standard NESA practical investigation utilises the reversible formation of the mono-thiocyanatoiron(III) complex ion:

$$\mathrm{\underset{\text{Pale Yellow / Colourless}}{\mathrm{Fe^{3+}_{(aq)}}} + \underset{\text{Colourless}}{\mathrm{SCN^-_{(aq)}}} \rightleftharpoons \underset{\text{Intense Blood-Red}}{\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}_{(aq)}}}} \quad (\Delta H < 0)$$

Optical Characteristics of Species

• Iron(III) Ions ($\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$): Pale yellow in acidic aqueous solution (dilute $\mathrm{HNO_3}$).
• Thiocyanate Ions ($\mathrm{SCN^-}$): Completely colourless in solution.
• Thiocyanatoiron(III) Complex ($\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$): Intense blood-red / deep orange complex ion that strongly absorbs blue-green light ($\lambda_{\max} \approx 447\text{ nm}$).

The $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Expression

Because all reacting species are dissolved aqueous solutes ($\text{aq}$), the homogeneous equilibrium constant expression is:

$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{eq}}}{[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_{\text{eq}} [\mathrm{SCN^-}]_{\text{eq}}}$$

To evaluate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, we must determine the equilibrium molar concentrations of all three aqueous species at constant room temperature ($25^\circ\text{C}$).

2. Theoretical Principles of Colourimetry & Beer-Lambert Law

Analytical Technique

Colourimetry is an analytical technique used to determine the concentration of a coloured solute in solution by measuring the absorption of specific wavelengths of visible light passing through the sample.

The Beer-Lambert Law

When monochromatic light of wavelength $\lambda_{\max} = 447\text{ nm}$ (blue light, the complementary colour of red) passes through a cuvette containing the blood-red $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ complex, the absorbance ($A$) is directly proportional to the concentration ($c$) of the complex and the optical path length ($l$):

$$A = \varepsilon \cdot l \cdot c$$

Where:

  • $A$ = Absorbance (dimensionless log ratio $\log_{10}(I_0/I)$ measured by the colorimeter).
  • $\varepsilon$ = Molar absorptivity coefficient of $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ at $447\text{ nm}$ ($\text{L mol}^{-1} \text{cm}^{-1}$).
  • $l$ = Optical path length of the cuvette (standardised at $1.0\text{ cm}$).
  • $c$ = Molar concentration of $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$).

Linearity of Absorbance vs Concentration

Because $\varepsilon$ and $l$ remain constant for a given instrument and cuvette, the Beer-Lambert Law simplifies to a direct linear proportionality: $$A \propto [\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}] \implies A = k \cdot [\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$$
Plotting Absorbance ($A$) on the y-axis against known concentrations of $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ on the x-axis produces a linear calibration graph passing through the origin $(0,0)$.

3. Preparing Calibration Standards: The Le Chatelier Forced-Yield Strategy

Methodological Genius

A critical practical challenge arises when constructing the calibration curve: How do you prepare a solution with a KNOWN concentration of $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$ when the system is reversible and does not naturally react to completion?

The Le Chatelier Forced-Yield Strategy

To overcome reversibility, we prepare calibration standard solutions using a massive excess of iron(III) ions relative to thiocyanate ions.

• Standard Solutions Composition: $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_{\text{initial}} = 0.200\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ versus $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_{\text{initial}} = 0.000200\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ (a 1000-fold excess of $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$!).

• Le Chatelier Drive: According to Le Chatelier's Principle, adding an overwhelming excess of reactant $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ drives the equilibrium position so far to the RIGHT ($\to$) that $100\%$ of the limiting reactant $\mathrm{SCN^-}$ is converted into $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$.

• Stoichiometric Equality for Standards: $$[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{standard}} \approx [\mathrm{SCN^-}]_{\text{initial}}$$

Serial Dilution Protocol for Calibration Standards

A standard stock solution of $0.00200\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{KSCN}$ is diluted into volumetric flasks containing $0.200\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{Fe(NO_3)_3}$ in $0.5\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ to produce five standard solutions of known $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$:

4. Preparing Equilibrium Test Solutions & ICE Table Deduction

Quantitative Deduction

Once the calibration curve is established, we prepare Equilibrium Test Solutions where the initial concentrations of $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{SCN^-}$ are of the same order of magnitude (e.g. both approximately $0.00200\text{ M}$). Under these conditions, the reaction does NOT proceed to completion; instead, significant concentrations of all three species co-exist at equilibrium.

Algebraic Deduction Protocol:

  1. Determine Initial Concentrations ($[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_0$ and $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_0$): Use dilution algebra ($c_1 V_1 = c_2 V_2$) to calculate starting concentrations immediately after mixing in the test tube.
  2. Measure Absorbance ($A_{\text{meas}}$) with Colorimeter: Record absorbance at $447\text{ nm}$.
  3. Read $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{eq}}$ from Calibration Curve: Interpolate $A_{\text{meas}}$ on the linear curve to find the true equilibrium complex concentration $x$.
  4. Construct the ICE Table to Deduce Unreacted Species: $$\begin{aligned} [\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_{\text{eq}} &= [\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_0 - x \\ [\mathrm{SCN^-}]_{\text{eq}} &= [\mathrm{SCN^-}]_0 - x \end{aligned}$$
  5. Calculate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$: Substitute the three equilibrium concentrations into the expression: $$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{x}{([\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_0 - x)([\mathrm{SCN^-}]_0 - x)}$$

Initial ($\text{M}$) $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_0 = \frac{c_1 V_1}{V_{\text{total}}}$ $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_0 = \frac{c_2 V_2}{V_{\text{total}}}$ $0.00$

5. Risk Assessment, Controls, and Systematic Errors

Experimental Validity

Risk Assessment & Safety Protocol

Chemical / Hazard Specific Risk & Impact Safety Control Measures
Iron(III) Nitrate in $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ Corrosive to skin and eyes; causes severe burns and staining. Wear nitrile gloves, safety goggles, and lab coat at all times. Rinse spills with water immediately.
Potassium Thiocyanate ($\mathrm{KSCN}$) Toxic if ingested; contacts with acid release toxic hydrogen cyanide ($\mathrm{HCN}$) gas. Keep solution dilute; maintain acidic pH with dilute $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ in well-ventilated laboratory; wear gloves.
Glassware / Cuvettes Fragile optical glass can crack/cut skin. Fingerprints smudge cuvette optical face. Handle cuvettes only by frosted sides; wipe optical faces with lint-free Kimwipes before measurement.

Key Experimental Controls for Validity

  • Acidified Solutions ($0.5\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ Solvent): $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ ions undergo hydrolysis in neutral water to form insoluble brown precipitates ($\mathrm{Fe(OH)_{3(s)}}$). Maintaining acidic conditions prevents precipitate formation and stabilises $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}_{(aq)}}$.
  • Constant Temperature ($25^\circ\text{C}$): Since $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ is highly temperature-dependent (exothermic reaction), all cuvettes and volumetric flasks must be kept in the same temperature bath during colorimetric readings.
  • Wavelength Selection ($\lambda_{\max} = 447\text{ nm}$): Using blue light ensures maximum absorbance by red $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ while minimising optical interference from pale yellow $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$.

7. Scaffolded Worked Examples

HSC Step-by-Step Solutions

Worked Example 1 (Easy): Beer-Lambert Calibration Curve Interpolation

2 Marks

A student constructs a Beer-Lambert calibration curve for $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$ at $447\text{ nm}$. The linear regression equation is $A = 3750 \times [\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$.
An equilibrium test mixture yields an absorbance reading of $A = 0.450$.
Calculate the equilibrium concentration of $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$.

Model Solution:

1. Rearrange linear Beer-Lambert equation: $$[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{eq}} = \frac{A}{3750}$$

2. Substitute measured absorbance $A = 0.450$: $$[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{eq}} = \frac{0.450}{3750} = 1.20 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$

Worked Example 2 (Medium): Initial Concentration Dilution Algebra

3 Marks

A test solution is prepared by mixing $5.00\text{ mL}$ of $0.00200\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{Fe(NO_3)_3}$, $3.00\text{ mL}$ of $0.00200\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{KSCN}$, and $2.00\text{ mL}$ of $0.5\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ (total volume $10.00\text{ mL}$).
Calculate the initial concentrations $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_0$ and $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_0$ immediately after mixing.

Model Solution:

1. Calculate total volume: $$V_{\text{total}} = 5.00 + 3.00 + 2.00 = 10.00\text{ mL}$$

2. Apply dilution formula $c_2 = \frac{c_1 V_1}{V_2}$ for $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$: $$[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_0 = \frac{(0.00200\text{ M}) \times (5.00\text{ mL})}{10.00\text{ mL}} = \mathbf{1.00 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol L}^{-1}}$$

3. Apply dilution formula for $\mathrm{SCN^-}$: $$[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_0 = \frac{(0.00200\text{ M}) \times (3.00\text{ mL})}{10.00\text{ mL}} = \mathbf{6.00 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol L}^{-1}}$$

Worked Example 3 (Hard): Complete Practical $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ ICE Table Calculation

4 Marks

Using the initial concentrations from Example 2 ($[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_0 = 1.00 \times 10^{-3}\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_0 = 6.00 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$), colourimetric analysis of the equilibrium mixture reveals $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{eq}} = 1.20 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$.
Construct an ICE table, calculate equilibrium concentrations of unreacted $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{SCN^-}$, and determine $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

Model Solution:

1. Construct the ICE Table ($x = 1.20 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$):

ICE Row $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}_{(aq)}}$ $\mathrm{SCN^-_{(aq)}}$ $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}_{(aq)}}$
Initial ($\text{M}$) $1.00 \times 10^{-3}$ $6.00 \times 10^{-4}$ $0.00$
Change ($\text{M}$) $-1.20 \times 10^{-4}$ $-1.20 \times 10^{-4}$ $+1.20 \times 10^{-4}$
Equilibrium ($\text{M}$) $8.80 \times 10^{-4}$ $4.80 \times 10^{-4}$ $1.20 \times 10^{-4}$

2. Substitute into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression: $$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]}{[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}][\mathrm{SCN^-}]} = \frac{1.20 \times 10^{-4}}{(8.80 \times 10^{-4})(4.80 \times 10^{-4})} = \frac{1.20 \times 10^{-4}}{4.224 \times 10^{-7}} = \mathbf{284} \quad (3\text{ sig. figs.})$$

Worked Example 4 (Extension): Evaluating Systematic Error in Le Chatelier Standard Assumption

5 Marks

In preparing calibration standards, students assume $100\%$ conversion of $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_0$ into $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$ due to the $1000$-fold excess of $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ ($0.200\text{ M}$).
Using $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 280$, calculate the TRUE equilibrium $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$ in a standard prepared with $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_0 = 0.200\text{ M}$ and $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_0 = 1.00 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$, and determine the percentage error in the $100\%$ conversion assumption.

Model Solution:

1. Set up equilibrium expression with $x = [\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{eq}}$: $$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{x}{(0.200 - x)(1.00 \times 10^{-4} - x)} = 280$$

2. Because $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_0 = 0.200\text{ M} \gg x$, approximate $(0.200 - x) \approx 0.200$: $$\frac{x}{0.200(1.00 \times 10^{-4} - x)} = 280 \implies \frac{x}{1.00 \times 10^{-4} - x} = 280 \times 0.200 = 56$$ $$x = 56(1.00 \times 10^{-4} - x) = 5.60 \times 10^{-3} - 56x \implies 57x = 5.60 \times 10^{-3}$$ $$x = \frac{5.60 \times 10^{-3}}{57} = 9.825 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$

3. Calculate percentage conversion and assumption error:
• True conversion = $\frac{9.825 \times 10^{-5}}{1.00 \times 10^{-4}} \times 100\% = 98.25\%$
• Percentage error = $\frac{|1.00 \times 10^{-4} - 9.825 \times 10^{-5}|}{9.825 \times 10^{-5}} \times 100\% = \mathbf{1.78\%}$

This confirms that the Le Chatelier assumption introduces an extremely small systematic error ($< 2\%$), making it highly valid for school laboratory determination!

• Using $447\text{ nm}$ maximises sensitivity (molar absorptivity $\varepsilon$) for $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ while ensuring pale yellow $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ ions do not interfere with absorbance readings.

Question 2 (Medium): Practical Role of Nitric Acid Solvent

3 Marks

In this practical investigation, all stock solutions are prepared in $0.5\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ rather than distilled water.
Explain the chemical necessity of using nitric acid as the solvent.

Hint: Consider what happens when $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ reacts with water ($\mathrm{OH^-}$) to form insoluble precipitates.

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
• In neutral aqueous solution, $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ undergoes hydrolysis to form insoluble iron(III) hydroxide precipitate: $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}_{(aq)} + 3H_2O_{(l)} \rightleftharpoons Fe(OH)_{3(s)} + 3H^+_{(aq)}}$.
• Precipitates cause light scattering in cuvettes, invalidating Beer-Lambert absorbance readings.
• High $\mathrm{H^+}$ concentration from $0.5\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{HNO_3}$ shifts hydrolysis equilibrium left by Le Chatelier's Principle, keeping $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ fully dissolved and preventing precipitate formation.

NESA Marking Schema:
  • 1 Mark: Identifies potential formation of $\mathrm{Fe(OH)_3}$ precipitate in neutral water.
  • 1 Mark: Explains that high $[\mathrm{H^+}]$ shifts hydrolysis equilibrium left to suppress precipitation.
  • 1 Mark: Connects precipitate prevention to maintaining solution clarity for valid colourimetry.

Question 3 (Hard): Practical Calculation from Experimental Absorbance

4 Marks

A test solution is prepared by mixing $4.00\text{ mL}$ of $0.00200\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{Fe(NO_3)_3}$ and $4.00\text{ mL}$ of $0.00200\text{ M}$ $\mathrm{KSCN}$ with $2.00\text{ mL}$ of dilute acid ($V_{\text{total}} = 10.00\text{ mL}$).
Colorimetric measurement at $447\text{ nm}$ yields $A = 0.300$. The calibration curve slope is $k = 2500\text{ L mol}^{-1}$.
Calculate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ for this system.

Hint: Initial $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_0 = \frac{4 \times 0.002}{10} = 8.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_0 = 8.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$. $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{eq}} = \frac{0.300}{2500} = 1.20 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$.

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
1. Initial Concentrations:
• $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_0 = \frac{0.00200 \times 4.00}{10.00} = 8.00 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$
• $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_0 = \frac{0.00200 \times 4.00}{10.00} = 8.00 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$
2. Equilibrium $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$:
• $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{eq}} = \frac{A}{k} = \frac{0.300}{2500} = 1.20 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$
3. Unreacted Species at Equilibrium:
• $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_{\text{eq}} = 8.00 \times 10^{-4} - 1.20 \times 10^{-4} = 6.80 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$
• $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_{\text{eq}} = 8.00 \times 10^{-4} - 1.20 \times 10^{-4} = 6.80 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$
4. Calculate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$: $$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{1.20 \times 10^{-4}}{(6.80 \times 10^{-4})(6.80 \times 10^{-4})} = \frac{1.20 \times 10^{-4}}{4.624 \times 10^{-7}} = \mathbf{260}$$

  • 1 Mark: Calculates $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{eq}} = 1.20 \times 10^{-4}\text{ M}$ from Beer-Lambert equation.

Question 4 (Extension): Evaluating Validity & Reliability in Colorimetric Determination

5 Marks

Evaluate the validity and reliability of using a school colorimeter to determine $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ for the $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ equilibrium system, suggesting two specific experimental improvements.

Hint: Address validity (does it measure what it claims?), reliability (consistency of trials), and temperature/cuvette controls.

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
• Validity Evaluation: HIGH VALIDITY. The method directly measures equilibrium complex concentration using a non-destructive optical technique governed by the Beer-Lambert Law. Using excess $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ for calibration standards effectively forces complete reaction by Le Chatelier's Principle.
• Reliability Evaluation: MODERATE RELIABILITY. Reliability depends on repeatable absorbance readings across multiple trials. Temperature fluctuations during measurements cause shifts in $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, reducing reliability.
• Improvement 1 (Thermostatic Control): Place cuvettes in a constant-temperature water bath ($25^\circ\text{C}$) prior to reading to eliminate thermal $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ drift.
• Improvement 2 (Cuvette Standardisation & Matched Optics): Use matched quartz cuvettes and ensure optical faces are consistently wiped with lint-free wipes to eliminate light scattering from fingerprints.

1. Dissociation Equilibria Fundamentals & Solvent Water Omission

Foundational Principles

In previous lessons, we evaluated homogeneous gaseous reactions and aqueous complex ion syntheses using the equilibrium law. We now extend these exact same thermodynamic principles to aqueous dissociation systems.

In aqueous chemistry, dissociation describes the process in which a dissolved substance separates or reacts with water to produce mobile hydrated ions. Dissociation processes fall into two fundamental chemical pathways:

1. Physical Ionic Dissolution

Solid crystalline ionic lattices break apart into mobile hydrated cations and anions when dissolved in water: $$\mathrm{NaCl(s) \xrightarrow{H_2O(l)} Na^+(aq) + Cl^-(aq)}$$ For highly soluble ionic compounds, this process proceeds to completion. For sparingly soluble salts (such as $\mathrm{AgCl(s)}$), a heterogeneous equilibrium is established between the solid crystal lattice and dissolved ions, governed by the solubility product constant ($K_{\mathrm{sp}}$) covered in Phase D.

2. Covalent Ionisation / Proton Transfer

Neutral molecular compounds react chemically with water molecules via proton transfer to generate ions: $$\mathrm{HA(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + A^-(aq)}$$ When a weak acid ($\mathrm{HA}$) or weak base ($\mathrm{B}$) dissolves in water, only a small fraction of the neutral molecules react to form ions. This dynamic chemical equilibrium is quantified using the equilibrium law.

The Solvent Water Omission Rule

In dilute aqueous solutions ($< 1.0\text{ mol L}^{-1}$), liquid water ($\mathrm{H_2O(l)}$) acts as the solvent. Its molar concentration is extraordinarily large and virtually constant: $$[\mathrm{H_2O}] = \frac{1000\text{ g L}^{-1}}{18.016\text{ g mol}^{-1}} \approx 55.5\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$ Because the concentration of liquid solvent water remains essentially unchanged during dilute dissociation reactions, $[\mathrm{H_2O}]$ is incorporated directly into the numerical value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

Pure liquid solvent water ($\mathrm{H_2O(l)}$) is ALWAYS omitted from the denominator of aqueous mass action expressions!

2. The Acid Dissociation Constant ($K_a$)

Quantitative Acid Equilibrium

When a monoprotic weak acid ($\mathrm{HA}$) dissolves in water, it donates a proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) to a water molecule to establish a dynamic equilibrium involving the hydronium ion ($\mathrm{H_3O^+}$) and the conjugate base anion ($\mathrm{A^-}$):

$$\mathrm{HA(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + A^-(aq)}$$

Applying the equilibrium law and omitting the solvent water concentration yields the standard mass action expression for the acid dissociation constant ($K_a$):

The Acid Dissociation Constant Expression ($K_a$)

$$K_a = \frac{[\mathrm{H_3O^+}][\mathrm{A^-}]}{[\mathrm{HA}]}$$

Where $[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]$, $[\mathrm{A^-}]$, and $[\mathrm{HA}]$ represent the molar concentrations ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$) of the respective species measured strictly at dynamic equilibrium.

Electrolyte Strength vs Solution Concentration: The Core Distinction

A vital concept in Module 5 is distinguishing between acid strength (degree of ionisation) and solution concentration (stoichiometric solute amount):

Property Acid Strength (Thermodynamic Feature) Solution Concentration (Stoichiometric Feature)
Definition Refers to the intrinsic tendency of acid molecules to donate protons to water ($100\%$ complete ionisation vs partial equilibrium). Refers to the total moles of acid dissolved per litre of solution ($c = \frac{n}{V}$).
Quantitative Metric Reflected by the numerical magnitude of $K_a$ (strong acids have $K_a \gg 1$; weak acids have $K_a \ll 1$). Measured in $\text{mol L}^{-1}$ (e.g. $10.0\text{ M}$ concentrated vs $0.001\text{ M}$ dilute).
Strong Acid Behaviour $\mathrm{HCl}$, $\mathrm{HNO_3}$, $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ ($100\%$ ionised, single arrow $\rightarrow$, no unreacted molecules). $10.0\text{ M} \mathrm{HCl}$ is concentrated strong; $0.001\text{ M} \mathrm{HCl}$ is dilute strong.
Weak Acid Behaviour $\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$, $\mathrm{HF}$, $\mathrm{HNO_2}$ (partial equilibrium, reversible arrow $\rightleftharpoons$, predominantly unreacted). $10.0\text{ M} \mathrm{CH_3COOH}$ is concentrated weak; $0.001\text{ M} \mathrm{CH_3COOH}$ is dilute weak.

3. Weak Base Dissociation & Polyprotic Step-wise Extent Trends

Conceptual Dynamics

1. Weak Base Dissociation Systems

When a weak base ($\mathrm{B}$) dissolves in water, it accepts a proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) from a solvent water molecule to produce the conjugate acid ($\mathrm{BH^+}$) and hydroxide ions ($\mathrm{OH^-}$):

$$\mathrm{B(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons BH^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)}$$

Applying the equilibrium law and omitting pure solvent liquid water yields the mass action expression:

$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{BH^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}]}{[\mathrm{B}]}$$

For example, the weak base ammonia ($\mathrm{NH_3}$) establishes the following aqueous equilibrium:

$$\mathrm{NH_3(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons NH_4^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)} \quad K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{NH_4^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}]}{[\mathrm{NH_3}]}$$

2. Polyprotic Step-wise Ionisation Trends ($K_{a1} \gg K_{a2} \gg K_{a3}$)

Polyprotic acids possess two or more ionisable hydrogen atoms per molecule. Rather than releasing all protons simultaneously, they ionise in successive step-wise equilibria:

Step-wise Ionisation of Phosphoric Acid ($\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$)

• Step 1: $$\mathrm{H_3PO_4(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + H_2PO_4^-(aq)} \quad K_{a1} = \frac{[\mathrm{H_3O^+}][\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-}]}{[\mathrm{H_3PO_4}]} = 7.1 \times 10^{-3}$$
• Step 2: $$\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + HPO_4^{2-}(aq)} \quad K_{a2} = \frac{[\mathrm{H_3O^+}][\mathrm{HPO_4^{2-}}]}{[\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-}]} = 6.3 \times 10^{-8}$$
• Step 3: $$\mathrm{HPO_4^{2-}(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + PO_4^{3-}(aq)} \quad K_{a3} = \frac{[\mathrm{H_3O^+}][\mathrm{PO_4^{3-}}]}{[\mathrm{HPO_4^{2-}}]} = 4.5 \times 10^{-13}$$

Sub-microscopic Electrostatic Justification for $K_{a1} \gg K_{a2} \gg K_{a3}$

Why does the numerical value of $K_a$ drop so dramatically across successive ionisation steps?

  1. Step 1 ($K_{a1}$): A positively charged proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) is being removed from a neutral molecule ($\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$). The electrostatic attraction between $\mathrm{H^+}$ and the neutral species is relatively small.
  2. Step 2 ($K_{a2}$): A positively charged proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) is being removed from a singly negatively charged anion ($\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-}$). Strong electrostatic attraction opposes the separation of positive charge from a negative ion.
  3. Step 3 ($K_{a3}$): A positively charged proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) is being removed from a doubly negatively charged anion ($\mathrm{HPO_4^{2-}}$). The electrostatic attraction is immense, making proton loss extremely unfavorable.

4. Module 5 Quantitative ICE Calculations (Direct Arithmetic)

Syllabus-Aligned Quantitative Method

In Module 5, quantitative questions test your ability to construct an ICE (Initial, Change, Equilibrium) table and apply basic subtraction and multiplication arithmetic to calculate raw $K_a$ values or equilibrium species concentrations.

The Direct Module 5 ICE Procedure

When given the initial acid concentration $[\mathrm{HA}]_0$ and the measured unreacted acid concentration $[\mathrm{HA}]_{\text{eq}}$ at equilibrium:

1. Calculate the change in concentration $x$: $$x = [\mathrm{HA}]_0 - [\mathrm{HA}]_{\text{eq}}$$ 2. Use 1:1 stoichiometry to deduce equilibrium ion concentrations: $$[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]_{\text{eq}} = x \quad \text{and} \quad [\mathrm{A^-}]_{\text{eq}} = x$$ 3. Substitute these values directly into the mass action expression: $$K_a = \frac{x^2}{[\mathrm{HA}]_{\text{eq}}}$$

This direct arithmetic method requires no quadratic formulas or weak-acid approximation shortcuts, keeping calculations perfectly within Module 5 scope while thoroughly testing mass action principles.

6. Scaffolded Worked Examples

HSC Step-by-Step Solutions

Worked Example 1 (Easy): Writing Mass Action Expressions with Solvent Omission

2 Marks

Write the balanced chemical equation and mass action equilibrium expression ($K_a$) for the aqueous dissociation of nitrous acid ($\mathrm{HNO_2(aq)}$), explicitly justifying the treatment of liquid water.

Model Solution:

1. Balanced Equation: $$\mathrm{HNO_2(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + NO_2^-(aq)}$$

2. Mass Action Expression ($K_a$): $$K_a = \frac{[\mathrm{H_3O^+}][\mathrm{NO_2^-}]}{[\mathrm{HNO_2}]}$$

3. Justification of Water Omission:
Liquid water ($\mathrm{H_2O(l)}$) is the solvent in dilute aqueous solution. Its concentration is extremely large ($\approx 55.5\text{ mol L}^{-1}$) and remains essentially constant during reaction. Therefore, $[\mathrm{H_2O}]$ is incorporated directly into the value of $K_a$ and omitted from the denominator.

Worked Example 2 (Medium): Calculating Raw $K_a$ from Known Equilibrium Concentrations

3 Marks

A sample of hypochlorous acid ($\mathrm{HClO}$) is allowed to reach dynamic equilibrium at $25^\circ\text{C}$. Analysis reveals the equilibrium concentrations are $[\mathrm{HClO}] = 0.040\text{ mol L}^{-1}$, $[\mathrm{H_3O^+}] = 3.5 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol L}^{-1}$, and $[\mathrm{ClO^-}] = 3.5 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol L}^{-1}$.
Calculate the value of the acid dissociation constant ($K_a$) for hypochlorous acid.

Model Solution:

1. Write mass action expression: $$K_a = \frac{[\mathrm{H_3O^+}][\mathrm{ClO^-}]}{[\mathrm{HClO}]}$$

2. Substitute equilibrium values: $$K_a = \frac{(3.5 \times 10^{-5}) \times (3.5 \times 10^{-5})}{0.040} = \frac{1.225 \times 10^{-9}}{0.040} = \mathbf{3.1 \times 10^{-8}} \quad (2\text{ sig. figs.})$$

Worked Example 3 (Hard): Molar ICE Table Calculation for Weak Monoprotic Acid

4 Marks

A $0.100\text{ M}$ solution of a generic weak monoprotic acid $\mathrm{HA}$ is prepared and allowed to reach dynamic equilibrium. The equilibrium concentration of the unreacted acid $[\mathrm{HA}]$ is found to be $0.095\text{ M}$.
Construct an ICE table and compute the raw value of $K_a$.

Model Solution:

1. Determine extent of ionisation ($x$): $$x = [\mathrm{HA}]_0 - [\mathrm{HA}]_{\text{eq}} = 0.100\text{ M} - 0.095\text{ M} = 0.005\text{ M}$$

2. Construct the ICE Table:

ICE Row $\mathrm{HA(aq)}$ $\mathrm{H_3O^+(aq)}$ $\mathrm{A^-(aq)}$
Initial ($\text{M}$) $0.100$ $0.000$ $0.000$
Change ($\text{M}$) $-0.005$ $+0.005$ $+0.005$
Equilibrium ($\text{M}$) $0.095$ $0.005$ $0.005$

3. Substitute into $K_a$ expression: $$K_a = \frac{[\mathrm{H_3O^+}][\mathrm{A^-}]}{[\mathrm{HA}]} = \frac{(0.005) \times (0.005)}{0.095} = \frac{0.000025}{0.095} = \mathbf{2.6 \times 10^{-4}} \quad (2\text{ sig. figs.})$$

Worked Example 4 (Extension): Polyprotic Step-wise Extent Short Answer

5 Marks

Phosphoric acid ($\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$) undergoes step-wise ionisation where $K_{a1} \gg K_{a2} \gg K_{a3}$ ($7.1 \times 10^{-3} \gg 6.3 \times 10^{-8} \gg 4.5 \times 10^{-13}$).
Explain this trend using sub-microscopic electrostatic behaviours.

Model Solution:

1. Write the step-wise ionisation equations:
• Step 1: $$\mathrm{H_3PO_4(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + H_2PO_4^-(aq)} \quad (K_{a1} = 7.1 \times 10^{-3})$$
• Step 2: $$\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + HPO_4^{2-}(aq)} \quad (K_{a2} = 6.3 \times 10^{-8})$$
• Step 3: $$\mathrm{HPO_4^{2-}(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + PO_4^{3-}(aq)} \quad (K_{a3} = 4.5 \times 10^{-13})$$

2. Electrostatic Sub-microscopic Explanation:
• In Step 1 ($K_{a1}$): A positively charged proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) is lost from a neutral $\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$ molecule. Electrostatic attractive forces opposing proton removal are minimal, so this step proceeds to the greatest relative extent.
• In Step 2 ($K_{a2}$): A positively charged proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) must be separated from a singly negatively charged anion ($\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-}$). Strong electrostatic attraction between $\mathrm{H^+}$ and $\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-}$ opposes dissociation, dramatically reducing $K_{a2}$.
• In Step 3 ($K_{a3}$): A positively charged proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) must be separated from a doubly negatively charged anion ($\mathrm{HPO_4^{2-}}$). Intense electrostatic attraction strongly resists charge separation, making $K_{a3}$ exceptionally small.

Question 3 (Hard): Molar ICE Table Calculation

4 Marks

A $0.100\text{ M}$ solution of a generic weak monoprotic acid $\mathrm{HA}$ is allowed to reach equilibrium. The equilibrium concentration of the unreacted acid $[\mathrm{HA}]$ is found to be $0.095\text{ M}$.
Write the mass action expression and compute the raw value of $K_a$.

Hint: Change in concentration $x = 0.100 - 0.095 = 0.005\text{ M}$. Thus $[\mathrm{H_3O^+}] = [\mathrm{A^-}] = 0.005\text{ M}$.

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
1. Write mass action expression: $$K_a = \frac{[\mathrm{H_3O^+}][\mathrm{A^-}]}{[\mathrm{HA}]}$$
2. Calculate extent of ionisation ($x$): $$x = [\mathrm{HA}]_0 - [\mathrm{HA}]_{\text{eq}} = 0.100\text{ M} - 0.095\text{ M} = 0.005\text{ M}$$
3. Determine equilibrium species concentrations: $$[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]_{\text{eq}} = 0.005\text{ M}, \quad [\mathrm{A^-}]_{\text{eq}} = 0.005\text{ M}, \quad [\mathrm{HA}]_{\text{eq}} = 0.095\text{ M}$$
4. Compute $K_a$: $$K_a = \frac{(0.005) \times (0.005)}{0.095} = \frac{0.000025}{0.095} = \mathbf{2.6 \times 10^{-4}} \quad (2\text{ sig. figs.})$$

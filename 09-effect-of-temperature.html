1. Thermodynamic Foundations & Conceptualising "Heat" in Equilibria

Thermodynamic Mechanics

In previous lessons, we examined how concentration, volume, and pressure disturb dynamic equilibrium by altering particle spacing or overall molarities. Temperature perturbations, however, act through a fundamentally different physical mechanism: modifying the average kinetic energy ($\bar{E}_k$) of all particles in the system.

Every chemical reaction involves an enthalpy change ($\Delta H$), defined as the difference between the enthalpy of products and the enthalpy of reactants:

$$\Delta H = H_{\text{products}} - H_{\text{reactants}}$$

Endothermic vs Exothermic Classifications

🔥

1. Exothermic Forward Reaction ($\Delta H < 0$)

• Energy Transfer: Thermal energy is released to the surroundings as chemical bonds are formed.
• Enthalpy Profile: Products possess lower potential energy than reactants ($H_{\text{products}} < H_{\text{reactants}}$).
• Conceptual Model: Treat "Heat" as a PRODUCT on the right-hand side: $$\mathrm{\text{Reactants} \rightleftharpoons \text{Products} + \text{Heat}} \quad (\Delta H < 0)$$

❄️

2. Endothermic Forward Reaction ($\Delta H > 0$)

• Energy Transfer: Thermal energy is absorbed from the surroundings to break chemical bonds.
• Enthalpy Profile: Products possess higher potential energy than reactants ($H_{\text{products}} > H_{\text{reactants}}$).
• Conceptual Model: Treat "Heat" as a REACTANT on the left-hand side: $$\mathrm{\text{Reactants} + \text{Heat} \rightleftharpoons \text{Products}} \quad (\Delta H > 0)$$

NESA Enthalpy Profile: Asymmetry between Forward ($E_{a,\text{f}}$) and Reverse ($E_{a,\text{r}}$) Activation Energies

The Key Thermodynamic Rule for Activation Energies

Notice the mathematical relationship between activation energy barriers and reaction enthalpy: $$\Delta H = E_{a,\text{f}} - E_{a,\text{r}}$$
• For Endothermic Reactions ($\Delta H > 0$): The forward activation energy is strictly greater than the reverse activation energy ($E_{a,\text{f}} > E_{a,\text{r}}$).
• For Exothermic Reactions ($\Delta H < 0$): The reverse activation energy is strictly greater than the forward activation energy ($E_{a,\text{r}} > E_{a,\text{f}}$).
• This barrier height asymmetry is the fundamental physical reason why temperature changes alter forward and reverse reaction rates unequally!

2. Qualitative Shift Rules Under Le Chatelier's Principle

System Adjustments

By applying Le Chatelier's Principle, we treat thermal energy ("heat") as a chemical entity in the reaction equation to determine shift directions.

Thermochemical Type Temperature Disturbance System Objective (LCP) Equilibrium Shift Direction Impact on Product Yield
Exothermic Forward ($\Delta H < 0$) Heating ($T \uparrow$) Absorb excess added thermal energy Shifts REVERSE ($\leftarrow$) in endothermic direction Product yield DECREASES
Exothermic Forward ($\Delta H < 0$) Cooling ($T \downarrow$) Release additional thermal energy to replace loss Shifts FORWARD ($\to$) in exothermic direction Product yield INCREASES
Endothermic Forward ($\Delta H > 0$) Heating ($T \uparrow$) Absorb excess added thermal energy Shifts FORWARD ($\to$) in endothermic direction Product yield INCREASES
Endothermic Forward ($\Delta H > 0$) Cooling ($T \downarrow$) Release additional thermal energy to replace loss Shifts REVERSE ($\leftarrow$) in exothermic direction Product yield DECREASES

Summary Rule for HSC Memory

• Heating ($T \uparrow$): ALWAYS favours the ENDOTHERMIC direction ($\Delta H > 0$) to absorb thermal energy.
• Cooling ($T \downarrow$): ALWAYS favours the EXOTHERMIC direction ($\Delta H < 0$) to release thermal energy.

3. Sub-Microscopic Mechanism: Collision Theory & Maxwell-Boltzmann Distributions

Microscopic Kinetics

Why does heating an endothermic reaction cause a net forward shift at the molecular level? To answer this with full NESA precision, we must combine Maxwell-Boltzmann kinetic energy distribution curves with activation energy barriers ($E_a$).

Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution Shifts Upon Heating

Temperature is a direct measure of the average kinetic energy of gas or solution particles ($\bar{E}_k = \frac{3}{2} k_B T$). At any given temperature, particles possess a distribution of kinetic energies ranging from near zero to extremely high values.

NESA Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution: Effect of Temperature Increase ($T_1 \to T_2$) on Kinetic Energy Tail

Sub-Microscopic Kinetic Justification (4-Step NESA Structure)

When temperature is increased from $T_1$ to $T_2$:

  1. Kinetic Energy Shift: The average kinetic energy of all particles increases. The Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution flattens and shifts to the right, expanding the high-energy tail of molecules with $E \ge E_a$.
  2. Overall Collision Frequency Increase: Particles move faster, so the overall collision rate between all reacting species increases. Therefore, BOTH forward ($r_{\mathrm{f}}$) and reverse ($r_{\mathrm{r}}$) reaction rates increase!
  3. Differential Percentage Increase:
    • For an endothermic forward reaction, $E_{a,\text{f}} > E_{a,\text{r}}$.
    • Raising temperature increases the fraction of collisions exceeding the higher activation energy barrier ($E_{a,\text{f}}$) by a GREATER PROPORTIONAL PERCENTAGE FACTOR than those exceeding the lower barrier ($E_{a,\text{r}}$).
  4. Rate Imbalance & Net Shift: Because $r_{\mathrm{f}}$ increases by a larger factor than $r_{\mathrm{r}}$, we get $r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$. The forward reaction proceeds faster than the reverse reaction, causing a net forward shift ($\to$) until product accumulation accelerates $r_{\mathrm{r}}$ to equal $r_{\mathrm{f}}'$ at a new, higher equal rate!

Critical Exam Distinction: Heating Increases BOTH Rates!

A very common student misconception is writing that "heating slows down the reverse reaction" when an endothermic shift occurs.

• Fact: Heating increases the kinetic energy of ALL particles, so BOTH $r_{\mathrm{f}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{r}}$ ALWAYS INCREASE upon heating!
• Why a Shift Occurs: A shift occurs because the reaction with the higher activation energy increases by a larger magnitude than the other, creating a temporary rate imbalance ($r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$ or $r_{\mathrm{r}} > r_{\mathrm{f}}$)!

4. Mandatory NESA Benchmark Practical Systems

Syllabus Mandated Practical Studies

The NESA Stage 6 Chemistry syllabus explicitly highlights two primary reversible chemical systems for investigating thermal disturbances in the laboratory.

1. System A: Cobalt(II) Complex Equilibrium ($\mathrm{[Co(H_2O)_6]^{2+} / [CoCl_4]^{2-}}$)

When cobalt(II) chloride is dissolved in a mixture of water and concentrated hydrochloric acid, an equilibrium is established between the pink hexaaquacobalt(II) ion and the deep blue tetrachlorocobaltate(II) ion:

$$\mathrm{\underset{\text{Pink}}{[Co(H_2O)_6]^{2+}(aq)} + 4Cl^-(aq) \rightleftharpoons \underset{\text{Deep Blue}}{[CoCl_4]^{2-}(aq)} + 6H_2O(l)} \quad \Delta H > 0 \text{ (Endothermic)}$$

Hot Water Bath Test ($T \uparrow$)

• Disturbance: Temperature is increased ($T \uparrow$).
• LCP Application: System shifts in the endothermic direction ($\Delta H > 0$) to absorb added heat.
• Shift Direction: Net Forward Shift ($\to$).
• Observable Change: The pink solution turns deep violet-blue as $[\mathrm{[CoCl_4]^{2-}}]$ increases.

Ice Water Bath Test ($T \downarrow$)

• Disturbance: Temperature is decreased ($T \downarrow$).
• LCP Application: System shifts in the exothermic direction ($\Delta H < 0$) to release thermal energy.
• Shift Direction: Net Reverse Shift ($\leftarrow$).
• Observable Change: The blue solution turns pale pink as $[\mathrm{[Co(H_2O)_6]^{2+}}]$ increases.

2. System B: Nitrogen Dioxide / Dinitrogen Tetroxide Gas System ($\mathrm{2NO_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons N_2O_{4(g)}}$)

The reversible gas equilibrium between dark brown $\mathrm{NO_2}$ and colourless $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$ is exothermic in the forward direction:

$$\mathrm{\underset{\text{Dark Brown Gas}}{2NO_{2(g)}} \rightleftharpoons \underset{\text{Colourless Gas}}{N_2O_{4(g)}}} \quad \Delta H = -57.2\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} \text{ (Exothermic)}$$
  • Heating in Hot Water Bath ($T \uparrow$): System shifts in the endothermic reverse direction ($\leftarrow$) to absorb heat. Complex converts colourless $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$ back into brown $\mathrm{NO_2}$. Observation: Gas inside ampoule turns INTENSE DARK BROWN.
  • Cooling in Ice Water Bath ($T \downarrow$): System shifts in the exothermic forward direction ($\to$) to release heat. Brown $\mathrm{NO_2}$ combines into colourless $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$. Observation: Gas inside ampoule FADES TO VERY PALE YELLOW / COLOURLESS.

5. Fundamental Uniqueness: Temperature as the SOLE Modifier of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$

Core Thermodynamic Pillar

One of the most important concepts in Module 5 Chemistry is distinguishing how temperature perturbations differ mathematically from concentration, volume, or pressure perturbations.

The Golden Rule of Equilibrium Constants

"The numerical value of the Equilibrium Constant ($K_{\mathrm{eq}}$) for a given reaction is DEPENDENT ONLY ON TEMPERATURE."

• Concentration Changes: $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ remains strictly constant.
• Pressure / Volume Changes: $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ remains strictly constant.
• Adding a Catalyst: $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ remains strictly constant.
• TEMPERATURE CHANGES: $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ NUMERICALLY CHANGES!

Mathematical Relationship Between Temperature and $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$

The equilibrium constant expression represents the ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations at equilibrium:

$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\text{Products}]_{\text{eq}}^p}{[\text{Reactants}]_{\text{eq}}^r}$$
Reaction Enthalpy ($\Delta H$) Temperature Change Net Shift Direction Numerical Effect on $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Value
Endothermic ($\Delta H > 0$) Heating ($T \uparrow$) Forward ($\to$) $\implies$ Products rise, Reactants fall $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ INCREASES ($\uparrow$)
Endothermic ($\Delta H > 0$) Cooling ($T \downarrow$) Reverse ($\leftarrow$) $\implies$ Products fall, Reactants rise $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ DECREASES ($\downarrow$)
Exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$) Heating ($T \uparrow$) Reverse ($\leftarrow$) $\implies$ Products fall, Reactants rise $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ DECREASES ($\downarrow$)
Exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$) Cooling ($T \downarrow$) Forward ($\to$) $\implies$ Products rise, Reactants fall $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ INCREASES ($\uparrow$)

6. Graphical Signatures of Temperature Disturbances ($c\text{ vs }t$ and $r\text{ vs }t$)

Graph Interpretation

NESA examination questions frequently assess whether students can distinguish temperature disturbances from concentration or volume disturbances on concentration-time ($c\text{ vs }t$) and rate-time ($r\text{ vs }t$) graphs.

Visual Signature Rules for Temperature Disturbances

  1. Concentration-Time ($c\text{ vs }t$) Graph:
    • NO INSTANTANEOUS VERTICAL STEP-CHANGES OR SPIKES AT $t_1$!
    • Unlike adding a reagent (single spike) or compressing volume (simultaneous spikes across all gases), a temperature change alters kinetic rates smoothly. All concentration lines remain perfectly continuous at $t_1$, transitioning into smooth exponential curves starting directly from $t_1$.
  2. Rate-Time ($r\text{ vs }t$) Graph:
    • At $t_1$, BOTH forward ($r_{\mathrm{f}}$) and reverse ($r_{\mathrm{r}}$) rates instantly jump UP upon heating (or drop DOWN upon cooling).
    • However, they jump to different heights due to $E_{a,\text{f}} \neq E_{a,\text{r}}$, creating an instantaneous rate gap ($r_{\mathrm{f}} \neq r_{\mathrm{r}}$) that gradually closes as the shift completes.

8. Scaffolded Worked Examples

HSC Step-by-Step Solutions

Worked Example 1 (Easy): Qualitative Shift Prediction for Heating

2 Marks

Predict the direction of the equilibrium shift when an equilibrium mixture of $\mathrm{2SO_{2(g)} + O_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2SO_{3(g)}} \quad (\Delta H = -197\text{ kJ mol}^{-1})$ is heated at constant volume.

Model Solution:

1. Identify Enthalpy Type: The forward reaction is exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$), meaning heat is released in the forward direction.

2. Apply Le Chatelier's Principle: Heating adds thermal energy to the system. To counteract this addition of heat, the system shifts in the endothermic direction ($\Delta H > 0$) to absorb energy.

3. Conclusion: The system undergoes a net reverse shift ($\leftarrow$), increasing the concentrations of reactants $\mathrm{SO_2}$ and $\mathrm{O_2}$ while decreasing product $\mathrm{SO_3}$.

Worked Example 2 (Medium): Experimental Observation Analysis

3 Marks

A test tube containing an aqueous mixture of cobalt(II) ions ($\mathrm{[Co(H_2O)_6]^{2+}}$, pink) and chloride complex ($\mathrm{[CoCl_4]^{2-}}$, blue) is placed into a hot water bath ($80^\circ\text{C}$). The solution turns from purple-pink to deep blue.
Deduce whether the forward complexation reaction is endothermic or exothermic, justifying your reasoning using Le Chatelier's Principle.

Model Solution:

1. Analyse Observation: Heating causes the solution to turn deep blue, indicating an increase in the concentration of blue $\mathrm{[CoCl_4]^{2-}}$ product (a net forward shift, $\to$).

2. Apply LCP Logic: According to Le Chatelier's Principle, heating a system causes it to shift in the endothermic direction to absorb excess thermal energy.

3. Deduce Enthalpy ($\Delta H$): Because heating drove a net forward shift, the forward reaction MUST be ENDOTHERMIC ($\Delta H > 0$).

Worked Example 3 (Hard): Collision Theory Justification for Temperature Shifts

4 Marks

Explain, using Collision Theory at the sub-microscopic level and Maxwell-Boltzmann kinetic energy distributions, why raising the temperature of an endothermic equilibrium system causes a net forward shift.

Model Solution:

1. Maxwell-Boltzmann Shift: Heating increases the average kinetic energy of all particles, shifting the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution curve to the right and expanding the high-energy tail.

2. Activation Energy Asymmetry: For an endothermic reaction ($\Delta H > 0$), the forward activation energy is greater than the reverse activation energy ($E_{a,\text{f}} > E_{a,\text{r}}$).

3. Differential Rate Increase: Because $E_{a,\text{f}}$ is higher, raising the temperature increases the fraction of collisions with $E \ge E_{a,\text{f}}$ by a larger proportional percentage factor than those with $E \ge E_{a,\text{r}}$.

4. Net Shift & Re-equalisation: Consequently, the forward reaction rate accelerates more than the reverse rate ($r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$), producing a net forward shift ($\to$) until product accumulation increases $r_{\mathrm{r}}$ to equal $r_{\mathrm{f}}'$ at a new, higher rate.

Worked Example 4 (Extension): Synthesis of Temperature, $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ & Rate Law Kinetics

5 Marks

An equilibrium reaction $\mathrm{A_{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2B_{(g)}}$ has $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 4.0\text{ M}$ at $298\text{ K}$. When heated to $400\text{ K}$, the new equilibrium constant becomes $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 25.0\text{ M}$.
a) Determine whether the forward reaction is endothermic or exothermic.
b) Explain the effect of this temperature increase on the numerical ratio of product to reactant concentrations at equilibrium.

Model Solution:

a) Determine Enthalpy Classification:
• As temperature increased from $298\text{ K} \to 400\text{ K}$, $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ increased from $4.0 \to 25.0$.
• An increase in $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{B}]_{\text{eq}}^2}{[\mathrm{A}]_{\text{eq}}}$ indicates an increase in product concentration relative to reactant concentration (a net forward shift).
• According to Le Chatelier's Principle, heating favours the endothermic direction. Because heating caused a net forward shift, the forward reaction is ENDOTHERMIC ($\Delta H > 0$).

b) Concentration Ratio Effect:
• $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{B}]_{\text{eq}}^2}{[\mathrm{A}]_{\text{eq}}}$.
• Increasing $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ from $4.0$ to $25.0$ means the equilibrium position lies significantly further to the right at $400\text{ K}$.
• At the higher temperature, the equilibrium mixture contains a substantially higher ratio of $[\mathrm{B}]$ to $[\mathrm{A}]$, representing a higher equilibrium conversion yield.

Question 3 (Hard): Collision Theory Analysis of Cooling Exothermic Reactions

4 Marks

Use Collision Theory and Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions to explain why cooling an exothermic reaction ($\Delta H < 0$) causes a net forward shift.

Hint: For an exothermic reaction, which activation energy barrier is higher ($E_{a,\text{f}}$ or $E_{a,\text{r}}$)? How does cooling affect the higher $E_a$ barrier?

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
• Maxwell-Boltzmann Shift: Cooling reduces the average kinetic energy of all particles, shifting the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution curve left and contracting the high-energy tail ($E \ge E_a$).
• Overall Collision Rate: BOTH forward ($r_{\mathrm{f}}$) and reverse ($r_{\mathrm{r}}$) rates decrease upon cooling.
• Activation Energy Asymmetry: For an exothermic reaction ($\Delta H < 0$), the reverse activation energy is higher than the forward activation energy ($E_{a,\text{r}} > E_{a,\text{f}}$).
• Differential Decrease: Cooling reduces the fraction of collisions exceeding the higher barrier ($E_{a,\text{r}}$) by a larger proportional percentage factor than those exceeding $E_{a,\text{f}}$. Thus, $r_{\mathrm{r}}$ drops more severely than $r_{\mathrm{f}}$, making $r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$ and causing a net forward shift ($\to$).

NESA Marking Schema:
  • 1 Mark: Identifies leftward shift in Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution and states BOTH rates decrease.
  • 1 Mark: Correctly states $E_{a,\text{r}} > E_{a,\text{f}}$ for exothermic reactions.
  • 1 Mark: Explains cooling reduces the higher barrier ($E_{a,\text{r}}$) fraction by a larger proportional percentage.
  • 1 Mark: Concludes $r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$, causing a net forward shift ($\to$).

Question 4 (Extension): Industrial Temperature Strategy Synthesis

5 Marks

In the Haber process ($\mathrm{N_{2(g)} + 3H_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2NH_{3(g)}}, \, \Delta H = -92\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$), a chemical engineer must choose an operating temperature.
Analyse the conflict between equilibrium yield (thermodynamics) and reaction rate (kinetics) regarding temperature selection, and explain how industrial plants resolve this conflict.

Hint: Low temperature gives high yield (LCP) but slow rate. High temperature gives fast rate but low yield. What is the compromise solution?

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
1. Thermodynamic Yield Conflict: Because the forward reaction is exothermic ($\Delta H = -92\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$), Le Chatelier's Principle predicts that low temperature favours a net forward shift, maximising $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ and $\mathrm{NH_3}$ yield.
2. Kinetic Rate Conflict: However, at low temperatures, average kinetic energy is low, and very few collisions exceed $E_a$. The reaction rate is impractically slow, taking months to reach equilibrium.
3. Industrial Resolution (Compromise Conditions): Industrial processing plants use a moderate compromise temperature ($\sim 400\text{--}450^\circ\text{C}$) paired with an iron-based catalyst. The moderate temperature ensures an economically viable reaction rate while maintaining an acceptable yield, and the catalyst accelerates both rates equally without shifting the equilibrium position.

  • 1 Mark: Explains low temperature favours forward shift ($\Delta H < 0$) to maximize yield via LCP.
  • 1 Mark: Explains low temperature results in impractically slow collision rates.
  • 1 Mark: Explains high temperature increases collision rate but severely lowers $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ and yield.
  • 2 Marks: Synthesises the solution: moderate compromise temperature ($400\text{--}450^\circ\text{C}$) paired with a catalyst to balance rate and yield.

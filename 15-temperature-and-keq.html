1. The Unique Status of Temperature in Equilibrium Thermodynamics

Core Foundational Rule

In previous lessons, we examined how altering species concentrations, total system pressure, or vessel volume perturbs a system at equilibrium. According to Le Chatelier's Principle, the system undergoes a directional shift ($Q \ne K_{\mathrm{eq}}$) to partially counteract the disturbance. Once dynamic equilibrium is re-established, the ratio of species concentrations returns to the EXACT same numerical value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

The Golden Axiom of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Stability

Concentration changes, volume/pressure fluctuations, and the addition of catalysts do NOT alter the numerical value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

Temperature is the ONE AND ONLY variable that changes the numerical magnitude of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ for a given chemical system!

When temperature changes, the system does not merely shift back to satisfy an unchanged $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$. Instead, the value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ itself changes to a brand new number. The reaction then shifts spontaneously until its new concentration quotient ($Q$) matches this newly established $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ benchmark.

Disturbance Applied Instantaneous Effect on $Q$ Net Directional Shift Final Numerical Value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$
Add Reactant / Remove Product $Q$ decreases ($Q < K_{\mathrm{eq}}$) Forward ($\to$) UNCHANGED
Decrease Volume / Increase Pressure $Q \ne K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ (if $\Delta n_{\text{gas}} \ne 0$) Shifts to fewer gas moles UNCHANGED
Add Catalyst $Q = K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ ($r_{\text{fwd}}$ & $r_{\text{rev}}$ increased equally) No Shift UNCHANGED
Change Temperature ($T$) $Q$ momentarily constant; $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ changes Shifts to match new $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ MODIFIED TO NEW VALUE ($K_{\mathrm{eq}}'$)

2. Qualitative Analysis via Le Chatelier's Principle

Qualitative Framework

To predict how $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ responds to temperature changes, we treat enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) as a chemical reactant or product within the balanced equation:

  • Exothermic Reactions ($\Delta H < 0$): Release thermal energy. Heat is treated as a product on the right-hand side. $$\mathrm{\text{Reactants} \rightleftharpoons \text{Products} + \text{Heat} \quad (\Delta H < 0)}$$
  • Endothermic Reactions ($\Delta H > 0$): Absorb thermal energy. Heat is treated as a reactant on the left-hand side. $$\mathrm{\text{Reactants} + \text{Heat} \rightleftharpoons \text{Products} \quad (\Delta H > 0)}$$

1. Exothermic Reaction Systems ($\Delta H < 0$)

Consider the reversible dimerisation of nitrogen dioxide: $$\mathrm{2NO_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons N_2O_{4(g)} + \text{Heat}} \quad (\Delta H = -57.2\text{ kJ mol}^{-1})$$

Temperature Increase ($\uparrow T$)

• System Disturbance: Addition of thermal energy (excess product heat).
• LCP Response: System shifts to absorb heat, favoring the endothermic REVERSE reaction ($\leftarrow$).
• Species Effect: $[\mathrm{NO_2}] \uparrow$ (denominator increases), $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}] \downarrow$ (numerator decreases).
• $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Response: $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ DECREASES.

Temperature Decrease ($\downarrow T$)

• System Disturbance: Removal of thermal energy (heat deficit).
• LCP Response: System shifts to release heat, favoring the exothermic FORWARD reaction ($\to$).
• Species Effect: $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}] \uparrow$ (numerator increases), $[\mathrm{NO_2}] \downarrow$ (denominator decreases).
• $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Response: $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ INCREASES.

2. Endothermic Reaction Systems ($\Delta H > 0$)

Consider the decomposition of dinitrogen tetroxide: $$\mathrm{\text{Heat} + N_2O_{4(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2NO_{2(g)}} \quad (\Delta H = +57.2\text{ kJ mol}^{-1})$$

Temperature Increase ($\uparrow T$)

• System Disturbance: Addition of thermal energy.
• LCP Response: System shifts to absorb heat, favoring the endothermic FORWARD reaction ($\to$).
• Species Effect: $[\mathrm{NO_2}] \uparrow$ (numerator increases), $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}] \downarrow$ (denominator decreases).
• $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Response: $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ INCREASES.

Temperature Decrease ($\downarrow T$)

• System Disturbance: Removal of thermal energy.
• LCP Response: System shifts to release heat, favoring the exothermic REVERSE reaction ($\leftarrow$).
• Species Effect: $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}] \uparrow$ (denominator increases), $[\mathrm{NO_2}] \downarrow$ (numerator decreases).
• $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Response: $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ DECREASES.

Summary Rule for $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Temperature Dependence

  • Exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$): Temperature and $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ move in OPPOSITE directions ($\uparrow T \implies \downarrow K_{\mathrm{eq}}$).
  • Endothermic ($\Delta H > 0$): Temperature and $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ move in the SAME direction ($\uparrow T \implies \uparrow K_{\mathrm{eq}}$).

3. Molecular Collision Theory Rationale ($E_{a,\text{fwd}}$ vs $E_{a,\text{rev}}$)

Kinetic Derivation

While Le Chatelier's Principle predicts which direction a system shifts, it does not explain why this occurs at a molecular level. NESA high-mark questions require a complete explanation using Collision Theory, Maxwell-Boltzmann kinetic energy distributions, and relative activation energy barriers ($E_a$).

Activation Energy Asymmetry in Reversible Reactions

The enthalpy change of a reaction is defined as the difference between forward and reverse activation energies:

$$\Delta H = E_{a,\text{fwd}} - E_{a,\text{rev}}$$

Exothermic Systems ($\Delta H < 0$)

The potential energy of products is lower than reactants.
• Forward Activation Energy ($E_{a,\text{fwd}}$) is SMALLER.
• Reverse Activation Energy ($E_{a,\text{rev}}$) is LARGER ($E_{a,\text{rev}} = E_{a,\text{fwd}} + \vert{}\Delta H\vert{}$).

Endothermic Systems ($\Delta H > 0$)

The potential energy of products is higher than reactants.
• Forward Activation Energy ($E_{a,\text{fwd}}$) is LARGER ($E_{a,\text{fwd}} = E_{a,\text{rev}} + \Delta H$).
• Reverse Activation Energy ($E_{a,\text{rev}}$) is SMALLER.

Figure 15.1: Activation Energy Threshold Asymmetry in Exothermic vs Endothermic Systems

The Kinetic Sensitivity Principle

According to the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution, an increase in temperature shifts the kinetic energy distribution of molecules to higher average energies and flattens the curve.

Band 6 Kinetic Rule: High $E_a$ Is More Temperature Sensitive!

The reaction direction with the LARGER activation energy ($E_a$) is proportionally MORE sensitive to temperature changes!

• When Temperature Increases ($\uparrow T$): The rate of BOTH forward and reverse reactions increases. However, the reaction with the larger $E_a$ (the endothermic direction) experiences a larger fractional increase in molecules exceeding its energy barrier. Therefore, $r_{\text{endo}}$ increases MORE than $r_{\text{exo}}$, resulting in a net shift towards the endothermic side!

• When Temperature Decreases ($\downarrow T$): The rate of BOTH directions decreases. However, the reaction with the larger $E_a$ (endothermic direction) suffers a greater fractional reduction in molecules exceeding its barrier. Therefore, $r_{\text{endo}}$ drops MORE severely than $r_{\text{exo}}$, resulting in a net shift towards the exothermic side!

4. Graphical Analysis of Thermal Disturbances ($c$ vs $t$ and $r$ vs $t$)

Graph Interpretation

NESA HSC examinations test your ability to distinguish temperature disturbances from concentration or pressure disturbances on Concentration vs Time ($c$ vs $t$) and Rate vs Time ($r$ vs $t$) graphs.

1. Concentration vs Time ($c$ vs $t$) Graph Characteristics

Concentration Discontinuities (Spikes)

• Adding/Removing Chemical Species: Causes a sharp vertical spike in that specific species line at time $t_{\text{disturb}}$.
• Changing Volume/Pressure: Causes simultaneous vertical spikes in ALL gas species lines.

Temperature Disturbance Signature

• NO Vertical Spikes! Temperature changes do not instantaneously add or remove matter.
• All concentration curves remain perfectly continuous at $t_{\text{disturb}}$, changing slope smoothly as the system re-equilibrates to its new $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ value!

7. Scaffolded Worked Examples

HSC Step-by-Step Solutions

Worked Example 1 (Easy): Deduce Shift and $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Change from Experimental Data

2 Marks

The reaction $\mathrm{2SO_{2(g)} + O_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2SO_{3(g)}}$ has $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 4.0 \times 10^2$ at $500\text{ K}$ and $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 1.2 \times 10^{-1}$ at $1000\text{ K}$.
Deduce whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic, giving a clear reason.

Model Solution:

1. Analyse the temperature vs $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ trend:
• Temperature increased from $500\text{ K}$ to $1000\text{ K}$ ($\uparrow T$).
• $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ decreased from $4.0 \times 10^2$ to $1.2 \times 10^{-1}$ ($\downarrow K_{\mathrm{eq}}$).

2. Apply Le Chatelier's Principle:
An increase in temperature causes the system to shift in the endothermic direction to absorb added thermal energy. Since a lower $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ indicates a shift towards reactants (left $\leftarrow$), the reverse reaction is endothermic.
Therefore, the forward reaction must be EXOTHERMIC ($\Delta H < 0$).

Worked Example 2 (Medium): Kinetic Explanation using Activation Energies

3 Marks

The thermal decomposition of dinitrogen tetroxide $\mathrm{N_2O_{4(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2NO_{2(g)}}$ is endothermic ($\Delta H = +57.2\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$).
Explain using collision theory and activation energy why $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ increases when temperature is increased.

Model Solution:

1. Identify relative activation energies:
Because the reaction is endothermic ($\Delta H > 0$), the forward activation energy is larger than the reverse activation energy ($E_{a,\text{fwd}} > E_{a,\text{rev}}$).

2. Explain temperature increase kinetics:
Increasing temperature increases the average kinetic energy of all gas molecules, increasing the collision frequency and the fraction of collisions with $E \ge E_a$.

3. Relate differential rate increase to $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$:
Because $E_{a,\text{fwd}}$ is larger, a temperature increase produces a greater proportional increase in the fraction of molecules exceeding $E_{a,\text{fwd}}$ compared to $E_{a,\text{rev}}$. Consequently, $r_{\text{fwd}}$ increases more than $r_{\text{rev}}$, causing a net forward shift.
This increases $[\mathrm{NO_2}]$ and decreases $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]$, increasing the value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{NO_2}]^2}{[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]}$.

Worked Example 3 (Hard): Graph Disturbance Identification

4 Marks

A mixture of $\mathrm{CO_{(g)}}$, $\mathrm{Cl_{2(g)}}$, and $\mathrm{COCl_{2(g)}}$ is at equilibrium according to: $$\mathrm{CO_{(g)} + Cl_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons COCl_{2(g)}} \quad (\Delta H = -108\text{ kJ mol}^{-1})$$ At time $t_1$, a disturbance is introduced. Concentrations change smoothly without vertical spikes: $[\mathrm{COCl_2}]$ decreases while $[\mathrm{CO}]$ and $[\mathrm{Cl_2}]$ increase.
Identify the disturbance at $t_1$, justify your answer using Le Chatelier's Principle, and state the effect on $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

Model Solution:

1. Identify disturbance type from graph features:
The absence of vertical spikes indicates that species were not added/removed and volume was not changed. The disturbance must be a Temperature Change.

2. Determine direction of temperature change using LCP:
The system shifted left ($\leftarrow$) to produce more reactants ($\mathrm{CO}$ and $\mathrm{Cl_2}$) and consume product ($\mathrm{COCl_2}$).
For an exothermic reaction ($\mathrm{\text{Reactants} \rightleftharpoons \text{Products} + \text{Heat}}$), a shift to the left absorbs heat. By LCP, a shift to absorb heat is driven by a TEMPERATURE INCREASE ($\uparrow T$).

3. State effect on $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$:
Because the system shifted left, $[\text{Products}]$ decreased and $[\text{Reactants}]$ increased. The numerical value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ DECREASED.

Worked Example 4 (Extension): Industrial Optimisation & Multi-Factor Evaluation

5 Marks

Consider the Contact Process step: $\mathrm{2SO_{2(g)} + O_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2SO_{3(g)}} \quad (\Delta H = -197\text{ kJ mol}^{-1})$.
Evaluate the thermodynamic and kinetic conditions required to maximise the rate and yield of $\mathrm{SO_{3(g)}}$, and justify the industrial operating conditions ($400 - 450^\circ\text{C}$, $1-2\text{ atm}$, $\mathrm{V_2O_5}$ catalyst).

Model Solution:

1. Thermodynamic Analysis ($K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Yield):
• The reaction is exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$). By LCP, lower temperatures favor the forward reaction, increasing $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ and equilibrium yield of $\mathrm{SO_3}$.
• The forward reaction reduces gas moles ($3\text{ mol} \to 2\text{ mol}$). High pressure favors the forward yield.

2. Kinetic Analysis (Reaction Rate):
• Higher temperatures increase molecular kinetic energy and collision frequency, increasing reaction rate.
• Low temperatures make the reaction impractically slow despite high $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

3. Justification of Industrial Conditions:
• Compromise Temperature ($400 - 450^\circ\text{C}$): Balances an acceptable rate with a high equilibrium conversion ($\approx 98\%$).
• $\mathrm{V_2O_5}$ Catalyst: Lowers $E_a$ for both directions equally, enabling rapid rate at moderate temperatures without reducing $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.
• Moderate Pressure ($1-2\text{ atm}$): High yield is already achieved at $450^\circ\text{C}$, making high-pressure equipment unnecessarily expensive.

Question 1 (Easy): Identifying $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Temperature Trends

2 Marks

For an endothermic reaction ($\Delta H > 0$), state what happens to the numerical value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ as temperature is decreased. Explain your answer briefly using Le Chatelier's Principle.

Hint: Endothermic: $\text{Reactants} + \text{Heat} \rightleftharpoons \text{Products}$. Decreasing $T$ removes heat.

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
As temperature is decreased, the numerical value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ DECREASES.
Reason: For an endothermic reaction ($\text{Reactants} + \text{Heat} \rightleftharpoons \text{Products}$), a temperature drop removes thermal energy. By LCP, the system shifts left ($\leftarrow$) to release heat, increasing reactant concentrations and decreasing product concentrations, thus lowering $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

NESA Marking Schema:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly states that $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ decreases.
  • 1 Mark: Justifies using LCP (shift left to release heat decreases product-to-reactant ratio).

Question 2 (Medium): Distinguishing Temperature from Concentration Disturbances on Graphs

3 Marks

Describe two distinct graphical features on a Concentration vs Time ($c$ vs $t$) graph that allow a student to differentiate a temperature disturbance from a concentration disturbance.

Hint: Think about vertical spikes (discontinuities) vs smooth continuous curves.

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
1. Absence of Vertical Discontinuities (Spikes): A concentration disturbance causes an immediate vertical spike in the curve of the added or removed species at $t_{\text{disturb}}$. In contrast, a temperature disturbance produces no vertical spikes; all concentration curves remain continuous at $t_{\text{disturb}}$.
2. Simultaneous Smooth Shifts in All Species: Following a temperature change, all reactant and product curves begin changing slopes simultaneously and smoothly as the system shifts toward a new $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ value.

NESA Marking Schema:
  • 1 Mark: Explains presence of vertical spike for concentration addition/removal vs absence for temperature.
  • 1 Mark: Describes smooth continuous change in all species slopes for temperature disturbances.
  • 1 Mark: Uses clear precise graphical terminology ($t_{\text{disturb}}$, continuity, slope change).

Question 3 (Hard): Kinetic Explanation of Temperature Decrease in Exothermic Systems

4 Marks

For an exothermic reversible reaction ($\Delta H < 0$), explain using collision theory and activation energy why a decrease in temperature causes $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ to increase.

Hint: Exothermic $\implies E_{a,\text{rev}} > E_{a,\text{fwd}}$. A temp drop reduces the endothermic reverse rate MORE than the forward rate.

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
• For an exothermic reaction ($\Delta H < 0$), the reverse activation energy is larger than the forward activation energy ($E_{a,\text{rev}} > E_{a,\text{fwd}}$).
• Decreasing temperature reduces the average kinetic energy of all molecules, decreasing collision frequency and the fraction of collisions with $E \ge E_a$.
• Because $E_{a,\text{rev}}$ is larger, the temperature drop causes a greater proportional reduction in the fraction of molecules exceeding $E_{a,\text{rev}}$ compared to $E_{a,\text{fwd}}$.
• Consequently, the reverse rate ($r_{\text{rev}}$) drops more severely than the forward rate ($r_{\text{fwd}}$), resulting in a net forward shift ($r_{\text{fwd}} > r_{\text{rev}}$). This increases product concentrations and decreases reactant concentrations, raising $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

NESA Marking Schema:
  • 1 Mark: States that $E_{a,\text{rev}} > E_{a,\text{fwd}}$ for an exothermic reaction.
  • 1 Mark: Explains that temperature drop lowers kinetic energy and collision fraction above $E_a$.
  • 1 Mark: Connects higher $E_{a,\text{rev}}$ to a larger proportional drop in $r_{\text{rev}}$ relative to $r_{\text{fwd}}$.
  • 1 Mark: Concludes that net forward shift increases $[\text{Products}] / [\text{Reactants}]$, increasing $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

Question 4 (Extension): Multi-Factor Rate vs Yield Industrial Analysis

5 Marks

The synthesis of methanol is an exothermic equilibrium: $$\mathrm{CO_{(g)} + 2H_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons CH_3OH_{(g)}} \quad (\Delta H = -91\text{ kJ mol}^{-1})$$ An industrial plant operates at $250^\circ\text{C}$ and $50-100\text{ atm}$ using a $\mathrm{CuO/ZnO/Al_2O_3}$ catalyst.
Evaluate how temperature, pressure, and catalyst selection represent an optimisation of thermodynamic yield ($K_{\mathrm{eq}}$) and kinetic rate.

Hint: Analyse temp (thermo vs kinetics), pressure (yield + collision rate), and catalyst (lowers $E_a$ without changing $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$).

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
1. Temperature Optimisation ($250^\circ\text{C}$):
• Thermodynamics: Since $\Delta H < 0$, low temperatures favor the forward exothermic reaction, maximizing $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ and yield. However, room temperature is too slow. $250^\circ\text{C}$ is a compromise temperature providing high yield without making the reaction impractically slow.
2. Pressure Optimization ($50-100\text{ atm}$):
• Thermodynamics & Kinetics: The forward reaction reduces gas moles ($3\text{ mol} \to 1\text{ mol}$). High pressure shifts equilibrium right (increasing yield) AND increases molecular concentration/collision frequency (increasing rate).
3. Catalyst Selection ($\mathrm{CuO/ZnO/Al_2O_3}$):
• Kinetics: Provides an alternate pathway with lower $E_a$ for both directions, allowing high reaction rates at the lower compromise temperature of $250^\circ\text{C}$ without altering the final $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ value.

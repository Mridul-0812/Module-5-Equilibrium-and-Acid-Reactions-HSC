1. Foundations of Quantitative Equilibrium Calculations

Stoichiometric Logic

In Lesson 12, we established that the equilibrium constant expression reflects the stoichiometric ratio of reactants and products at dynamic equilibrium:

$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]^c [\mathrm{D}]^d}{[\mathrm{A}]^a [\mathrm{B}]^b}$$

In practical laboratory or industrial scenarios, however, we rarely measure all equilibrium concentrations directly. Instead, chemists typically measure the initial amounts of reactants mixed in a vessel of volume $V$, and then measure the equilibrium concentration of ONE single species once the system comes to rest.

To bridge the gap between initial state and final equilibrium state, we must apply the law of conservation of mass via stoichiometric proportions. Every mole of product formed requires the consumption of a mathematically defined stoichiometric number of moles of reactants!

The Molar Concentration Imperative ($c = \frac{n}{V}$)

Square brackets in a $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression represent molar concentrations in $\text{mol L}^{-1}$.

• If a problem provides quantities in moles ($n$), grams ($m$), or partial pressures ($p$), you MUST convert them to concentration in $\text{mol L}^{-1}$ before substituting into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$!

• Always divide the number of moles by the container volume in Litres ($V$): $$c = \frac{n}{V}$$
• Crucial Exception: If container volume $V = 1.0\text{ L}$, then $c = n$. However, if $V = 2.0\text{ L}$ or $V = 500\text{ mL}$, failing to divide by $V$ will lead to incorrect values for non-equal mole systems!

2. The ICE Table Framework (Initial, Change, Equilibrium)

Systematic Tool

The ICE Table (an acronym for Initial, Change, Equilibrium) is the standard tool used in chemistry to organise stoichiometric changes in reversible reactions.

Anatomy of an ICE Table

Consider the general homogeneous gas-phase reaction: $$\mathrm{a A_{(g)} + b B_{(g)} \rightleftharpoons c C_{(g)}}$$

Reaction Row $\mathrm{a A_{(g)}}$ $\mathrm{b B_{(g)}}$ $\mathrm{c C_{(g)}}$
Initial Concentration ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$) $[\mathrm{A}]_0$ $[\mathrm{B}]_0$ $[\mathrm{C}]_0$ (usually $0$)
Change in Concentration ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$) $-a x$ $-b x$ $+c x$
Equilibrium Concentration ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$) $[\mathrm{A}]_{\text{eq}} = [\mathrm{A}]_0 - a x$ $[\mathrm{B}]_{\text{eq}} = [\mathrm{B}]_0 - b x$ $[\mathrm{C}]_{\text{eq}} = [\mathrm{C}]_0 + c x$

Row 1: Initial Concentration ($\text{I}$)

• Represents molarities immediately upon mixing, before any chemical reaction occurs ($t = 0$).
• Usually, product concentrations are zero ($0\text{ M}$) unless products were intentionally added to the starting mixture.
• Must be expressed in $\text{mol L}^{-1}$ ($c = \frac{n}{V}$).

Row 2: Change in Concentration ($\text{C}$)

• Tracks the gain or loss of species as the reaction moves toward equilibrium.
• Reactants are lost (assigned negative sign, $-a x$).
• Products are gained (assigned positive sign, $+c x$).
• Coefficients of $x$ MUST match the mole ratio in the balanced chemical equation!

Row 3: Equilibrium Concentration ($\text{E}$)

The equilibrium row is simply the algebraic sum of the Initial row and Change row: $$\text{Equilibrium} = \text{Initial} + \text{Change}$$ $$\text{e.g., } [\mathrm{A}]_{\text{eq}} = [\mathrm{A}]_0 + (-a x) = [\mathrm{A}]_0 - a x$$
These algebraic expressions in the $\text{E}$-row are then substituted directly into the $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression to solve for $x$ or $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$!

3. Scenario Type A: Calculating $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ from Initial and Equilibrium Data

Calculation Type 1

In Scenario Type A, you are given the initial amounts of starting reactants and the equilibrium amount of at least one species. Your objective is to find $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

Standard 4-Step Solution Algorithm (Type A):

Step 1: Write the balanced chemical equation and deduce the corresponding $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression.

Step 2: Convert all given quantities to molar concentrations ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$) using $c = \frac{n}{V}$. Enter these initial values into Row $\text{I}$ of the ICE table.

Step 3: Determine the value of $x$ (the extent of change) by comparing the initial concentration $[\text{X}]_0$ and equilibrium concentration $[\text{X}]_{\text{eq}}$ of the known species: $$\Delta [\text{X}] = [\text{X}]_{\text{eq}} - [\text{X}]_0$$ Use stoichiometry (the mole ratios in the balanced equation) to complete the rest of Row $\text{C}$ and calculate all remaining values in Row $\text{E}$.

Step 4: Substitute the final Row $\text{E}$ concentrations into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ and compute the numerical answer.

Detailed Walkthrough Demonstration

Suppose $2.00\text{ moles}$ of $\mathrm{SO_{2(g)}}$ and $1.00\text{ mole}$ of $\mathrm{O_{2(g)}}$ are placed in a $2.00\text{ L}$ sealed flask at $1000\text{ K}$. At equilibrium, $0.80\text{ moles}$ of $\mathrm{SO_{3(g)}}$ is present. Calculate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

1. Convert to initial molar concentrations ($c = \frac{n}{V}$):

  • $[\mathrm{SO_2}]_0 = \frac{2.00\text{ mol}}{2.00\text{ L}} = 1.00\text{ M}$
  • $[\mathrm{O_2}]_0 = \frac{1.00\text{ mol}}{2.00\text{ L}} = 0.50\text{ M}$
  • $[\mathrm{SO_3}]_0 = 0.00\text{ M}$
  • $[\mathrm{SO_3}]_{\text{eq}} = \frac{0.80\text{ mol}}{2.00\text{ L}} = 0.40\text{ M}$

2. Construct the ICE Table for $\mathrm{2SO_{2(g)} + O_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2SO_{3(g)}}$:

ICE Component $\mathrm{2SO_{2(g)}}$ $\mathrm{O_{2(g)}}$ $\mathrm{2SO_{3(g)}}$
Initial ($\text{M}$) $1.00$ $0.50$ $0.00$
Change ($\text{M}$) $-2x = -0.40$ $-x = -0.20$ $+2x = +0.40$
Equilibrium ($\text{M}$) $1.00 - 0.40 = \mathbf{0.60}$ $0.50 - 0.20 = \mathbf{0.30}$ $0.00 + 0.40 = \mathbf{0.40}$

3. Substitute Row $\text{E}$ into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression:

$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{SO_3}]^2}{[\mathrm{SO_2}]^2 [\mathrm{O_2}]} = \frac{(0.40)^2}{(0.60)^2 \times (0.30)} = \frac{0.16}{0.36 \times 0.30} = \frac{0.16}{0.108} = 1.4815 \dots \approx 1.48$$

4. Scenario Type B: Calculating Unknown Equilibrium Concentrations from $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$

Calculation Type 2

In Scenario Type B, you are given the value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ alongside initial concentrations, and you must calculate the equilibrium concentration of reactants or products.

Algebraic Solving Strategies (Type B):

1. Single Unknown Direct Substitution: When all equilibrium concentrations except one are known, substitute directly into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ and solve linearly.

2. Perfect Square Simplification: If the reaction stoichiometry yields a perfect square in the $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression (e.g., $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{(2x)^2}{(1.0 - x)^2}$), take the square root of both sides ($\sqrt{\phantom{X}}$) to convert the quadratic equation into a simple linear equation!

3. Quadratic Formula Application: If the expression cannot be simplified as a perfect square, expand into standard quadratic form $a x^2 + b x + c = 0$ and solve using: $$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$ Discard roots that give physically impossible negative equilibrium concentrations ($c < 0$)!

5. High-Frequency HSC Examination Pitfalls

Examiner Insights
Common Student Error Why it Loses Marks in HSC Correct NESA Methodology
Using Mole Quantities Instead of Molarity Placing moles directly into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ gives incorrect numerical values whenever sum of reactant powers $\neq$ sum of product powers. Always divide moles by vessel volume ($c = \frac{n}{V}$) before inserting into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ or ICE table rows!
Confusing Initial Amounts with Equilibrium Amounts Placing initial starting concentrations directly into the $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression instead of constructing an ICE table. Initial concentrations belong in Row $\text{I}$. Only Row $\text{E}$ concentrations can be substituted into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$!
Incorrect Coefficient Multiplication in Change Row Writing $-x$ for a reactant with coefficient $3$ instead of $-3x$. The Change row ($\text{C}$) MUST explicitly multiply $x$ by the stoichiometric coefficient ($e.g., -3x$ for $\mathrm{3H_2}$)!
Selecting the Wrong Quadratic Root Selecting a mathematical root for $x$ that results in a negative equilibrium concentration ($c < 0$). Explicitly state "Reject $x = \dots$ as $[\text{X}]_{\text{eq}}$ cannot be negative" and choose the physically valid root.

HSC Step-by-Step Solutions

Worked Example 1 (Easy): Direct $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Substitution

2 Marks

At equilibrium in a $1.00\text{ L}$ vessel at $448^\circ\text{C}$, a gas mixture contains $0.106\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{H_{2(g)}}$, $0.038\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{I_{2(g)}}$, and $0.420\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{HI_{(g)}}$.
Calculate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ for the reaction: $\mathrm{H_{2(g)} + I_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2HI_{(g)}}$.

Model Solution:

1. Calculate concentrations ($c = \frac{n}{V}$ with $V = 1.00\text{ L}$):
• $[\mathrm{H_2}] = 0.106\text{ M}$
• $[\mathrm{I_2}] = 0.038\text{ M}$
• $[\mathrm{HI}] = 0.420\text{ M}$

2. Substitute directly into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression: $$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{HI}]^2}{[\mathrm{H_2}][\mathrm{I_2}]} = \frac{(0.420)^2}{(0.106) \times (0.038)} = \frac{0.1764}{0.004028} = 43.79 \approx \mathbf{43.8} \quad (3\text{ sig. figs.})$$

Worked Example 2 (Medium): ICE Table with Volume Adjustment

3 Marks

A $5.00\text{ L}$ vessel is initially charged with $3.00\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{NO_{2(g)}}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$. Once equilibrium is established according to $\mathrm{2NO_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons N_2O_{4(g)}}$, $0.400\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{N_2O_{4(g)}}$ is found in the container.
Calculate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ for this reaction.

Model Solution:

1. Convert moles to molarity ($c = \frac{n}{5.00\text{ L}}$):
• $[\mathrm{NO_2}]_0 = \frac{3.00\text{ mol}}{5.00\text{ L}} = 0.600\text{ M}$
• $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_{\text{eq}} = \frac{0.400\text{ mol}}{5.00\text{ L}} = 0.0800\text{ M}$

2. Construct ICE Table:

ICE Component $\mathrm{2NO_{2(g)}}$ $\mathrm{N_2O_{4(g)}}$
Initial ($\text{M}$) $0.600$ $0.000$
Change ($\text{M}$) $-2x = -0.160$ $+x = +0.0800$
Equilibrium ($\text{M}$) $0.600 - 0.160 = \mathbf{0.440}$ $\mathbf{0.0800}$

3. Compute $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$: $$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]}{[\mathrm{NO_2}]^2} = \frac{0.0800}{(0.440)^2} = \frac{0.0800}{0.1936} = \mathbf{0.413} \quad (3\text{ sig. figs.})$$

Worked Example 3 (Hard): Perfect Square Algebraic Solution

4 Marks

At $425^\circ\text{C}$, $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 54.0$ for the reaction: $\mathrm{H_{2(g)} + I_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2HI_{(g)}}$.
If $1.00\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{H_{2(g)}}$ and $1.00\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{I_{2(g)}}$ are sealed in a $1.00\text{ L}$ flask, calculate the equilibrium concentrations of all three gases.

Model Solution:

1. Initial Concentrations: $[\mathrm{H_2}]_0 = 1.00\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{I_2}]_0 = 1.00\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{HI}]_0 = 0\text{ M}$.

2. ICE Table:

ICE Row $\mathrm{H_{2(g)}}$ $\mathrm{I_{2(g)}}$ $\mathrm{2HI_{(g)}}$
Initial ($\text{M}$)1.001.000
Change ($\text{M}$)$-x$$-x$$+2x$
Equilibrium ($\text{M}$)$1.00 - x$$1.00 - x$$2x$

3. Substitute into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression: $$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{HI}]^2}{[\mathrm{H_2}][\mathrm{I_2}]} \implies 54.0 = \frac{(2x)^2}{(1.00 - x)(1.00 - x)} = \frac{(2x)^2}{(1.00 - x)^2}$$

4. Take square root of both sides ($\sqrt{\phantom{X}}$): $$\sqrt{54.0} = \frac{2x}{1.00 - x} \implies 7.3485 = \frac{2x}{1.00 - x}$$ $$7.3485 (1.00 - x) = 2x \implies 7.3485 - 7.3485x = 2x$$ $$9.3485x = 7.3485 \implies x = 0.786\text{ M}$$

5. Final Equilibrium Concentrations:
• $[\mathrm{H_2}]_{\text{eq}} = 1.00 - 0.786 = \mathbf{0.214\text{ M}}$
• $[\mathrm{I_2}]_{\text{eq}} = 1.00 - 0.786 = \mathbf{0.214\text{ M}}$
• $[\mathrm{HI}]_{\text{eq}} = 2(0.786) = \mathbf{1.572\text{ M}} \approx \mathbf{1.57\text{ M}}$

Worked Example 4 (Extension): Quadratic Formula Equilibrium Solution

5 Marks

At $250^\circ\text{C}$, $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 0.0420$ for the decomposition: $\mathrm{PCl_{5(g)} \rightleftharpoons PCl_{3(g)} + Cl_{2(g)}}$.
If $0.500\text{ mol}$ of pure $\mathrm{PCl_{5(g)}}$ is placed in a $2.00\text{ L}$ rigid flask, determine the equilibrium concentration of $\mathrm{Cl_{2(g)}}$.

Model Solution:

1. Initial Concentration: $[\mathrm{PCl_5}]_0 = \frac{0.500\text{ mol}}{2.00\text{ L}} = 0.250\text{ M}$.

2. ICE Table:

ICE Row $\mathrm{PCl_{5(g)}}$ $\mathrm{PCl_{3(g)}}$ $\mathrm{Cl_{2(g)}}$
Initial ($\text{M}$)0.25000
Change ($\text{M}$)$-x$$+x$$+x$
Equilibrium ($\text{M}$)$0.250 - x$$x$$x$

3. Substitute into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression: $$0.0420 = \frac{x \cdot x}{0.250 - x} = \frac{x^2}{0.250 - x}$$ $$0.0420 (0.250 - x) = x^2 \implies 0.0105 - 0.0420x = x^2$$ $$x^2 + 0.0420x - 0.0105 = 0$$

4. Apply Quadratic Formula ($a=1, b=0.0420, c=-0.0105$): $$x = \frac{-0.0420 \pm \sqrt{(0.0420)^2 - 4(1)(-0.0105)}}{2(1)} = \frac{-0.0420 \pm \sqrt{0.001764 + 0.0420}}{2}$$ $$x = \frac{-0.0420 \pm \sqrt{0.043764}}{2} = \frac{-0.0420 \pm 0.2092}{2}$$
• $x_1 = \frac{0.1672}{2} = 0.0836\text{ M}$
• $x_2 = \frac{-0.2512}{2} = -0.1256\text{ M}$ (Reject: concentration cannot be negative!)

5. Conclusion: $$[\mathrm{Cl_2}]_{\text{eq}} = x = \mathbf{0.0836\text{ M}}$$

Question 1 (Easy): Evaluating $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ from Equilibrium Molarities

2 Marks

An equilibrium mixture for the gas reaction $\mathrm{2NO_{(g)} + O_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2NO_{2(g)}}$ at $230^\circ\text{C}$ in a $1.00\text{ L}$ vessel contains $[\mathrm{NO}] = 0.054\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{O_2}] = 0.12\text{ M}$, and $[\mathrm{NO_2}] = 0.38\text{ M}$.
Calculate the value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

Hint: Substitute concentrations directly into $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{NO_2}]^2}{[\mathrm{NO}]^2[\mathrm{O_2}]}$. Don't forget to square $[\mathrm{NO_2}]$ and $[\mathrm{NO}]$!

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer: $$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{NO_2}]^2}{[\mathrm{NO}]^2[\mathrm{O_2}]} = \frac{(0.38)^2}{(0.054)^2 \times (0.12)} = \frac{0.1444}{0.002916 \times 0.12} = \frac{0.1444}{0.00034992} = 412.66 \approx 410 \quad (2\text{ sig. figs.})$$

NESA Marking Schema:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly writes $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression with accurate exponents.
  • 1 Mark: Calculates correct final numerical value ($410$ or $4.1 \times 10^2$).

Question 2 (Medium): ICE Table Calculation with Vessel Scaling

3 Marks

A $2.00\text{ L}$ flask is filled with $1.60\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{COCl_{2(g)}}$ (phosgene). At equilibrium according to $\mathrm{COCl_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons CO_{(g)} + Cl_{2(g)}}$, the flask contains $0.400\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{CO_{(g)}}$.
Calculate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ for this decomposition.

Hint: First divide moles by $2.00\text{ L}$ to get initial $[\mathrm{COCl_2}]_0 = 0.800\text{ M}$ and $[\mathrm{CO}]_{\text{eq}} = 0.200\text{ M}$. Construct the ICE table.

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
1. Initial Molarities: $[\mathrm{COCl_2}]_0 = \frac{1.60\text{ mol}}{2.00\text{ L}} = 0.800\text{ M}$.
2. Equilibrium $[\mathrm{CO}]_{\text{eq}} = \frac{0.400\text{ mol}}{2.00\text{ L}} = 0.200\text{ M}$.
3. ICE Row E: $[\mathrm{COCl_2}]_{\text{eq}} = 0.800 - 0.200 = 0.600\text{ M}$; $[\mathrm{Cl_2}]_{\text{eq}} = 0.200\text{ M}$.
4. Substitution: $$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{CO}][\mathrm{Cl_2}]}{[\mathrm{COCl_2}]} = \frac{(0.200) \times (0.200)}{0.600} = \frac{0.0400}{0.600} = \mathbf{0.0667} \quad (3\text{ sig. figs.})$$

NESA Marking Schema:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly converts initial and equilibrium mole quantities to molar concentrations ($\text{M}$).
  • 1 Mark: Populates ICE table accurately using $1:1:1$ stoichiometry.
  • 1 Mark: Calculates correct final $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ value ($0.0667$ or $6.67 \times 10^{-2}$).

Question 3 (Hard): Solving for Unknown Equilibrium Reactant

4 Marks

The reaction $\mathrm{2A_{(g)} \rightleftharpoons B_{(g)} + C_{(g)}}$ has $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 0.250$ at $300\text{ K}$.
A reaction vessel initially contains $[\mathrm{A}]_0 = 2.00\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{B}]_0 = 0\text{ M}$, and $[\mathrm{C}]_0 = 0\text{ M}$.
Calculate the concentration of reactant $\mathrm{A}$ remaining at equilibrium.

Hint: Let Change for $\mathrm{A}$ be $-2x$. Then $[\mathrm{B}]_{\text{eq}} = x$ and $[\mathrm{C}]_{\text{eq}} = x$. Take the square root of both sides!

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
1. ICE Table: $[\mathrm{A}]_{\text{eq}} = 2.00 - 2x$, $[\mathrm{B}]_{\text{eq}} = x$, $[\mathrm{C}]_{\text{eq}} = x$.
2. Substitute into $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$: $$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{B}][\mathrm{C}]}{[\mathrm{A}]^2} \implies 0.250 = \frac{x^2}{(2.00 - 2x)^2}$$
3. Take square root ($\sqrt{\phantom{X}}$): $$\sqrt{0.250} = \frac{x}{2.00 - 2x} \implies 0.500 = \frac{x}{2.00 - 2x}$$ $$0.500 (2.00 - 2x) = x \implies 1.00 - 1.00x = x \implies 2.00x = 1.00 \implies x = 0.500\text{ M}$$
4. Calculate $[\mathrm{A}]_{\text{eq}}$: $$[\mathrm{A}]_{\text{eq}} = 2.00 - 2(0.500) = 2.00 - 1.00 = \mathbf{1.00\text{ M}}$$

NESA Marking Schema:
  • 1 Mark: Constructs correct ICE table with Change row matching $-2x$, $+x$, $+x$.
  • 1 Mark: Sets up correct algebraic expression $0.250 = \frac{x^2}{(2.00 - 2x)^2}$.
  • 1 Mark: Solves for $x = 0.500\text{ M}$ using square root method.
  • 1 Mark: Correctly calculates final equilibrium concentration $[\mathrm{A}]_{\text{eq}} = 1.00\text{ M}$.

Question 4 (Extension): Quadratic Analysis with Non-Zero Starting Products

5 Marks

At a high temperature, $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 1.00$ for $\mathrm{H_{2(g)} + CO_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons H_2O_{(g)} + CO_{(g)}}$.
A $1.00\text{ L}$ container is charged with $1.00\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{H_2}$, $2.00\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{CO_2}$, and $1.00\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{H_2O}$.
Determine the equilibrium concentration of carbon monoxide gas ($\mathrm{CO_{(g)}}$).

Hint: Initial concentrations: $[\mathrm{H_2}]_0 = 1.00$, $[\mathrm{CO_2}]_0 = 2.00$, $[\mathrm{H_2O}]_0 = 1.00$, $[\mathrm{CO}]_0 = 0$. Expand the expression and solve the quadratic.

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:
1. ICE Table ($V = 1.00\text{ L}$):
• $[\mathrm{H_2}]_{\text{eq}} = 1.00 - x$
• $[\mathrm{CO_2}]_{\text{eq}} = 2.00 - x$
• $[\mathrm{H_2O}]_{\text{eq}} = 1.00 + x$
• $[\mathrm{CO}]_{\text{eq}} = x$
2. Substitute into $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 1.00$: $$1.00 = \frac{(1.00 + x)(x)}{(1.00 - x)(2.00 - x)} = \frac{x + x^2}{2.00 - 3.00x + x^2}$$ $$2.00 - 3.00x + x^2 = x + x^2$$ $$2.00 - 4.00x = 0 \implies 4.00x = 2.00 \implies x = 0.500\text{ M}$$
3. Conclusion: $$[\mathrm{CO}]_{\text{eq}} = x = \mathbf{0.500\text{ M}}$$

NESA Marking Schema:
  • 1 Mark: Identifies non-zero initial concentration for product $[\mathrm{H_2O}]_0 = 1.00\text{ M}$.
  • 1 Mark: Populates ICE table correctly ($+x$ for products, $-x$ for reactants).
  • 1 Mark: Sets up full $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ algebraic equation correctly.
  • 1 Mark: Simplifies algebraic terms accurately (note: quadratic terms $x^2$ cancel out!).
  • 1 Mark: Obtains correct final answer $[\mathrm{CO}]_{\text{eq}} = 0.500\text{ M}$.

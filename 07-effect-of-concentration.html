1. Fundamentals of Concentration Disturbances & Collision Dynamics

Microscopic Kinetics

In Lesson 06, we introduced Le Chatelier's Principle as a qualitative predictive model for dynamic equilibrium systems. In this lesson, we perform a deep kinetic and empirical investigation into concentration changes—one of the most fundamental disturbances tested in Stage 6 Chemistry.

A concentration disturbance occurs whenever the molarity ($C = n/V$) of an individual dissolved aqueous ion or gas particle in a closed system is altered. This can happen via direct addition of a chemical reagent, indirect removal of a species through precipitation or complex formation, or solvent dilution.

Le Chatelier Prediction vs Collision Theory Mechanism

1. Le Chatelier's Qualitative Prediction

• Adding a Species ($+[\text{Species}]$): The system shifts in a direction that consumes the added species to lower its concentration.
• Removing a Species ($-[\text{Species}]$): The system shifts in a direction that produces the removed species to replenish its concentration.

2. Collision Theory Physical Cause

• Increasing $[\text{Reactant}]$ raises the number of reactant particles per unit volume.
• This increases the frequency of collisions between reactant molecules per second ($Z_{\mathrm{f}} \uparrow$), instantly making the forward reaction rate greater than the reverse rate ($r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$).
• Net forward reaction proceeds until product accumulation elevates reverse rate ($r_{\mathrm{r}} \uparrow$) to re-equalise rates ($r_{\mathrm{f}}' = r_{\mathrm{r}}'$).

The Partial Counteraction Rule for Concentration Spikes

When a species concentration is abruptly spiked by adding a reagent, the net shift consumes part of the added species. However, at the new dynamic equilibrium state, the final concentration of the spiked species will remain higher than its pre-spike baseline, but lower than the maximum height of the instantaneous spike!

2. The Benchmark Practical System: Iron(III) Thiocyanate Equilibrium

Syllabus Mandatory Experiment

The NESA Stage 6 Chemistry syllabus specifically mandates the practical investigation of the equilibrium between iron(III) ions ($\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$), thiocyanate ions ($\mathrm{SCN^-}$), and the thiocyanatoiron(III) complex ion ($\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$).

Net Ionic Chemical Equation & Optical Characteristics

When iron(III) nitrate ($\mathrm{Fe(NO_3)_3}$) is mixed with potassium thiocyanate ($\mathrm{KSCN}$) in aqueous solution, an equilibrium is established:

$$\mathrm{\underset{\text{Pale Yellow / Amber}}{Fe^{3+}(aq)} + \underset{\text{Colourless}}{SCN^-(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \underset{\text{Deep Blood-Red}}{FeSCN^{2+}(aq)}} \quad \Delta H < 0$$
Chemical Species System Role Solution Appearance / Spectrophotometric Colour
Iron(III) Ion ($\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$) Aqueous Reactant Cation Pale Yellow / Amber (at high concentrations) or nearly colourless when dilute.
Thiocyanate Ion ($\mathrm{SCN^-}$) Aqueous Reactant Anion Colourless.
Thiocyanatoiron(III) Ion ($\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$) Aqueous Complex Product Deep Blood-Red (absorbs strongly at $\lambda_{\text{max}} \approx 447\text{ nm}$).
Spectator Ions ($\mathrm{K^+}$, $\mathrm{NO_3^-}$) Inert Spectators Colourless; do not participate in collision mechanics.

Visual Indicator for Shift Directions

Because the product complex ion $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ is intensely blood-red while reactants are pale yellow/colourless, the solution's colour intensity acts as a built-in optical indicator:
• Darker Blood-Red Colour: Indicates a net forward shift ($\to$) (product $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ increases).
• Paler / Lighter Red or Yellow Colour: Indicates a net reverse shift ($\leftarrow$) (product $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ decreases).

3. Perturbing the Benchmark System: Direct Ion Additions

Reagent Additions

Let us systematically examine the effects of injecting additional reactant or product ions into the benchmark iron(III) thiocyanate equilibrium mixture.

Case A: Adding Iron(III) Nitrate Solution ($\mathrm{Fe(NO_3)_3} \to +\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$)

  • Disturbance: Concentration of reactant $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]$ is abruptly increased.
  • Le Chatelier Prediction: System shifts to consume added $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$.
  • Shift Direction: Net Forward Shift ($\to$).
  • Observable Change: Solution turns significantly darker blood-red due to the accumulation of $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$.
  • Collision Theory: Higher $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]$ elevates forward collision frequency ($Z_{\mathrm{f}} \uparrow$), making $r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$ until products build up.

Case B: Adding Potassium Thiocyanate Solution ($\mathrm{KSCN} \to +\mathrm{SCN^-}$)

  • Disturbance: Concentration of reactant $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]$ is abruptly increased.
  • Le Chatelier Prediction: System shifts to consume added $\mathrm{SCN^-}$.
  • Shift Direction: Net Forward Shift ($\to$).
  • Observable Change: Solution turns darker blood-red as $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$ rises.
  • Collision Theory: Higher $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]$ increases forward collision frequency ($Z_{\mathrm{f}} \uparrow$), driving $r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$.

Case C: Adding Pure Complex Salt ($\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$)

  • Disturbance: Concentration of product $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$ is increased.
  • Le Chatelier Prediction: System shifts to consume excess product $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$.
  • Shift Direction: Net Reverse Shift ($\leftarrow$).
  • Collision Theory: Higher $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$ raises reverse decomposition collision frequency ($Z_{\mathrm{r}} \uparrow$), driving $r_{\mathrm{r}} > r_{\mathrm{f}}$.

4. Perturbing the Benchmark System: Removing Ions via Precipitation & Complexation

High-Frequency Exam Traps

Examiners frequently test indirect concentration removal pathways where a added chemical reagent reacts with $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ or $\mathrm{SCN^-}$, binding them into insoluble precipitates or highly stable secondary complex ions.

1. Adding Silver Nitrate ($\mathrm{AgNO_3} \to -\mathrm{SCN^-}$)

• Chemical Mechanism: Silver ions ($\mathrm{Ag^+}$) combine with thiocyanate ions ($\mathrm{SCN^-}$) to precipitate solid white silver thiocyanate ($\mathrm{AgSCN_{(s)}}$): $$\mathrm{Ag^+(aq) + SCN^-(aq) \to AgSCN(s)} \quad (\text{White Precipitate})$$ • Disturbance: $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]$ is precipitously lowered (removed from solution).
• LCP Shift: System undergoes a net reverse shift ($\leftarrow$) to replace lost $\mathrm{SCN^-}$.
• Observable Change: The blood-red colour fades to pale yellow, and a white milky suspension forms.

2. Adding Disodium Hydrogen Phosphate ($\mathrm{Na_2HPO_4} \to -\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$)

• Chemical Mechanism: Hydrogen phosphate ions ($\mathrm{HPO_4^{2-}}$) bind iron(III) ions into a stable, colourless phosphatoiron(III) complex ion: $$\mathrm{Fe^{3+}(aq) + HPO_4^{2-}(aq) \rightleftharpoons [Fe(HPO_4)]^+(aq)} \quad (\text{Colourless Complex})$$ • Disturbance: Free $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]$ is removed from the primary equilibrium.
• LCP Shift: System undergoes a net reverse shift ($\leftarrow$) to replace lost $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$.
• Observable Change: The blood-red colour fades completely to colourless.

3. Adding Sodium Fluoride ($\mathrm{NaF} \to -\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$)

• Chemical Mechanism: Fluoride ions ($\mathrm{F^-}$) form an extremely stable, colourless hexafluoroferrate(III) complex ion: $$\mathrm{Fe^{3+}(aq) + 6F^-(aq) \rightleftharpoons [FeF_6]^{3-}(aq)} \quad (\text{Colourless Complex})$$ • Disturbance: Free $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]$ is strongly sequestered.
• LCP Shift: System undergoes a net reverse shift ($\leftarrow$).
• Observable Change: Blood-red solution rapidly decolourises to clear/colourless.

4. Adding Sodium Hydroxide ($\mathrm{NaOH} \to -\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$)

• Chemical Mechanism: Hydroxide ions ($\mathrm{OH^-}$) react with $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ to precipitate iron(III) hydroxide: $$\mathrm{Fe^{3+}(aq) + 3OH^-(aq) \to Fe(OH)_3(s)} \quad (\text{Red-Brown Precipitate})$$ • Disturbance: $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]$ is precipitated out of solution.
• LCP Shift: System undergoes a net reverse shift ($\leftarrow$).
• Observable Change: Blood-red solution fades, and a rusty red-brown solid precipitates.

5. Aqueous Dilution & The Volume-Concentration Paradox

Solution Dynamics

What happens when distilled water is added to the benchmark $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}/SCN^-/FeSCN^{2+}}$ solution?

Mathematical Count of Dissolved Species

$$\mathrm{\underset{1\text{ Aq Mole}}{Fe^{3+}(aq)} + \underset{1\text{ Aq Mole}}{SCN^-(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \underset{1\text{ Aq Mole}}{FeSCN^{2+}(aq)}}$$

• Reactant Side: $1 + 1 = 2\text{ moles of dissolved ions}$.
• Product Side: $1\text{ mole of dissolved complex ion}$.

Application of Le Chatelier's Dilution Principle

Disturbance: Adding water doubles solution volume ($V \uparrow$), instantly halving the concentrations ($C = n/V$) of all dissolved species ($\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}, \mathrm{SCN^-}, \mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$).

LCP Rule: To counteract the overall drop in ion concentration, the system shifts towards the side with the GREATER number of dissolved aqueous particles ($\sum \text{aq moles}$).

Outcome: Shifts in reverse ($\leftarrow$) towards the reactant side ($2\text{ aq moles} > 1\text{ aq mole}$) to produce more total dissolved ions. Blood-red $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ dissociates into $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{SCN^-}$, fading the red intensity beyond the simple optical dilution effect!

6. Mathematical Analysis: Reaction Quotient ($Q_c$) vs Equilibrium Constant ($K_{\mathrm{eq}}$)

Quantitative Proof

While Le Chatelier's Principle provides qualitative predictions, the Reaction Quotient ($Q_c$) provides rigorous mathematical proof of concentration-induced shifts.

For the equilibrium $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}(aq) + SCN^-(aq) \rightleftharpoons FeSCN^{2+}(aq)}$, the equilibrium expression is:

$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{eq}}}{[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_{\text{eq}}[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_{\text{eq}}}$$

When a concentration disturbance is imposed, we substitute the new instantaneous concentrations $[\text{Species}]_i$ into the same expression to calculate the instantaneous Reaction Quotient ($Q_c$):

$$Q_c = \frac{[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_i}{[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_i[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_i}$$
Comparison Condition Mathematical Relationship System Equilibrium Status Net Shift Direction
$Q_c < K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Denominator is too large (too many reactants) or numerator is too small. System is NOT at equilibrium. Shifts FORWARD ($\to$) to form more product.
$Q_c = K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Instantaneous concentrations match equilibrium ratio. System IS at dynamic equilibrium. NO SHIFT ($r_{\mathrm{f}} = r_{\mathrm{r}}$).
$Q_c > K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Numerator is too large (too many products) or denominator is too small. System is NOT at equilibrium. Shifts REVERSE ($\leftarrow$) to reform reactants.

Mathematical Proof for Adding Reactant $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$

If extra $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ is injected into an equilibrium system, $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_i > [\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_{\text{eq}}$.
Because $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_i$ sits in the denominator of $Q_c$, increasing the denominator causes the fraction value to drop:

$$Q_c = \frac{[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_{\text{eq}}}{([\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_{\text{eq}} + \text{spike})[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_{\text{eq}}} < K_{\mathrm{eq}}$$

Since $Q_c < K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, the system mathematically MUST shift forward ($\to$) to convert reactants into products until $Q_c$ rises back up to equal $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$!

9. Scaffolded Worked Examples

HSC Step-by-Step Solutions

Worked Example 1 (Easy): Qualitative Reagent Addition Prediction

2 Marks

A student adds a few drops of concentrated potassium thiocyanate ($\mathrm{KSCN}$) solution to an equilibrium mixture of $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}(aq) + SCN^-(aq) \rightleftharpoons FeSCN^{2+}(aq)}$.
Predict the shift direction and describe the observable colour change.

Model Solution:

1. Identify Disturbance: Adding $\mathrm{KSCN}$ increases the concentration of reactant thiocyanate ions ($[\mathrm{SCN^-}]$).

2. Apply LCP: To counteract the increase in $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]$, the system shifts in the direction that consumes $\mathrm{SCN^-}$.

3. Conclusion: The system undergoes a net forward shift ($\to$). The solution turns a darker blood-red due to the increased concentration of product $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$.

Worked Example 2 (Medium): Explaining Ion Removal via Precipitation

3 Marks

A solution of silver nitrate ($\mathrm{AgNO_3}$) is added to the equilibrium system $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}(aq) + SCN^-(aq) \rightleftharpoons FeSCN^{2+}(aq)}$.
Explain, using net ionic equations and Le Chatelier's Principle, why the solution colour fades and a white precipitate forms.

Model Solution:

1. Net Ionic Precipitation Equation: Silver ions ($\mathrm{Ag^+}$) react with thiocyanate ions ($\mathrm{SCN^-}$) to precipitate insoluble silver thiocyanate: $$\mathrm{Ag^+(aq) + SCN^-(aq) \to AgSCN(s)} \quad (\text{White Solid})$$

2. LCP Explanation: Precipitation removes free reactant ions $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]$ from solution. According to Le Chatelier's Principle, the system shifts in the reverse direction ($\leftarrow$) to replace the lost $\mathrm{SCN^-}$.

3. Observable Change: As $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ decomposes to replace $\mathrm{SCN^-}$, the blood-red colour fades to pale yellow, and a cloudy white precipitate ($\mathrm{AgSCN_{(s)}}$) is observed.

Worked Example 3 (Hard): Collision Theory Mechanism for Concentration Shifts

4 Marks

Explain, using Collision Theory at the sub-microscopic level, why adding $\mathrm{Fe(NO_3)_3}$ to the $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}/SCN^-/FeSCN^{2+}}$ system causes a net forward shift.

Model Solution:

1. Initial State: Prior to addition, the system is at dynamic equilibrium where forward collision frequency equals reverse collision frequency ($Z_{\mathrm{f}} = Z_{\mathrm{r}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{f}} = r_{\mathrm{r}}$).

2. Disturbance Mechanics: Injecting $\mathrm{Fe(NO_3)_3}$ increases the concentration of $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ particles per unit volume. This increases the collision frequency between reactant $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{SCN^-}$ ions per second ($Z_{\mathrm{f}} \uparrow$).

3. Rate Imbalance: Because reactant collision frequency rises while product concentration is initially unchanged, the forward reaction rate instantly exceeds the reverse reaction rate ($r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$).

4. Re-establishing Equilibrium: More $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ complex ions are synthesized per second than decompose. As $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ accumulates, reverse collision frequency increases ($Z_{\mathrm{r}} \uparrow$) until forward and reverse rates re-equalise ($r_{\mathrm{f}}' = r_{\mathrm{r}}'$) at a higher $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]$.

Worked Example 4 (Extension): Quantitative Reaction Quotient ($Q_c$) Proof

5 Marks

An equilibrium solution at $25^\circ\text{C}$ contains $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}] = 0.020\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{SCN^-}] = 0.015\text{ M}$, and $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}] = 0.045\text{ M}$.
a) Calculate the equilibrium constant $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.
b) Solid $\mathrm{Na_2HPO_4}$ is dissolved in the mixture, binding $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ into a complex and instantly reducing free $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]$ to $0.005\text{ M}$. Calculate the instantaneous Reaction Quotient ($Q_c$) and mathematically prove the shift direction.

Model Solution:

a) Calculate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$:

$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]}{[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}][\mathrm{SCN^-}]} = \frac{0.045}{0.020 \times 0.015} = \frac{0.045}{0.00030} = 150\text{ M}^{-1}$$

b) Calculate $Q_c$ and Prove Shift Direction:

Instantaneous concentrations after $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ sequestration: $[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_i = 0.005\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_i = 0.015\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_i = 0.045\text{ M}$.

$$Q_c = \frac{[\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}]_i}{[\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}]_i[\mathrm{SCN^-}]_i} = \frac{0.045}{0.005 \times 0.015} = \frac{0.045}{0.000075} = 600\text{ M}^{-1}$$

Mathematical Comparison: $Q_c (600) > K_{\mathrm{eq}} (150)$.

Because $Q_c > K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, the numerator (product concentration) is too large relative to the denominator. The system must shift in REVERSE ($\leftarrow$) to convert products back into reactants until $Q_c$ decreases back to $150$.

Question 4 (Extension): Synthesis of Dilution & Reaction Quotient

5 Marks

An aqueous equilibrium system $\mathrm{A(aq) + B(aq) \rightleftharpoons C(aq)}$ has $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 100\text{ M}^{-1}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$. At equilibrium, $[\mathrm{A}] = 0.10\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{B}] = 0.10\text{ M}$, and $[\mathrm{C}] = 1.00\text{ M}$.
Distilled water is added to double the total solution volume ($V \times 2$).
a) Calculate instantaneous concentrations immediately after water addition.
b) Calculate $Q_c$ and prove mathematically which direction the system must shift to restore equilibrium.

Hint: Doubling volume halves all concentrations: $[\text{Species}]_i = \frac{1}{2}[\text{Species}]_{\text{eq}}$. Substitute these halved values into $Q_c$.

Model Answer & Rubric

a) Instantaneous Concentrations: $$\text{Doubling volume halves all concentrations: } [\mathrm{A}]_i = 0.050\text{ M}, \, [\mathrm{B}]_i = 0.050\text{ M}, \, [\mathrm{C}]_i = 0.50\text{ M}$$

b) Calculate $Q_c$ and Prove Shift Direction:

$$Q_c = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]_i}{[\mathrm{A}]_i[\mathrm{B}]_i} = \frac{0.50}{0.050 \times 0.050} = \frac{0.50}{0.0025} = 200\text{ M}^{-1}$$

Comparison: $Q_c (200) > K_{\mathrm{eq}} (100)$.

Because $Q_c > K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, the numerator (product concentration) is too high relative to the denominator. The system must shift in REVERSE ($\leftarrow$) to convert products back into reactants until $Q_c$ falls back to $100$. This rigorously proves the LCP dilution rule: shifting towards the side with more aqueous species ($2\text{ aq moles} > 1\text{ aq mole}$)!

  • 2 Marks: Correctly calculates $Q_c = 200\text{ M}^{-1}$.

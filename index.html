Module Overview

Syllabus Scope

Module 5 transitions your study of chemistry from static stoichiometry to dynamic systems in flux. Standard chemical reactions are often modelled as one-way transformations, but physical reality frequently involves reversible processes where reactants and products exist in continuous exchange.

Core Inquiry Questions

  • What happens when chemical reactions do not go to completion?
  • How can dynamic equilibrium be manipulated qualitatively and quantitatively?
  • How does solubility control chemical reactions in aqueous environments?

Mathematical Foundations

You will derive and compute equilibrium constants ($K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ and $K_{\mathrm{sp}}$), evaluate reaction quotients ($Q$), and model concentrations through algebraic ICE tables:

Sample HSC Question: Dynamic Equilibrium Expression

3 Marks

Consider the homogeneous gas-phase synthesis of ammonia: $$\mathrm{N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3(g)} \quad \Delta H = -92.4\text{ kJ/mol}$$ Write the equilibrium constant expression ($K_{\mathrm{eq}}$) for this system and state the impact of adding a solid catalyst on $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

Hint: Remember that $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\text{products}]^p}{[\text{reactants}]^r}$. A catalyst lowers activation energy equally for both forward and reverse pathways.

Model Answer & Rubric

$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{NH_3}]^2}{[\mathrm{N_2}][\mathrm{H_2}]^3}$$

Catalyst Effect: Addition of a catalyst speeds up both forward and reverse reaction rates equally by providing an alternate reaction pathway with lower activation energy ($E_a$). Consequently, a catalyst has no effect on the equilibrium position or the magnitude of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

NESA Marking Schema:
  • 1 Mark: Correct $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression with products over reactants and accurate stoichiometric exponents.
  • 1 Mark: Correctly states that a catalyst does not alter $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.
  • 1 Mark: Explains catalyst effect using activation energy and equal rate increases in both directions.

Question 1

Hornsby Girls 2023 1 Mark

Which of the following may occur in a closed chemical system?

Correct Answer: (B)

A closed thermodynamic system permits the exchange of energy (e.g. heat or work) with its surroundings across its boundary, but prevents any exchange of matter. (An open system exchanges both energy and matter, whereas an isolated system exchanges neither).

Question 2

Hornsby Girls 2023 1 Mark

What are the conditions required for an effective collision between reactants?

Correct Answer: (D)

According to collision theory, for a chemical collision to result in a reaction (a successful/effective collision), the colliding reactant particles must possess kinetic energy equal to or exceeding the activation energy ($E_a$) and collide with appropriate molecular orientation.

Question 3

Abbotsleigh 2025 1 Mark

Which of the following statements is always true of a system at dynamic equilibrium?

Correct Answer: (A)

Dynamic equilibrium is defined as the state in a closed system where the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction ($r_{\text{forward}} = r_{\text{reverse}} > 0$). Consequently, macroscopic properties (like concentrations) remain constant over time, though reactant and product concentrations are rarely equal to each other.

Question 5

Hornsby Girls 2022 1 Mark

Consider the following reversible reaction: $$2\mathrm{NO_{(g)}} + \mathrm{Cl_{2(g)}} \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{NOCl_{(g)}} \quad K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 6.5 \times 10^4 \text{ at } 35^\circ\mathrm{C}$$ Which of the following statements describes the relative concentrations of reactants and products in this system when equilibrium is established in a closed vessel at $35^\circ\mathrm{C}$?

Correct Answer: (B)

The equilibrium constant $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{NOCl}]^2}{[\mathrm{NO}]^2[\mathrm{Cl_2}]} = 6.5 \times 10^4 \gg 1$. A $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ much greater than $1$ signifies that the equilibrium position lies strongly to the right, meaning product concentration significantly exceeds reactant concentration at equilibrium.

Question 6

Hornsby Girls 2022 1 Mark

Bromine and chlorine gases can react to form the compound $\mathrm{BrCl}$. The equation for the reaction is: $$\mathrm{Br_{2(g)}} + \mathrm{Cl_{2(g)}} \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{BrCl_{(g)}} \quad K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 225 \text{ at } 200^\circ\mathrm{C}$$ Given the above information, what is the $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ for the reaction $\mathrm{BrCl_{(g)}} \rightleftharpoons \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Br_{2(g)}} + \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Cl_{2(g)}}$ at $200^\circ\mathrm{C}$?

Correct Answer: (D)

Step-by-step mathematical derivation:

  1. Reversing the equation inverts $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$: $K_{\text{reversed}} = \frac{1}{K_{\mathrm{eq}}} = \frac{1}{225}$.
  2. Halving all stoichiometric coefficients raises $K_{\text{reversed}}$ to the power of $\frac{1}{2}$ (square root):
$$K_{\text{new}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{225}} = \frac{1}{15} \approx 0.0667 \approx 0.067$$

Question 7

Hornsby Girls 2022 1 Mark

A change is made to an equilibrium mixture of $\mathrm{Br_{2(g)}} + \mathrm{Cl_{2(g)}} \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{BrCl_{(g)}}$ ($\Delta H < 0, K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 225$). $\mathrm{Br_2}$ is dark brown, $\mathrm{Cl_2}$ is light green, and $\mathrm{BrCl}$ is colourless. The brown intensity is seen to increase as equilibrium is re-established. The change could have been:

Correct Answer: (C)

An increase in brown colour intensity indicates an increase in $[\mathrm{Br_{2(g)}}]$, meaning the equilibrium shifted to the left. The forward reaction is exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$). By Le Chatelier's Principle, increasing the temperature drives the system in the endothermic (reverse) direction to absorb heat, generating more $\mathrm{Br_{2(g)}}$. (Note: Volume change does not shift equilibrium as moles of gas are equal on both sides: $2 \rightleftharpoons 2$).

Question 8

North Sydney Girls 2022 1 Mark

At elevated temperatures, hydrogen iodide decomposes into hydrogen gas and iodine gas: $2\mathrm{HI_{(g)}} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{H_{2(g)}} + \mathrm{I_{2(g)}}$ ($K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 0.020$ at $500^\circ\mathrm{C}$). In a $1\text{ L}$ vessel, initial $\mathrm{HI} = 2.0\text{ mol}$ with no $\mathrm{H_2}$ or $\mathrm{I_2}$. At time $t$, $\mathrm{HI} = 1.16\text{ mol}$. Which statement describes the system at time $t$?

Correct Answer: (C)

Calculate reaction quotient $Q$ at time $t$:

  • Moles of $\mathrm{HI}$ decomposed $= 2.0 - 1.16 = 0.84\text{ mol}$.
  • Stoichiometric production of $\mathrm{H_2}$ and $\mathrm{I_2} = \frac{0.84}{2} = 0.42\text{ mol}$ each.
  • In $1\text{ L}$: $[\mathrm{HI}] = 1.16\text{ M}, [\mathrm{H_2}] = 0.42\text{ M}, [\mathrm{I_2}] = 0.42\text{ M}$.
$$Q = \frac{[\mathrm{H_2}][\mathrm{I_2}]}{[\mathrm{HI}]^2} = \frac{(0.42)^2}{(1.16)^2} = \frac{0.1764}{1.3456} \approx 0.131$$

Since $Q (0.131) > K_{\mathrm{eq}} (0.020)$, the concentration of products is higher than at equilibrium, so the system favours the reverse reaction to reach equilibrium.

Hydrogen carbonate ions react with water: $\mathrm{HCO_{3(aq)}^-} + \mathrm{H_2O_{(l)}} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{CO_{3(aq)}^{2-}} + \mathrm{H_3O_{(aq)}^+}$ ($\Delta H = +\text{ve}$). Predict the effects of: (1) adding $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$, (2) increasing temperature, (3) adding a catalyst.

  1. Adding $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$ increases product concentration, shifting equilibrium left (reverse favoured).
  2. Endothermic reaction ($\Delta H > 0$): increasing temperature shifts equilibrium right (forward favoured) to absorb heat.
  3. A catalyst increases forward and reverse rates equally; position of equilibrium undergoes no change.

Question 14

Hornsby Girls 2022 1 Mark

An equilibrium is represented by $\mathrm{P_{(aq)}} + \mathrm{Z_{(aq)}} \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{R_{(aq)}} + \mathrm{S_{(aq)}}$ in a $1\text{ L}$ solution. Initial $[\mathrm{Z}] = 0.8\text{ mol L}^{-1}$. What is the new equilibrium $[\mathrm{Z}]$ if $0.4\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{Z}$ is added?

Correct Answer: (B)

Adding $0.4\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{Z}$ instantly spikes $[\mathrm{Z}]$ to $0.8 + 0.4 = 1.2\text{ mol L}^{-1}$. By Le Chatelier's Principle, the equilibrium shifts right to consume some of the added $\mathrm{Z}$, partially counteracting the increase. Hence, the final $[\mathrm{Z}]$ will be reduced below $1.2\text{ M}$, but will remain greater than the original $0.8\text{ M}$.

Question 18

Hornsby Girls 2023 1 Mark

The enthalpy change, $\Delta H$, of a reaction is $-30\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$, and the entropy change, $\Delta S$, is $-90\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$. At what temperature will this reaction change from spontaneous to non-spontaneous?

Correct Answer: (D)

Transition point occurs where $\Delta G = 0$:

$$\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S = 0 \implies T = \frac{\Delta H}{\Delta S}$$

Convert $\Delta H$ to $\text{J mol}^{-1}$: $\Delta H = -30,000\text{ J mol}^{-1}$.

$$T = \frac{-30,000\text{ J mol}^{-1}}{-90\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}} = 333.33\text{ K} \approx 333\text{ K}$$

Question 20

Hornsby Girls 2023 1 Mark

Carbon monoxide reacts with steam: $\mathrm{CO_{(g)}} + \mathrm{H_2O_{(g)}} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{CO_{2(g)}} + \mathrm{H_{2(g)}}$. $1.00\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{CO}$ reacts with $2.00\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{H_2O}$ in a $2.00\text{ L}$ vessel. At equilibrium, $[\mathrm{CO_2}] = 0.250\text{ mol L}^{-1}$. What is $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$?

Correct Answer: (C)

ICE Table calculation in $2.00\text{ L}$:

  • $[\mathrm{CO}]_{\text{initial}} = \frac{1.00}{2.00} = 0.500\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{H_2O}]_{\text{initial}} = \frac{2.00}{2.00} = 1.000\text{ M}$.
  • At equilibrium: $[\mathrm{CO_2}] = 0.250\text{ M} \implies [\mathrm{H_2}] = 0.250\text{ M}$.
  • $[\mathrm{CO}]_{\text{eq}} = 0.500 - 0.250 = 0.250\text{ M}$.
  • $[\mathrm{H_2O}]_{\text{eq}} = 1.000 - 0.250 = 0.750\text{ M}$.
$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{CO_2}][\mathrm{H_2}]}{[\mathrm{CO}][\mathrm{H_2O}]} = \frac{(0.250)(0.250)}{(0.250)(0.750)} = \frac{0.250}{0.750} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.333$$

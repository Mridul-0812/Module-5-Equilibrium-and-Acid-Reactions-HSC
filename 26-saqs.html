Question 1: Indigenous Cycad Detoxification

North Sydney Boys 2024 3 Marks

Cycads were a vital food source for Indigenous Australians. However, untreated cycad seeds contain cycasin, a potent water-soluble toxin. Before cooking, Indigenous peoples processed the seeds by crushing them and placing them in woven bags submerged in running river water (a method known as leaching).

Explain the chemical and equilibrium principles that make leaching in running water an effective method for removing cycasin from cycad seeds.

Hint: Formulate a solubility equilibrium equation for solid cycasin dissolving in water: $\mathrm{Cycasin_{(s)}} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Cycasin_{(aq)}}$. Consider how continuous river flow alters $[\mathrm{Cycasin_{(aq)}}]$ and how Le Chatelier's Principle responds.

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:

Cycasin is a polar, water-soluble toxin that establishes a dynamic solubility equilibrium between its solid form in the cycad seed and its dissolved aqueous state: $$\mathrm{Cycasin_{(s)}} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Cycasin_{(aq)}}$$ Submerging crushed seeds in running water continuously washes away the dissolved $\mathrm{Cycasin_{(aq)}}$, maintaining its aqueous concentration near zero ($[\mathrm{Cycasin_{(aq)}}] \approx 0$).

According to Le Chatelier's Principle, when a disturbance is imposed on a system at equilibrium, the system shifts to counteract the disturbance. The continuous removal of aqueous product drives the equilibrium persistently to the right (forward direction), causing all solid cycasin to dissolve completely out of the seed matrix into the river, rendering the remaining cycad pulp non-toxic.

NESA Marking Schema:
1 Mark: Identifies that cycasin establishes a dissolution equilibrium ($\mathrm{Cycasin_{(s)}} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Cycasin_{(aq)}}$) due to its polar/water-soluble nature.
1 Mark: Explains that running water continuously removes dissolved $\mathrm{Cycasin_{(aq)}}$, maintaining an extremely low product concentration.
1 Mark: Applies Le Chatelier's Principle to explain that the system shifts continuously in the forward direction until solid toxin is completely leached out.

Question 2: Fractional & Selective Precipitation

North Sydney Girls 2022 5 Marks

A solution contains $0.030\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ carbonate ions ($\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}$) and $0.025\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ hydroxide ions ($\mathrm{OH^-}$). A highly concentrated lead(II) nitrate solution ($\mathrm{Pb(NO_3)_2}$) is added dropwise without altering the overall solution volume.

Data: $K_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathrm{PbCO_3}) = 7.4 \times 10^{-14}$; $K_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathrm{Pb(OH)_2}) = 1.2 \times 10^{-15}$.

(a) Calculate the threshold $[\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}]$ required to initiate the precipitation of each compound. (2 Marks)
(b) Identify which anion precipitates first, and calculate the percentage of this anion remaining in solution at the exact point when the second precipitate begins to form. (3 Marks)

Hint: Set $Q = K_{\mathrm{sp}}$ for each salt to find the minimum $[\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}]$ needed for precipitation. Remember that $\mathrm{Pb(OH)_2}$ has a 1:2 stoichiometry ($K_{\mathrm{sp}} = [\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}][\mathrm{OH^-}]^2$)!

Model Answer & Rubric

Part (a) Calculation:

For $\mathrm{PbCO_3}$ precipitation threshold:

$$[\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}]_{\mathrm{PbCO_3}} = \frac{K_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathrm{PbCO_3})}{[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]} = \frac{7.4 \times 10^{-14}}{0.030} = 2.47 \times 10^{-12}\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$

For $\mathrm{Pb(OH)_2}$ precipitation threshold:

$$[\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}]_{\mathrm{Pb(OH)_2}} = \frac{K_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathrm{Pb(OH)_2})}{[\mathrm{OH^-}]^2} = \frac{1.2 \times 10^{-15}}{(0.025)^2} = 1.92 \times 10^{-12}\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$

Part (b) Calculation:

Since $1.92 \times 10^{-12}\text{ M} < 2.47 \times 10^{-12}\text{ M}$, $\mathrm{OH^-}$ precipitates first as $\mathrm{Pb(OH)_2}$.

The second precipitate ($\mathrm{PbCO_3}$) starts forming when $[\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}] = 2.47 \times 10^{-12}\text{ mol L}^{-1}$. At this $[\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}]$, the remaining concentration of $\mathrm{OH^-}$ is: $$[\mathrm{OH^-}]_{\text{remaining}} = \sqrt{\frac{K_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathrm{Pb(OH)_2})}{[\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}]}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.2 \times 10^{-15}}{2.47 \times 10^{-12}}} = \sqrt{4.858 \times 10^{-4}} = 0.02204\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$ $$\text{Percentage remaining} = \frac{0.02204}{0.025} \times 100 = 88.2\%$$ $$\text{Percentage precipitated} = 100\% - 88.2\% = 11.8\%$$

NESA Marking Schema:
1 Mark: Correctly calculates threshold $[\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}] = 2.47 \times 10^{-12}\text{ M}$ for $\mathrm{PbCO_3}$.
1 Mark: Correctly calculates threshold $[\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}] = 1.92 \times 10^{-12}\text{ M}$ for $\mathrm{Pb(OH)_2}$.
1 Mark: Correctly identifies $\mathrm{OH^-}$ as precipitating first due to requiring a lower $[\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}]$.
1 Mark: Calculates remaining $[\mathrm{OH^-}] = 0.0220\text{ M}$ at the onset of $\mathrm{PbCO_3}$ precipitation.
1 Mark: Calculates percentage of $\mathrm{OH^-}$ remaining ($88.2\%$) or precipitated ($11.8\%$) with correct working.

Question 3: Common Ion Effect & Small $x$ Approximation

North Sydney Boys 2024 5 Marks

Barium sulfate ($\mathrm{BaSO_4}$) is ingested as an aqueous suspension in medical X-ray imaging of the gastrointestinal tract. Because free $\mathrm{Ba^{2+}}$ ions are highly toxic, its solubility is suppressed by adding $0.10\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ sodium sulfate ($\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}$) solution.

Data: $K_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathrm{BaSO_4}) = 1.08 \times 10^{-10}$; $M(\mathrm{BaSO_4}) = 233.39\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.

(a) Calculate the solubility of $\mathrm{BaSO_4}$ in $0.10\text{ mol L}^{-1}\ \mathrm{Na_2SO_4}$ in $\text{g / 100 mL}$. (3 Marks)
(b) Explain under what conditions the small $x$ (simplifying) approximation can and cannot be validly applied in solubility calculations. (2 Marks)

Hint: Initial $[\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}] = 0.10\text{ M}$. Write $K_{\mathrm{sp}} = [\mathrm{Ba^{2+}}][\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}] = s(0.10 + s) \approx 0.10s$. Convert molar solubility $s$ to $\text{g L}^{-1}$ then divide by 10 for $\text{g / 100 mL}$!

Model Answer & Rubric

Part (a) Calculation:

$$\mathrm{BaSO_{4(s)}} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Ba_{(aq)}^{2+}} + \mathrm{SO_{4(aq)}^{2-}}$$

Let molar solubility be $s$. $[\mathrm{Ba^{2+}}] = s$, $[\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}] = 0.10 + s \approx 0.10\text{ M}$ (since $K_{\mathrm{sp}} \ll 0.10$).

$$K_{\mathrm{sp}} = s \times 0.10 = 1.08 \times 10^{-10} \implies s = \frac{1.08 \times 10^{-10}}{0.10} = 1.08 \times 10^{-9}\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$ $$\text{Mass solubility in g L}^{-1} = 1.08 \times 10^{-9}\text{ mol L}^{-1} \times 233.39\text{ g mol}^{-1} = 2.52 \times 10^{-7}\text{ g L}^{-1}$$ $$\text{Solubility in g / 100 mL} = \frac{2.52 \times 10^{-7}}{10} = 2.52 \times 10^{-8}\text{ g / 100 mL}$$

Part (b) Explanation:

The small $x$ approximation ($c \pm x \approx c$) is valid when the initial concentration $c$ is vastly greater than $K$ (typically $c/K > 1000$), meaning $x$ constitutes $<5\%$ of $c$.

The approximation cannot be made when $K$ is relatively large, when the initial common ion concentration is extremely small, or when $x$ represents $>5\%$ of the initial value, in which case a full quadratic solution is required.

NESA Marking Schema:
1 Mark: Correctly calculates molar solubility $s = 1.08 \times 10^{-9}\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ using the common ion effect.
1 Mark: Converts molar solubility to mass concentration in $\text{g L}^{-1}$ ($2.52 \times 10^{-7}\text{ g L}^{-1}$).
1 Mark: Correctly converts to $\text{g / 100 mL}$ ($2.52 \times 10^{-8}\text{ g / 100 mL}$).
1 Mark: Explains valid condition: $c/K$ is large ($>1000$) so $x$ is negligible ($<5\%$).
1 Mark: Explains invalid condition: $K$ is large or initial common ion concentration is low/comparable to $x$.

Question 4: Non-Standard ICE Table & Re-equilibration

Oakhill College 2023 6 Marks

Consider the homogeneous gas-phase water-gas shift reaction: $$\mathrm{CO_{(g)}} + \mathrm{H_2O_{(g)}} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{CO_{2(g)}} + \mathrm{H_{2(g)}} \quad K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 10.0 \text{ at } 600^\circ\mathrm{C}$$ Initially, $1.60\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{CO}$, $1.60\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{H_2O}$, $6.00\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{CO_2}$, and $6.00\text{ mol}$ of $\mathrm{H_2}$ are injected into a sealed $2.00\text{ L}$ reaction vessel at $600^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

(a) Calculate the reaction quotient ($Q$) and determine whether the system is initially at equilibrium. (2 Marks)
(b) Calculate the equilibrium concentration of $\mathrm{CO_{2(g)}}$ in $\text{mol L}^{-1}$ once equilibrium is established at $600^\circ\mathrm{C}$. (4 Marks)

Hint: Remember to divide all mole quantities by $2.00\text{ L}$ to obtain initial concentrations in $\text{mol L}^{-1}$! Since $Q > K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, the shift is to the LEFT (reverse). Take the square root of both sides of your $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expression to solve for $x$ quickly.

Model Answer & Rubric

Part (a) Calculation:

Initial concentrations in $2.00\text{ L}$ vessel:

$$[\mathrm{CO}] = \frac{1.60}{2.00} = 0.80\text{ M}, \quad [\mathrm{H_2O}] = 0.80\text{ M}, \quad [\mathrm{CO_2}] = \frac{6.00}{2.00} = 3.00\text{ M}, \quad [\mathrm{H_2}] = 3.00\text{ M}$$ $$Q = \frac{[\mathrm{CO_2}][\mathrm{H_2}]}{[\mathrm{CO}][\mathrm{H_2O}]} = \frac{(3.00)(3.00)}{(0.80)(0.80)} = \frac{9.00}{0.64} = 14.06$$

Since $Q (14.06) > K_{\mathrm{eq}} (10.0)$, the system is not at equilibrium. The system will shift to the left (reverse direction) to consume products and form reactants.

Part (b) Calculation:

ICE Table ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$):

Species $\mathrm{CO}$ $\mathrm{H_2O}$ $\mathrm{CO_2}$ $\mathrm{H_2}$
Initial (M) 0.80 0.80 3.00 3.00
Change (M) $+x$ $+x$ $-x$ $-x$
Equilibrium (M) $0.80 + x$ $0.80 + x$ $3.00 - x$ $3.00 - x$
$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{(3.00 - x)^2}{(0.80 + x)^2} = 10.0$$

Taking square root of both sides:

$$\frac{3.00 - x}{0.80 + x} = \sqrt{10.0} = 3.1623$$ $$3.00 - x = 3.1623(0.80 + x) = 2.5298 + 3.1623x$$ $$0.4702 = 4.1623x \implies x = 0.113\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$ $$[\mathrm{CO_2}]_{\text{eq}} = 3.00 - 0.113 = 2.89\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$
NESA Marking Schema:
1 Mark: Converts initial moles to concentrations ($0.80\text{ M}$ and $3.00\text{ M}$).
1 Mark: Calculates $Q = 14.06$ and correctly concludes $Q > K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ shifts reverse.
1 Mark: Constructs accurate ICE table with change $-x$ for products and $+x$ for reactants.
1 Mark: Correctly applies square root simplification to set up $\frac{3.00 - x}{0.80 + x} = 3.1623$.
1 Mark: Solves algebraically for $x = 0.113\text{ mol L}^{-1}$.
1 Mark: Calculates final equilibrium $[\mathrm{CO_2}] = 2.89\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ with units.

Question 5: Volume Disturbance & Concentration-Time Analysis

James Ruse 2021 6 Marks

An investigation tracked the concentrations of species in the equilibrium system: $$2\mathrm{SO_{3(g)}} \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{SO_{2(g)}} + \mathrm{O_{2(g)}}$$ At time $T_0$, initial equilibrium concentrations were $[\mathrm{SO_3}] = 0.30\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{SO_2}] = 0.20\text{ M}$, and $[\mathrm{O_2}] = 0.40\text{ M}$. At time $T_1$, the volume of the reaction container was decreased by $20\%$ (causing an instantaneous concentration spike of $+25\%$ for all gases). The system re-established equilibrium by time $T_2$, at which point $[\mathrm{O_2}]$ was recorded to be one-sixth higher than its original $T_0$ value.

(a) Calculate the exact equilibrium concentrations of $\mathrm{SO_3}$, $\mathrm{SO_2}$, and $\mathrm{O_2}$ at time $T_2$. (3 Marks)
(b) Explain the direction of shift from $T_1$ to $T_2$ using both Le Chatelier's Principle and Collision Theory. (3 Marks)

Hint: Instantaneous spike at $T_1$: Multiply each conc by $1.25$. Final $[\mathrm{O_2}]$ at $T_2 = 0.40 \times \frac{7}{6} = 0.467\text{ M}$. Use $2:2:1$ stoichiometry to determine changes in $[\mathrm{SO_2}]$ and $[\mathrm{SO_3}]$!

Model Answer & Rubric

Part (a) Calculation:

1. Concentrations immediately after $+25\%$ spike at $T_1$:

$$[\mathrm{SO_3}]_{T_1} = 0.30 \times 1.25 = 0.375\text{ M}, \quad [\mathrm{SO_2}]_{T_1} = 0.20 \times 1.25 = 0.250\text{ M}, \quad [\mathrm{O_2}]_{T_1} = 0.40 \times 1.25 = 0.500\text{ M}$$

2. Target $[\mathrm{O_2}]$ at $T_2 = 0.40 \times \left(1 + \frac{1}{6}\right) = 0.467\text{ M}$.

$$\Delta [\mathrm{O_2}] = 0.467 - 0.500 = -0.033\text{ M} \quad (\text{shift left})$$

3. Applying stoichiometric ratio $2\mathrm{SO_3} : 2\mathrm{SO_2} : 1\mathrm{O_2}$:

$$\Delta [\mathrm{SO_2}] = 2 \times (-0.033) = -0.067\text{ M} \implies [\mathrm{SO_2}]_{T_2} = 0.250 - 0.067 = 0.183\text{ M}$$ $$\Delta [\mathrm{SO_3}] = +2 \times (0.033) = +0.067\text{ M} \implies [\mathrm{SO_3}]_{T_2} = 0.375 + 0.067 = 0.442\text{ M}$$

Part (b) Explanation:

Le Chatelier's Principle: Reducing volume increases total pressure. The system counteracts this by shifting in the direction of fewer gas moles. The left side has $2\text{ moles}$ of gas while the right side has $3\text{ moles}$ ($2+1$), so equilibrium shifts left (reverse direction).

Collision Theory: Decreasing volume increases the particle concentration of all species. Because the reverse reaction involves 3 reactant gas particles ($2\mathrm{SO_2} + \mathrm{O_2}$), its collision frequency increases by a larger factor than the 2-particle forward reaction ($2\mathrm{SO_3}$), causing $r_{\text{reverse}} > r_{\text{forward}}$ until a new equilibrium is reached.

NESA Marking Schema:
1 Mark: Correctly computes spiked concentrations at $T_1$ ($0.375\text{ M}, 0.250\text{ M}, 0.500\text{ M}$).
1 Mark: Determines final $[\mathrm{O_2}] = 0.467\text{ M}$ and change $\Delta [\mathrm{O_2}] = -0.033\text{ M}$.
1 Mark: Uses $2:2:1$ stoichiometry to arrive at $[\mathrm{SO_2}] = 0.183\text{ M}$ and $[\mathrm{SO_3}] = 0.442\text{ M}$.
1 Mark: Explains LCP shift to left due to fewer gas moles ($3 \rightarrow 2$).
1 Mark: Explains collision frequency increase being greater for the 3-particle side.
1 Mark: Connects equalised rates at $T_2$ to the re-established plateau.

Question 6: Thermodynamics & Spontaneity Threshold

James Ruse 2021 6 Marks

Sulfur dioxide oxidation in the Contact Process is represented by: $$2\mathrm{SO_{2(g)}} + \mathrm{O_{2(g)}} \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{SO_{3(g)}} \quad \Delta H^\circ = -197\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$ Data: Standard molar entropies ($S^\circ$): $S^\circ(\mathrm{SO_2}) = 248.1\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$, $S^\circ(\mathrm{O_2}) = 205.2\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$, $S^\circ(\mathrm{SO_3}) = 256.8\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$.

(a) Calculate $\Delta S^\circ_{\text{system}}$ for this reaction in $\text{J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$. (2 Marks)
(b) Calculate $\Delta G^\circ$ at $298\text{ K}$ and deduce whether the reaction is spontaneous at room temperature. (2 Marks)
(c) Calculate the temperature threshold $T$ (in $\text{K}$ and $^\circ\mathrm{C}$) above which the reaction becomes non-spontaneous ($\Delta G^\circ > 0$). (2 Marks)

Hint: Watch your units! Convert $\Delta H^\circ$ to $\text{J mol}^{-1}$ ($-197,000\text{ J mol}^{-1}$) or $\Delta S^\circ$ to $\text{kJ K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$ before applying $\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$! Transition temperature occurs where $\Delta G^\circ = 0 \implies T = \frac{\Delta H^\circ}{\Delta S^\circ}$.

Model Answer & Rubric

Part (a) Calculation:

$$\Delta S^\circ = \sum S^\circ(\text{products}) - \sum S^\circ(\text{reactants})$$ $$\Delta S^\circ = 2(256.8) - [2(248.1) + 205.2] = 513.6 - 701.4 = -187.8\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$$

Part (b) Calculation:

$$\Delta H^\circ = -197,000\text{ J mol}^{-1}$$ $$\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ = -197,000 - 298(-187.8) = -197,000 + 55,964.4 = -141,036\text{ J mol}^{-1} = -141.0\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$

Since $\Delta G^\circ < 0$ (negative), the reaction is spontaneous at $298\text{ K}$.

Part (c) Calculation:

Setting $\Delta G^\circ = 0$:

$$T = \frac{\Delta H^\circ}{\Delta S^\circ} = \frac{-197,000\text{ J mol}^{-1}}{-187.8\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}} = 1049.0\text{ K}$$ $$T(^\circ\mathrm{C}) = 1049.0 - 273.15 = 775.85^\circ\mathrm{C} \approx 776^\circ\mathrm{C}$$
NESA Marking Schema:
1 Mark: Correctly calculates $\Delta S^\circ = -187.8\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$ showing stoichiometric working.
1 Mark: Converts units correctly ($\text{J}$ to $\text{kJ}$ or vice versa).
1 Mark: Calculates $\Delta G^\circ_{298} = -141.0\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$.
1 Mark: Deduces spontaneity correctly based on negative $\Delta G^\circ$ sign.
1 Mark: Sets $\Delta G^\circ = 0$ to derive $T = \frac{\Delta H}{\Delta S}$.
1 Mark: Calculates threshold $T = 1049\text{ K}$ ($776^\circ\mathrm{C}$).

Question 7: Collision Theory & Rate-Time Graph Analysis

James Ruse 2022 6 Marks

The thermal decomposition of dinitrogen tetroxide is an endothermic system: $$\mathrm{N_2O_{4(g)}} \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{NO_{2(g)}} \quad \Delta H^\circ = +58\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$ $(\text{colourless}) \quad \quad (\text{brown})$

(a) Account for the initial shapes of both forward and reverse rate curves from time $t=0$ until equilibrium is achieved in terms of collision theory. (4 Marks)
(b) Use Le Chatelier's Principle to explain changes to the macroscopic intensity of brown colour and the new rate equilibrium when the system is cooled. (2 Marks)

Hint: At $t=0$, $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]$ is at its peak while $[\mathrm{NO_2}] = 0$. Relate collision frequency of molecules exceeding $E_a$ to rate changes as reactant is consumed and product builds up!

Model Answer & Rubric

Part (a) Explanation:

At $t=0$, $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]$ is at maximum concentration, yielding a high frequency of successful collisions per unit time exceeding $E_a$, so initial forward rate $r_{\text{forward}}$ is maximum. As $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$ decomposes, $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]$ drops, reducing collision frequency and causing $r_{\text{forward}}$ to decrease exponentially.

Conversely, at $t=0$, $[\mathrm{NO_2}] = 0$, so reverse rate $r_{\text{reverse}} = 0$. As $\mathrm{NO_2}$ accumulates, rising $[\mathrm{NO_2}]$ increases collision frequency between $\mathrm{NO_2}$ molecules, causing $r_{\text{reverse}}$ to increase. Equilibrium is reached when $r_{\text{forward}} = r_{\text{reverse}} > 0$, forming a constant plateau.

Part (b) Explanation:

The forward reaction is endothermic ($\Delta H^\circ > 0$). Cooling removes thermal energy; by Le Chatelier's Principle, the system shifts left (reverse direction) to generate heat. $[\mathrm{NO_2}]$ decreases while $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]$ increases, causing the brown colour intensity to fade/become lighter. Both reaction rates decrease due to lower overall kinetic energy, settling at a new, lower equal rate equilibrium.

NESA Marking Schema:
1 Mark: Connects initial maximum forward rate to peak $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]$ and maximum collision frequency.
1 Mark: Explains decay of forward rate due to reactant consumption reducing collision rate.
1 Mark: Explains rise of reverse rate from zero as product $[\mathrm{NO_2}]$ builds up.
1 Mark: Identifies equilibrium plateau where $r_{\text{forward}} = r_{\text{reverse}} > 0$.
1 Mark: Applies LCP to deduce leftward shift on cooling for endothermic reaction.
1 Mark: States that brown colour fades and both rates decrease to a lower equal plateau.

Question 8: Spectrophotometric $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Determination

Abbotsleigh 2025 8 Marks

A student investigated the cobalt complexation equilibrium: $$[\mathrm{Co(H_2O)_6}]^{2+}_{(aq)} + 4\mathrm{Cl^-}_{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons [\mathrm{CoCl_4}]^{2-}_{(aq)} + 6\mathrm{H_2O}_{(l)}$$ $(\text{maroon-red}) \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad (\text{blue})$
An equilibrium solution prepared at $10^\circ\mathrm{C}$ had initial concentrations $[\mathrm{Co(H_2O)_6}^{2+}]_{\text{initial}} = 0.0382\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ and $[\mathrm{Cl^-}]_{\text{initial}} = 6.05\text{ mol L}^{-1}$.

(a) Justify the selection of $690\text{ nm}$ as the analytical wavelength for spectrophotometric measurement. (2 Marks)
(b) The equilibrium absorbance at $690\text{ nm}$ was $A = 0.236$. Using the Beer-Lambert calibration relation $A = 576 \times [[\mathrm{CoCl_4}]^{2-}]$, determine $[\mathrm{CoCl_4}^{2-}]_{\text{eq}}$. (2 Marks)
(c) Construct an ICE table and calculate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ at $10^\circ\mathrm{C}$. (4 Marks)

Hint: $[[\mathrm{CoCl_4}]^{2-}]_{\text{eq}} = \frac{0.236}{576}\text{ M}$. Write $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[[\mathrm{CoCl_4}]^{2-}]}{[[\mathrm{Co(H_2O)_6}]^{2+}][\mathrm{Cl^-}]^4}$. Remember to subtract $4x$ from $[\mathrm{Cl^-}]$ initial!

Model Answer & Rubric

Part (a) Justification:

At $690\text{ nm}$, the blue complex $[\mathrm{CoCl_4}]^{2-}$ exhibits maximum absorbance ($\lambda_{\max}$), whereas the maroon-red complex $[\mathrm{Co(H_2O)_6}]^{2+}$ has zero absorbance ($A = 0.00$). Measuring at $690\text{ nm}$ eliminates spectral interference from the reactant ion, ensuring valid concentration measurements.

Part (b) Calculation:

$$[[\mathrm{CoCl_4}]^{2-}]_{\text{eq}} = \frac{A}{576} = \frac{0.236}{576} = 4.10 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol L}^{-1} \quad (0.410\text{ mM})$$

Part (c) Calculation:

ICE Table ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$):

Species $[\mathrm{Co(H_2O)_6}]^{2+}$ $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ $[\mathrm{CoCl_4}]^{2-}$
Initial (M) 0.0382 6.05 0
Change (M) $-4.10 \times 10^{-4}$ $-4(4.10 \times 10^{-4})$ $+4.10 \times 10^{-4}$
Equilibrium (M) 0.03779 6.0484 $\approx 6.05$ $4.10 \times 10^{-4}$
$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[[\mathrm{CoCl_4}]^{2-}]}{[[\mathrm{Co(H_2O)_6}]^{2+}][\mathrm{Cl^-}]^4} = \frac{4.10 \times 10^{-4}}{(0.03779)(6.05)^4} = \frac{4.10 \times 10^{-4}}{(0.03779)(1339.8)} = 8.10 \times 10^{-6}$$

Question 9: Industrial Reaction Optimisation (Haber Process)

James Ruse 2022 8 Marks

The Haber process synthesises ammonia gas industrially: $$\mathrm{N_{2(g)}} + 3\mathrm{H_{2(g)}} \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{NH_{3(g)}} \quad \Delta H^\circ = -92.4\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$

(a) Identify from the graph the conditions that yield the theoretical maximum percentage of $\mathrm{NH_3}$. (1 Mark)
(b) Industrial plants operate at $200-250\text{ atm}$ and $400-450^\circ\mathrm{C}$ over a magnetite ($\mathrm{Fe_3O_4}$) catalyst. Justify why these compromise conditions are chosen over theoretical maximum conditions, integrating LCP, collision theory, economic considerations, and safety. (7 Marks)

Hint: Address temperature (exothermic yield vs kinetic rate $E_a$), pressure ($4:2$ gas mole ratio yield vs compressor costs/safety), magnetite catalyst role, and unreacted gas recycling!

Model Answer & Rubric

Part (a) Identification:

Part (b) Justification:

Temperature Compromise: The synthesis is exothermic ($\Delta H^\circ = -92.4\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$). By Le Chatelier's Principle, lowering temperature shifts equilibrium right, increasing yield. Operating at $200-250\text{ atm}$ yields optimal output safely and economically.

(b) Calculate the total number of moles of $\mathrm{O_{2(g)}}$ that were added to the vessel. (5 Marks)

By $2:2:1$ stoichiometry:

$$\Delta [\mathrm{NO}] = -0.0250\text{ M} \implies [\mathrm{NO}]_{\text{new\_eq}} = 0.500 - 0.0250 = 0.4750\text{ M}$$ $$\Delta [\mathrm{O_2}]_{\text{consumed}} = -\frac{0.0250}{2} = -0.0125\text{ M}$$

Cancelling $(0.4750)^2$ on top and bottom:

$$0.0075 + y = 0.02469 \implies y = 0.02469 - 0.0075 = 0.01719\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$ $$\text{Total moles of } \mathrm{O_2} \text{ added} = 0.01719\text{ mol L}^{-1} \times 10.0\text{ L} = 0.172\text{ moles}$$

Question 13: Cobalt Chloride Equilibrium & Collision Model

Knox Grammar 2022 7 Marks

Consider the cobalt chloride complexation equilibrium: $$[\mathrm{Co(H_2O)_6}]^{2+}_{(aq)} + 4\mathrm{Cl^-}_{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons [\mathrm{CoCl_4}]^{2-}_{(aq)} + 6\mathrm{H_2O}_{(l)} \quad \Delta H^\circ > 0$$ $(\text{pink}) \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad (\text{blue})$

(b) Explain, using collision theory and activation energy pathways, why $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ increases when temperature is increased for an endothermic reaction. (4 Marks)

Hint: Endothermic forward reaction has $E_{a,\text{forward}} > E_{a,\text{reverse}}$! Relate how temperature rise shifts Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution and increases $r_{\text{forward}}$ by a larger factor than $r_{\text{reverse}}$.

Model Answer & Rubric

Part (a) Explanation:

Image 1 (Blue) represents heating. Since the forward reaction is endothermic ($\Delta H^\circ > 0$), adding thermal energy causes the system to shift right (LCP) to absorb heat, generating blue $[\mathrm{CoCl_4}]^{2-}$.

Image 3 (Pink) represents cooling. Cooling removes heat, causing the system to shift left (exothermic direction) to release thermal energy, generating pink $[\mathrm{Co(H_2O)_6}]^{2+}$.

Part (b) Explanation:

For an endothermic reaction ($\Delta H^\circ > 0$), the forward reaction has a higher activation energy than the reverse reaction ($E_{a,\text{f}} > E_{a,\text{r}}$).

Increasing temperature shifts the Maxwell-Boltzmann kinetic energy distribution toward higher energies. Because $E_{a,\text{f}} > E_{a,\text{r}}$, the fraction of colliding particles gaining sufficient energy to exceed $E_a$ increases exponentially more for the forward reaction than for the reverse reaction.

Consequently, forward rate $r_{\text{f}}$ increases by a greater factor than reverse rate $r_{\text{r}}$, driving a net shift to the right until a new equilibrium is established with higher product concentrations and lower reactant concentrations. Because $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\text{products}]}{[\text{reactants}]}$, this net forward shift increases the magnitude of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

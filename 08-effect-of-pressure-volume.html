1. Physical Foundations: Gas Pressure, Volume, & Molar Concentration

Gas Phase Physics

To understand how pressure and volume changes disturb chemical equilibrium, we must first establish the fundamental physical relationship governing gases in a closed vessel. Gas pressure is a macroscopic manifestation of sub-microscopic particle collisions: it represents the total momentum transferred per second as gas molecules collide with the interior walls of their container.

According to the Ideal Gas Law:

$$PV = nRT$$

Where $P$ is total gas pressure ($\text{kPa}$ or $\text{atm}$), $V$ is container volume ($\text{L}$ or $\text{m}^3$), $n$ is total gas moles ($\text{mol}$), $R$ is the universal gas constant ($8.314\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$), and $T$ is absolute temperature ($\text{K}$).

Rearranging this equation yields a direct expression for molar concentration ($C_i = n_i / V$) and partial pressure ($P_i$) of any individual gas species $i$:

$$C_i = \frac{n_i}{V} = \frac{P_i}{RT} \quad \implies \quad P_i = C_i RT$$

The Inverse Volume-Concentration Law

At constant temperature ($T$), the molar concentration ($C_i$) and partial pressure ($P_i$) of every gaseous species in a container are inversely proportional to the vessel volume ($V$):

• Compression (Halving Volume, $V \to \frac{1}{2}V$): The total pressure instantly doubles ($P_{\text{total}} \to 2P_{\text{total}}$), and the concentration of every single gaseous species instantly doubles ($C_i \to 2C_i$).
• Expansion (Doubling Volume, $V \to 2V$): The total pressure is instantly halved ($P_{\text{total}} \to \frac{1}{2}P_{\text{total}}$), and the concentration of every single gaseous species is instantly halved ($C_i \to \frac{1}{2}C_i$).

2. Le Chatelier's Principle & Qualitative Shift Rules

System Adjustments

When a gas reaction system at dynamic equilibrium experiences a sudden change in volume or pressure, the balance between forward and reverse reaction rates is disrupted. Le Chatelier's Principle allows us to make immediate, qualitative predictions regarding the shift direction.

📦

1. Decreasing Volume / Increasing Pressure

• Stress Imposed: Total container volume decreases ($V \downarrow$), causing total pressure and all gas concentrations to rise ($P \uparrow$, $C_i \uparrow$).
• System Objective (LCP): The system acts to partially counteract the pressure increase by reducing the total number of gas molecules in the vessel.
• Shift Direction: Shifts towards the side of the balanced equation with FEWER stoichiometric gas moles ($\Delta n_{\text{gas}}$).

🎈

2. Increasing Volume / Decreasing Pressure

• Stress Imposed: Total container volume increases ($V \uparrow$), causing total pressure and all gas concentrations to drop ($P \downarrow$, $C_i \downarrow$).
• System Objective (LCP): The system acts to partially counteract the pressure drop by producing additional gas molecules to replenish vessel pressure.
• Shift Direction: Shifts towards the side of the balanced equation with MORE stoichiometric gas moles ($\Delta n_{\text{gas}}$).

How to Calculate Stoichiometric Gas Mole Differentials ($\Delta n_{\text{gas}}$)

To determine the shift direction, count only gaseous species ($\text{g}$) on each side of the balanced equation: $$\Delta n_{\text{gas}} = \sum n_{\text{gas, products}} - \sum n_{\text{gas, reactants}}$$
• Example 1 (Ammonia Synthesis): $\mathrm{N_{2(g)} + 3H_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2NH_{3(g)}}$
Reactants = $1 + 3 = 4\text{ gas moles}$; Products = $2\text{ gas moles}$.
Halving volume shifts Forward ($\to$) towards fewer gas moles ($2 < 4$).

• Example 2 (Methane Steam Reforming): $\mathrm{CH_{4(g)} + H_2O_{(g)} \rightleftharpoons CO_{(g)} + 3H_{2(g)}}$
Reactants = $1 + 1 = 2\text{ gas moles}$; Products = $1 + 3 = 4\text{ gas moles}$.
Halving volume shifts Reverse ($\leftarrow$) towards fewer gas moles ($2 < 4$).

3. Benchmark Practical Gas System: $\mathrm{2NO_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons N_2O_{4(g)}}$

Syllabus Benchmark System

The NESA Stage 6 Chemistry syllabus explicitly highlights the reversible gaseous equilibrium between nitrogen dioxide ($\mathrm{NO_2}$) and dinitrogen tetroxide ($\mathrm{N_2O_4}$) as the primary model system for investigating volume, pressure, and temperature disturbances.

Chemical & Optical Profile

$$\mathrm{\underset{\text{Dark Brown Gas}}{2NO_{2(g)}} \rightleftharpoons \underset{\text{Colourless Gas}}{N_2O_{4(g)}}} \quad \Delta H^\circ = -57.2\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$
Chemical Species Molecular Formula Stoichiometric Gas Moles Gas Appearance / Colour
Nitrogen Dioxide $\mathrm{NO_2(g)}$ 2 Moles (Reactant side) Intense Dark Reddish-Brown
Dinitrogen Tetroxide $\mathrm{N_2O_4(g)}$ 1 Mole (Product side) Completely Colourless

Detailed Step-by-Step Compression Analysis (Gas Syringe Experiment)

Imagine an equilibrium mixture of $\mathrm{NO_2}$ and $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$ sealed inside a transparent glass syringe at constant room temperature. The gas displays a medium brown tint. The plunger is suddenly pushed inward, halving the syringe volume from $100\text{ mL}$ to $50\text{ mL}$ ($V \to \frac{1}{2}V$).

Phase 1: Instantaneous Disturbance ($t = t_1$)

• Physical Change: The gas volume is halved instantly.
• Concentration Effect: Molar concentrations of both gases instantly double: $[\mathrm{NO_2}]_i = 2[\mathrm{NO_2}]_0$ and $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_i = 2[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_0$.
• Visual Signature: The gas inside the syringe instantly turns much DARKER brown! This rapid darkening is purely optical—twice as many brown $\mathrm{NO_2}$ molecules are squeezed into each cubic centimetre of space before any chemical reaction has time to occur.

Phase 2: Transition Shift ($t_1 < t < t_2$)

• LCP Response: The total pressure inside the syringe has doubled. To counteract this stress, the system shifts towards the side with fewer gas moles ($1\text{ mole of }\mathrm{N_2O_4} < 2\text{ moles of }\mathrm{NO_2}$).
• Shift Direction: Net Forward Shift ($\to$).
• Chemical Transformation: Two molecules of brown $\mathrm{NO_2}$ combine to form one molecule of colourless $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$.
• Visual Signature: Over the following seconds, the dark brown colour gradually fades / lightens, settling at a new equilibrium tint that is lighter than the peak spike, but still slightly darker than the initial baseline!

HSC Exam Visual Pitfall: The Two-Stage Colour Sequence

When describing the compression of $\mathrm{2NO_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons N_2O_{4(g)}}$, you MUST describe both sequential visual phases to earn full NESA marks:

1. Instantaneous Effect at $t_1$: The gas turns darker brown immediately due to volume compression squeezing molecules closer together ($C = n/V \uparrow$).
2. Equilibrium Shift Effect ($t_1 \to t_2$): The gas gradually fades to a lighter brown as the net forward shift converts brown $\mathrm{NO_2}$ into colourless $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$ to lower total pressure.
3. Final State Comparison: At the new equilibrium, $[\mathrm{NO_2}]_{\text{eq2}}$ remains slightly higher than $[\mathrm{NO_2}]_{\text{eq1}}$ due to partial counteraction, so the final colour is slightly darker than the original starting mixture, but significantly lighter than the peak instant spike!

4. Collision Theory Kinetic Mechanism for Pressure/Volume Shifts

Microscopic Kinetics

Le Chatelier's Principle predicts what shift occurs, but Collision Theory explains why it occurs at the sub-microscopic particle level. In NESA HSC examinations, high-mark response rubrics require a complete kinetic justification based on collision frequencies ($Z$) and rate laws.

Rate Laws & Molecularity Analysis for $\mathrm{2NO_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons N_2O_{4(g)}}$

• Forward Reaction ($\to$): Bi-molecular combination. Two $\mathrm{NO_2}$ molecules must collide with sufficient energy ($E \ge E_{a,\text{f}}$) and correct orientation:

$$r_{\mathrm{f}} = k_{\mathrm{f}} [\mathrm{NO_2}]^2$$

• Reverse Reaction ($\leftarrow$): Mono-molecular decomposition. Single $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$ molecules undergo thermal dissociation:

$$r_{\mathrm{r}} = k_{\mathrm{r}} [\mathrm{N_2O_4}]$$

Mathematical Proof of Rate Imbalance During Compression

Suppose the system is initially at dynamic equilibrium ($r_{\mathrm{f},0} = r_{\mathrm{r},0}$). The volume is suddenly halved ($V \to \frac{1}{2}V$), instantly doubling all concentrations ($[\text{Species}]_i = 2[\text{Species}]_0$).

Substitute these doubled concentrations into the forward and reverse rate laws:

$$\text{New Forward Rate: } r_{\mathrm{f},i} = k_{\mathrm{f}} (2[\mathrm{NO_2}]_0)^2 = k_{\mathrm{f}} \cdot 4[\mathrm{NO_2}]_0^2 = 4 \cdot r_{\mathrm{f},0}$$ $$\text{New Reverse Rate: } r_{\mathrm{r},i} = k_{\mathrm{r}} (2[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_0) = 2 \cdot r_{\mathrm{r},0}$$

The Kinetic Foundation of Pressure Shifts

Notice the dramatic kinetic asymmetry:

• Doubling concentrations causes the forward rate to jump by a factor of 4 ($4\times$) because two $\mathrm{NO_2}$ molecules are involved in the forward collision step ($[\mathrm{NO_2}]^2$).
• Doubling concentrations causes the reverse rate to jump by a factor of only 2 ($2\times$) because only one $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$ molecule is involved in reverse decomposition ($[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]^1$).
• Therefore, immediately following compression: $$r_{\mathrm{f},i} = 4 r_0 > r_{\mathrm{r},i} = 2 r_0 \quad \implies \quad r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$$
• Because the forward rate is twice as fast as the reverse rate, a net forward transformation occurs! As $\mathrm{NO_2}$ is consumed and $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$ accumulates, $r_{\mathrm{f}}$ gradually decreases and $r_{\mathrm{r}}$ gradually increases until $r_{\mathrm{f}}' = r_{\mathrm{r}}'$ at a new, higher equal rate.

5. Mathematical Rigour: Reaction Quotient ($Q_c$ and $Q_p$) Proofs

Quantitative Proof

In addition to collision theory, we can mathematically prove the shift direction caused by volume changes by evaluating the Reaction Quotient ($Q_c$) relative to the equilibrium constant ($K_{\mathrm{eq}}$).

For the equilibrium $\mathrm{2NO_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons N_2O_{4(g)}}$, the equilibrium expression is:

$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_{\text{eq}}}{[\mathrm{NO_2}]_{\text{eq}}^2}$$

1. Quantitative Proof for Volume Compression ($V \to \frac{1}{2}V$)

When volume is halved, instantaneous concentrations become $[\mathrm{NO_2}]_i = 2[\mathrm{NO_2}]_{\text{eq}}$ and $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_i = 2[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_{\text{eq}}$.
Substituting into $Q_c$:

$$Q_c = \frac{[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_i}{[\mathrm{NO_2}]_i^2} = \frac{2[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_{\text{eq}}}{(2[\mathrm{NO_2}]_{\text{eq}})^2} = \frac{2[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_{\text{eq}}}{4[\mathrm{NO_2}]_{\text{eq}}^2} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_{\text{eq}}}{[\mathrm{NO_2}]_{\text{eq}}^2} = \frac{1}{2} K_{\mathrm{eq}}$$

Mathematical Conclusion for Compression

$$Q_c = 0.5 K_{\mathrm{eq}} < K_{\mathrm{eq}}$$
Because $Q_c < K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, the numerator (product $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$) is mathematically too small relative to the squared denominator (reactant $\mathrm{NO_2}$). To restore $Q_c$ back up to $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, the system MUST shift in the FORWARD direction ($\to$)!

2. Quantitative Proof for Volume Expansion ($V \to 2V$)

When volume is doubled, instantaneous concentrations become $[\mathrm{NO_2}]_i = 0.5[\mathrm{NO_2}]_{\text{eq}}$ and $[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_i = 0.5[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_{\text{eq}}$.
Substituting into $Q_c$:

$$Q_c = \frac{[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_i}{[\mathrm{NO_2}]_i^2} = \frac{0.5[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_{\text{eq}}}{(0.5[\mathrm{NO_2}]_{\text{eq}})^2} = \frac{0.5[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_{\text{eq}}}{0.25[\mathrm{NO_2}]_{\text{eq}}^2} = 2 \cdot \frac{[\mathrm{N_2O_4}]_{\text{eq}}}{[\mathrm{NO_2}]_{\text{eq}}^2} = 2 K_{\mathrm{eq}}$$

Mathematical Conclusion for Expansion

$$Q_c = 2 K_{\mathrm{eq}} > K_{\mathrm{eq}}$$
Because $Q_c > K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, the numerator is mathematically too large. To reduce $Q_c$ back down to $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, the system MUST shift in the REVERSE direction ($\leftarrow$)!

6. Crucial NESA Edge Cases & Examination Pitfalls

High-Frequency Exam Traps

NESA examiners routinely construct tricky questions around three specific edge cases that catch unprepared students off-guard.

Edge Case 1: Equal Gas Moles ($\Delta n_{\text{gas}} = 0$)

• Reaction Example: $\mathrm{H_{2(g)} + I_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2HI_{(g)}}$ ($\Delta n_{\text{gas}} = 2 - 2 = 0$).
• Disturbance: Container volume is halved ($V \to \frac{1}{2}V$).
• $Q_c$ Proof: $Q_c = \frac{(2[\mathrm{HI}]_{\text{eq}})^2}{(2[\mathrm{H_2}]_{\text{eq}})(2[\mathrm{I_2}]_{\text{eq}})} = \frac{4[\mathrm{HI}]_{\text{eq}}^2}{4[\mathrm{H_2}]_{\text{eq}}[\mathrm{I_2}]_{\text{eq}}} = K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.
• Outcome: $Q_c$ remains exactly equal to $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$! Therefore, NO SHIFT OCCURS. All concentrations double, but relative proportions are unchanged.

Edge Case 2: Inert Gas Addition

• Rigid Vessel ($V = \text{Constant}$): Injecting Argon increases total pressure ($P_{\text{total}} \uparrow$), but because volume is fixed, molar concentrations ($C_i = n_i/V$) and partial pressures ($P_i$) of reacting gases are completely unchanged. NO SHIFT OCCURS!
• Moveable Piston ($P = \text{Constant}$): Injecting Argon forces vessel volume to expand ($V \uparrow$) to keep pressure constant. This volume expansion dilutes all reacting gases, causing a SHIFT towards MORE gas moles!

Edge Case 3: Heterogeneous Equilibria (Solids & Liquids)

• Reaction Example: $\mathrm{C_{(s)} + CO_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2CO_{(g)}}$.
• Counting Rule: Pure solids ($\text{s}$) and pure liquids ($\text{l}$) have fixed active densities and DO NOT count towards gas mole totals!
• Mole Calculation: Reactant side has $1\text{ gas mole}$ ($\mathrm{CO_2}$); Product side has $2\text{ gas moles}$ ($\mathrm{CO}$).
• Outcome: Compressing this system shifts Reverse ($\leftarrow$) towards $1\text{ gas mole}$. Solid carbon ($\mathrm{C_{(s)}}$) is IGNORED during mole counting!

Worked Example 1 (Easy): Qualitative Gas Shift Prediction

2 Marks

Predict the direction of the equilibrium shift when the volume of the reaction container is halved for the following gas-phase reaction at constant temperature: $$\mathrm{PCl_{5(g)} \rightleftharpoons PCl_{3(g)} + Cl_{2(g)}}$$

Model Solution:

1. Count Stoichiometric Gas Moles:
• Reactant side = $1\text{ mole of }\mathrm{PCl_5(g)}$.
• Product side = $1 + 1 = 2\text{ moles of gas}$ ($\mathrm{PCl_3 + Cl_2}$).

2. Apply Le Chatelier's Principle: Halving container volume increases total pressure. To counteract this pressure increase, the system shifts towards the side with fewer gas moles ($1 < 2$).

3. Conclusion: The system undergoes a net reverse shift ($\leftarrow$), converting $\mathrm{PCl_3}$ and $\mathrm{Cl_2}$ back into $\mathrm{PCl_5}$.

Worked Example 2 (Medium): Two-Stage Colour Sequence Explanation

3 Marks

A syringe containing an equilibrium mixture of $\mathrm{2NO_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons N_2O_{4(g)}}$ is compressed rapidly.
Describe and explain the two-stage color change observed inside the syringe.

Model Solution:

1. Stage 1 (Instantaneous Effect at $t_1$): The gas instantly turns darker brown. Compressing the syringe volume ($V \downarrow$) squeezes all gas particles closer together, doubling molar concentrations ($C = n/V \uparrow$) before chemical reaction occurs.

2. Stage 2 (Equilibrium Shift at $t_1 \to t_2$): The gas gradually fades to a lighter brown tint. To counteract the pressure increase, the system undergoes a net forward shift ($\to$) towards the side with fewer gas moles ($1\text{ mole of }\mathrm{N_2O_4} < 2\text{ moles of }\mathrm{NO_2}$). Two molecules of brown $\mathrm{NO_2}$ combine into one molecule of colourless $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$.

3. Final State: The final colour is slightly darker than the initial starting baseline (due to partial counteraction), but lighter than the peak instant spike.

Worked Example 3 (Hard): Collision Theory Justification for Gas Compression

4 Marks

Explain, using Collision Theory and reaction rate laws, why increasing total pressure causes a net forward shift in the Haber process reaction: $$\mathrm{N_{2(g)} + 3H_{2(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2NH_{3(g)}}$$

Model Solution:

1. Initial Dynamic Equilibrium: $r_{\mathrm{f}} = r_{\mathrm{r}}$, where forward collisions between $\mathrm{N_2}$ and $\mathrm{H_2}$ equal reverse decomposition collisions of $\mathrm{NH_3}$.

2. Pressure Increase Effect: Increasing total pressure by compressing volume increases particle concentrations per unit volume for all gases.

3. Rate Law Dependence:
• Forward reaction is multi-molecular ($1\text{ molecule of }\mathrm{N_2} + 3\text{ molecules of }\mathrm{H_2} = 4\text{ reactant particles}$): $r_{\mathrm{f}} = k_{\mathrm{f}} [\mathrm{N_2}][\mathrm{H_2}]^3$.
• Reverse reaction involves only $2\text{ product molecules}$: $r_{\mathrm{r}} = k_{\mathrm{r}} [\mathrm{NH_3}]^2$.

4. Rate Imbalance & Re-equalisation: Because the forward reaction depends on a higher overall concentration power ($4\text{th order}$) than the reverse reaction ($2\text{nd order}$), compressing the volume increases forward collision frequency ($Z_{\mathrm{f}}$) by a much larger factor than reverse collision frequency ($Z_{\mathrm{r}}$). Thus, $r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$, producing a net forward shift ($\to$) until new equilibrium rates match.

Worked Example 4 (Extension): Reaction Quotient ($Q_c$) Proof for Gas Expansion

5 Marks

An equilibrium gas mixture $\mathrm{2A_{(g)} + B_{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2C_{(g)}}$ has $K_{\mathrm{eq}} = 50.0\text{ M}^{-1}$ at $300\text{ K}$.
At equilibrium, $[\mathrm{A}] = 0.20\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{B}] = 0.10\text{ M}$, and $[\mathrm{C}] = 0.45\text{ M}$.
The container volume is suddenly doubled ($V \times 2$) at constant temperature.
a) Calculate instantaneous concentrations immediately following volume expansion.
b) Calculate $Q_c$ and prove mathematically which direction the system must shift to restore dynamic equilibrium.

Model Solution:

a) Instantaneous Concentrations:
Doubling vessel volume halves all molar concentrations instantly: $$[\mathrm{A}]_i = 0.10\text{ M}, \quad [\mathrm{B}]_i = 0.050\text{ M}, \quad [\mathrm{C}]_i = 0.225\text{ M}$$

b) Calculate $Q_c$ and Prove Shift Direction:

$$Q_c = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]_i^2}{[\mathrm{A}]_i^2 [\mathrm{B}]_i} = \frac{(0.225)^2}{(0.10)^2 (0.050)} = \frac{0.050625}{(0.010)(0.050)} = \frac{0.050625}{0.00050} = 101.25\text{ M}^{-1}$$

Mathematical Comparison: $Q_c (101.25) > K_{\mathrm{eq}} (50.0)$.

Because $Q_c > K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, the numerator (product concentration) is mathematically too large relative to the denominator. The system must shift in REVERSE ($\leftarrow$) to convert product $\mathrm{C}$ back into reactants $\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathrm{B}$ until $Q_c$ decreases back to $50.0$.
This rigorously confirms LCP: Doubling volume shifts towards the side with more gas moles ($3\text{ gas moles on reactant side} > 2\text{ gas moles on product side}$)!

A gas reaction $\mathrm{A_{(g)} + 2B_{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2C_{(g)}}$ is at equilibrium in a $5.0\text{ L}$ vessel at $400\text{ K}$ with $[\mathrm{A}] = 0.20\text{ M}$, $[\mathrm{B}] = 0.30\text{ M}$, and $[\mathrm{C}] = 0.60\text{ M}$.
The vessel volume is suddenly compressed to $2.5\text{ L}$ at constant temperature.
a) Calculate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ and the instantaneous Reaction Quotient ($Q_c$) post-compression.
b) Prove the shift direction using both $Q_c$ and Collision Theory rate laws.

Hint: Halving volume doubles concentrations ($0.40\text{ M}, 0.60\text{ M}, 1.20\text{ M}$). Calculate $Q_c = [\mathrm{C}]^2 / ([\mathrm{A}][\mathrm{B}]^2)$.

Model Answer & Rubric

a) Calculate $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ and Instantaneous $Q_c$:

$$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]^2}{[\mathrm{A}][\mathrm{B}]^2} = \frac{(0.60)^2}{(0.20)(0.30)^2} = \frac{0.36}{(0.20)(0.09)} = \frac{0.36}{0.018} = 20.0\text{ M}^{-1}$$

Halving volume doubles all concentrations: $[\mathrm{A}]_i = 0.40\text{ M}, [\mathrm{B}]_i = 0.60\text{ M}, [\mathrm{C}]_i = 1.20\text{ M}$.

$$Q_c = \frac{(1.20)^2}{(0.40)(0.60)^2} = \frac{1.44}{(0.40)(0.36)} = \frac{1.44}{0.144} = 10.0\text{ M}^{-1}$$

b) Shift Proofs:
• $Q_c$ Mathematical Proof: $Q_c (10.0) < K_{\mathrm{eq}} (20.0)$. Because $Q_c < K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, the numerator (product) is too small. The system must shift FORWARD ($\to$) to form more product $\mathrm{C}$.
• Collision Theory Proof: $r_{\mathrm{f}} = k_{\mathrm{f}} [\mathrm{A}][\mathrm{B}]^2$ ($3\text{rd order}$) vs $r_{\mathrm{r}} = k_{\mathrm{r}} [\mathrm{C}]^2$ ($2\text{nd order}$). Doubling concentrations increases $r_{\mathrm{f}}$ by $2 \times 2^2 = 8\times$, while $r_{\mathrm{r}}$ increases by $2^2 = 4\times$. Because $r_{\mathrm{f}} = 8 r_0 > r_{\mathrm{r}} = 4 r_0$, $r_{\mathrm{f}} > r_{\mathrm{r}}$, causing a net forward shift ($\to$)!

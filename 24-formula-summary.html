2. Complete Module 5 Mathematical Formula Matrix

Formula Cheat Sheet

Every equation required for quantitative equilibrium, reaction quotient, and solution solubility calculations in Module 5:

Formula Name / Concept Mathematical Expression Variable Definitions & SI Units Application Context
Molarity Concentration $$c = \frac{n}{V}$$ $c$: Conc ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$), $n$: Moles ($\text{mol}$), $V$: Volume ($\text{L}$) Quantitative solution stoichiometry.
Dilution Formula $$c_1 V_1 = c_2 V_2$$ $c_1, c_2$: Initial/final conc ($\text{M}$), $V_1, V_2$: Initial/final vol ($\text{L}$ or $\text{mL}$) Pre-calculation dilution before $Q_{\mathrm{sp}}$ evaluation.
Equilibrium Constant ($K_{\mathrm{eq}}$) $$K_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]^c [\mathrm{D}]^d}{[\mathrm{A}]^a [\mathrm{B}]^b}$$ $[\mathrm{A}], [\mathrm{B}], [\mathrm{C}], [\mathrm{D}]$: Equilibrium conc ($\text{M}$), lower case: Stoichiometric coefficients Homogeneous system at dynamic equilibrium (solids/pure liquids omitted).
Reaction Quotient ($Q$ or $Q_{\mathrm{sp}}$) $$Q = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]_i^c [\mathrm{D}]_i^d}{[\mathrm{A}]_i^a [\mathrm{B}]_i^b}$$ $[\mathrm{X}]_i$: Instantaneous non-equilibrium initial concentrations ($\text{M}$) Comparing with $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ or $K_{\mathrm{sp}}$ to predict shift direction.
Solubility Product ($K_{\mathrm{sp}}$) $$K_{\mathrm{sp}} = [\mathrm{M}^{m+}]^x [\mathrm{A}^{n-}]^y$$ $[\mathrm{M}^{m+}], [\mathrm{A}^{n-}]$: Equilibrium dissolved ion concentrations ($\text{M}$) Saturated heterogenous dissolution of sparingly soluble salt $\mathrm{M}_x \mathrm{A}_{y(s)}$.
$1:1$ Salt Molar Solubility ($s$) $$s = \sqrt{K_{\mathrm{sp}}}$$ $s$: Molar solubility ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$), $K_{\mathrm{sp}} = s^2$ Salts like $\mathrm{AgCl}, \mathrm{BaSO_4}, \mathrm{CaCO_3}$.
$1:2$ or $2:1$ Salt Solubility ($s$) $$s = \sqrt[3]{\frac{K_{\mathrm{sp}}}{4}}$$ $s$: Molar solubility ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$), $K_{\mathrm{sp}} = 4s^3$ Salts like $\mathrm{PbCl_2}, \mathrm{CaF_2}, \mathrm{Ag_2CrO_4}$.
$1:3$ Salt Molar Solubility ($s$) $$s = \sqrt[4]{\frac{K_{\mathrm{sp}}}{27}}$$ $s$: Molar solubility ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$), $K_{\mathrm{sp}} = 27s^4$ Salts like $\mathrm{Fe(OH)_3}, \mathrm{Al(OH)_3}$.
$2:3$ Salt Molar Solubility ($s$) $$s = \sqrt[5]{\frac{K_{\mathrm{sp}}}{108}}$$ $s$: Molar solubility ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$), $K_{\mathrm{sp}} = 108s^5$ Salts like $\mathrm{Ca_3(PO_4)_2}, \mathrm{Bi_2S_3}$.
Mass Solubility ($S_{\text{mass}}$) $$S_{\text{mass}} = s \times M$$ $S_{\text{mass}}$: Mass solubility ($\text{g L}^{-1}$), $M$: Molar mass ($\text{g mol}^{-1}$) Converting molar solubility to gravimetric mass per litre.
Maximum Dissolved Mass $$m_{\text{max}} = s \times V \times M$$ $m_{\text{max}}$: Mass ($\text{g}$), $V$: Volume ($\text{L}$), $M$: Molar mass ($\text{g mol}^{-1}$) Mass limit before precipitation in volume $V$.
Gibbs Free Energy ($\Delta G^\circ$) $$\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T \Delta S^\circ$$ $\Delta G^\circ$: Free energy ($\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$), $\Delta H^\circ$: Enthalpy ($\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$), $\Delta S^\circ$: Entropy ($\text{kJ K}^{-1} \text{ mol}^{-1}$), $T$: Temp ($\text{K}$) Evaluating reaction spontaneity ($\Delta G^\circ < 0$ is spontaneous).
Thermodynamic $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ & $\Delta G^\circ$ $$\Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K_{\mathrm{eq}}$$ $R = 8.314\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$, $T$: Temp ($\text{K}$), $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$: Constant Relating equilibrium position to standard Gibbs free energy.

5. Bite-Sized Module 5 Core Concept Summaries

High-Yield Content Review

Phase A: Foundations of Equilibrium

• Reversible Reactions: Reactants convert to products and products convert back to reactants simultaneously ($\rightleftharpoons$). Occurs when activation energy $E_a$ for both forward and reverse pathways is accessible.
• Open vs Closed Systems: Closed systems exchange energy but NOT matter with surroundings (mandatory for dynamic equilibrium). Open systems exchange both matter and energy.
• Dynamic Equilibrium Criteria: 1. Closed system, 2. Macroscopic properties constant, 3. Microscopic rates equal ($\text{Rate}_{\text{forward}} = \text{Rate}_{\text{reverse}} > 0$), 4. Both reactants and products present.
• Non-Equilibrium Systems: Combustion ($\Delta H \ll 0, \Delta S > 0$) and Photosynthesis are essentially irreversible due to extreme enthalpy changes or continuous open-system mass transfer.

Phase B: Factors Affecting Equilibrium (LCP)

• Le Chatelier's Principle (LCP): If a dynamic equilibrium is subject to an external change in conditions (concentration, pressure, volume, temperature), the system shifts in a direction that partially counteracts that change.
• Concentration Shift: Adding species shifts AWAY from addition; removing species shifts TOWARDS removal.
• Pressure / Volume Shift: Decreasing volume (increasing pressure) shifts equilibrium towards the side with FEWER moles of gas particles.
• Temperature Shift: Heating an exothermic reaction ($\Delta H < 0$) shifts equilibrium LEFT ($\leftarrow$), decreasing $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$. Heating an endothermic reaction ($\Delta H > 0$) shifts equilibrium RIGHT ($\to$), increasing $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$.
• Collision Theory Mechanism: Concentration/pressure changes alter collision frequencies ($Z$). Temperature changes alter both collision frequency AND energy distribution relative to $E_a$.

Phase C: The Equilibrium Constant ($K_{\mathrm{eq}}$)

• $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ Significance: $K_{\mathrm{eq}} \gg 1$ indicates products favoured at equilibrium; $K_{\mathrm{eq}} \ll 1$ indicates reactants favoured.
• Pure Solids & Liquids: Solids ($\text{s}$) and pure solvents ($\text{l}$) have constant effective activity $= 1$ and are omitted from $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expressions.
• Temperature Dependence: Temperature is the ONLY variable that alters the numerical value of $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$!
• Reaction Quotient $Q$: Evaluates non-equilibrium states. If $Q < K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, system shifts RIGHT ($\to$). If $Q > K_{\mathrm{eq}}$, system shifts LEFT ($\leftarrow$).

Phase D: Solution Equilibria & Solubility

• Dissolution Mechanics: Ion-dipole forces between polar water molecules and ionic lattice overcome lattice enthalpy ($\Delta H_{\text{lattice}}$) to form hydrated aqueous ions.
• Indigenous Cycad Detoxification: Traditional Aboriginal Peoples use leaching (flowing water) to exploit dynamic solubility equilibrium, washing water-soluble neurotoxins (cycasin) out of crushed cycad seeds.
• Precipitation Prediction: Dilute stock solutions ($c_1 V_1 = c_2 V_2$), calculate $Q_{\mathrm{sp}}$, compare with $K_{\mathrm{sp}}$. If $Q_{\mathrm{sp}} > K_{\mathrm{sp}}$, precipitate forms.

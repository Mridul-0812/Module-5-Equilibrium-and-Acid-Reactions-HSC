1. Reversibility Spectrum & Non-Equilibrium Boundaries

Thermodynamic Foundations

In earlier lessons of Module 5, we established that many chemical reactions are reversible and proceed in closed systems to establish a state of dynamic equilibrium. At dynamic equilibrium, the rates of the forward and reverse reactions are equal and non-zero ($r_{\mathrm{forward}} = r_{\mathrm{reverse}} > 0$), resulting in constant macroscopic concentrations of both reactants and products.

However, a vast array of vital biological, environmental, and industrial chemical reactions do NOT reach dynamic chemical equilibrium. These systems are classified as non-equilibrium systems. A non-equilibrium system is one that proceeds virtually in one direction until at least one limiting reactant is completely consumed. Such reactions are effectively irreversible under ambient physical conditions.

⛓️

Equilibrium Systems

Reactions where the activation energies for both forward and reverse pathways ($E_{a,\text{f}}$ and $E_{a,\text{r}}$) are moderately accessible at ambient temperatures, occurring inside a closed system boundary.

  • State: Dynamic equilibrium ($r_{\mathrm{f}} = r_{\mathrm{r}} > 0$).
  • Gibbs Free Energy: System seeks minimum free energy; at equilibrium, $\Delta G = 0$.
  • Reversibility: Both reactants and products are present simultaneously in non-zero concentrations.
  • Example: $\mathrm{N_2O_4(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NO_2(g)}$ in a sealed glass vessel.
⚡

Non-Equilibrium Systems

Reactions driven continuously in one direction by extreme thermodynamic enthalpy/entropy changes or open system boundary mass losses.

  • State: Driven to 100% completion ($r_{\mathrm{f}} \gg r_{\mathrm{r}} \approx 0$).
  • Gibbs Free Energy: Unidirectional free energy change ($\Delta G^\circ \ll 0$ or continuously coupled solar photon input).
  • Reversibility: Effectively irreversible; reverse rate is practically zero.
  • Examples: Complete combustion of hydrocarbons; biological photosynthesis in autotrophs.

System Boundary Influence on Non-Equilibrium Behaviour

System boundaries dictate whether matter can escape:
• Open Systems: Permit the exchange of both matter and energy with surroundings. If gaseous or volatile products escape into the room atmosphere, their effective concentration inside the reaction vessel drops toward zero. Without product molecules present, reverse collision rates become zero ($r_{\mathrm{reverse}} = 0$), forcing the forward reaction to proceed to completion.
• Closed Systems: Permit energy transfer but prevent mass exchange ($\Delta m = 0$). Even inside a closed system, certain reactions fail to establish equilibrium because the thermodynamic driving forces ($\Delta H^\circ$ and $\Delta S^\circ$) generate an immensely negative $\Delta G^\circ$, creating a reverse activation energy barrier ($E_{a,\text{reverse}}$) so large that no reverse collisions possess sufficient energy to react.

2. Enthalpy ($\Delta H^\circ$) and Entropy ($\Delta S^\circ$) Mechanics

Thermodynamic Laws

Every chemical transformation is governed by two fundamental thermodynamic driving forces defined by the Laws of Thermodynamics:

1. The Enthalpy Drive ($\Delta H^\circ$)

Enthalpy ($H$) represents the total internal heat energy stored within chemical bonds and intermolecular forces at constant pressure. The change in standard enthalpy ($\Delta H^\circ$) during a chemical reaction represents the net heat absorbed or evolved:

$$\Delta H^\circ = H_{\text{products}}^\circ - H_{\text{reactants}}^\circ = \sum \text{Bond Energies (Broken)} - \sum \text{Bond Energies (Formed)}$$
  • Exothermic Processes ($\Delta H^\circ < 0$): Energy released when forming product bonds is greater than the energy required to break reactant bonds. Heat is released to the surroundings. Systems naturally tend toward states of lower potential energy, making exothermic processes thermodynamically favourable.
  • Endothermic Processes ($\Delta H^\circ > 0$): Energy required to break reactant bonds exceeds the energy released upon forming product bonds. Heat is absorbed from surroundings. Endothermic processes are thermodynamically unfavourable unless compensated by entropy increases.

2. The Entropy Drive ($\Delta S^\circ$)

Entropy ($S$) is a quantitative measure of the degree of randomness, molecular disorder, or energy dispersal among constituent particles within a system, measured in units of $\text{J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$. According to statistical mechanics, entropy increases as the number of available microstates ($\Omega$) for distributing energy increases.

$$\Delta S^\circ = \sum S^\circ(\text{products}) - \sum S^\circ(\text{reactants})$$
Physical / Chemical Transformation Sign of $\Delta S^\circ$ Microscopic Kinetic Explanation
Phase Changes: $\text{Solid} \rightarrow \text{Liquid} \rightarrow \text{Gas}$ $\Delta S^\circ > 0$ (Positive) Particles move from highly ordered crystal lattices into free translational, rotational, and vibrational gas motion, drastically increasing microstates.
Increase in Moles of Gas ($\Delta n_{\text{gas}} > 0$) $\Delta S^\circ > 0$ (Positive) Gas particles possess vastly greater spatial freedom than solids or liquids. Producing more gas moles expands system configuration entropy.
Decrease in Moles of Gas ($\Delta n_{\text{gas}} < 0$) $\Delta S^\circ < 0$ (Negative) Consolidating multiple free gas molecules into fewer structured liquid or solid species restricts spatial disorder.
Dissolution of Solid Solute ($\text{Solid} \rightarrow \text{Aqueous}$) $\Delta S^\circ > 0$ (Positive) Solute particles separate from rigid lattices and disperse throughout solvent molecules, increasing mixture disorder.
Thermal Heating ($\Delta T > 0$) $\Delta S^\circ > 0$ (Positive) Higher thermal kinetic energy spreads particles across a broader continuum of molecular velocity distributions.

3. Deep Dive 1: Hydrocarbon & Fuel Combustion

Exothermic & Entropy-Driven

Combustion is a rapid high-temperature exothermic redox reaction between a fuel (such as a hydrocarbon or alcohol) and an oxidant (typically atmospheric oxygen gas, $\mathrm{O_2}$), producing oxidised gaseous products ($\mathrm{CO_2}$ and $\mathrm{H_2O}$), light, and substantial thermal energy.

Chemical Model Equations

Consider two standard combustion systems studied in Stage 6 Chemistry:

$$\mathrm{CH_4(g) + 2O_2(g) \rightarrow CO_2(g) + 2H_2O(g)} \quad \Delta H^\circ = -802.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$ $$\mathrm{2C_8H_{18}(l) + 25O_2(g) \rightarrow 16CO_2(g) + 18H_2O(g)} \quad \Delta H^\circ = -10940\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$

Thermodynamic & Molecular Breakdown

1. Enthalpy Change ($\Delta H^\circ \ll 0$)

Combustion is intensely exothermic. The bond enthalpy absorbed to cleave relatively weak reactant bonds ($\text{C-H}$ single bonds at $\sim 414\text{ kJ/mol}$; $\text{O=O}$ double bonds at $\sim 498\text{ kJ/mol}$) is vastly smaller than the immense energy released upon synthesising double covalent $\text{C=O}$ bonds in $\mathrm{CO_2}$ ($\sim 804\text{ kJ/mol}$) and polar covalent $\text{O-H}$ bonds in $\mathrm{H_2O}$ ($\sim 464\text{ kJ/mol}$). This massive net surplus of energy is released as heat.

2. Entropy Change ($\Delta S^\circ > 0$)

Entropy increases during combustion due to two primary mechanisms:
• Molar Expansion ($\Delta n_{\text{gas}} > 0$): In liquid octane combustion, $27\text{ moles}$ of liquid/gas reactants yield $34\text{ moles}$ of high-temperature gaseous products ($\Delta n_{\text{gas}} = +9$), vastly expanding spatial microstates.
• Thermal Energy Dissipation: Enormous heat emission ($\Delta H^\circ \ll 0$) floods the surrounding air with thermal kinetic energy, increasing entropy of the surroundings ($\Delta S_{\text{surroundings}} = -\frac{\Delta H^\circ}{T} \gg 0$).

Stage 6 Energy Profile Diagram: Complete Combustion Reaction

Why Combustion Reactions Never Reach Dynamic Equilibrium

NESA examinations frequently test why combustion reactions proceed strictly to completion. Three definitive reasons account for this behaviour:

  1. Open System Mass Escape: Combustion typically occurs in open air (e.g., engines, bushfires, Bunsen burners). Gaseous products ($\mathrm{CO_2}$ and water vapour) escape into the room atmosphere, preventing product accumulation and reducing reverse collision rates to zero ($r_{\mathrm{reverse}} = 0$).
  2. Extreme Gibbs Free Energy Driving Force ($\Delta G^\circ \ll 0$): Because $\Delta H^\circ$ is heavily negative and $\Delta S^\circ$ is positive, $\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$ is immensely negative under all physical conditions, making the process highly exergonic and spontaneous.
  3. Insurmountable Reverse Activation Energy ($E_{a,\text{reverse}}$): The reverse reaction requires recombining $\mathrm{CO_2(g)}$ and $\mathrm{H_2O(g)}$ to reform methane and oxygen. The reverse activation energy barrier ($E_{a,\text{reverse}} = E_{a,\text{forward}} + \vert{}\Delta H^\circ\vert{}$) is extremely high. At room temperature, no gaseous collisions possess sufficient kinetic energy to overcome $E_{a,\text{reverse}}$.

4. Deep Dive 2: Photosynthesis in Autotrophs

Endothermic & Non-Spontaneous

Photosynthesis is the fundamental bio-energetic biochemical pathway whereby photoautotrophic organisms (such as plants and algae) convert light energy from solar photons into chemical energy stored within glucose molecules.

Chemical Model Equation

$$\mathrm{6CO_2(g) + 6H_2O(l) \xrightarrow{\text{light, chlorophyll}} C_6H_{12}O_6(aq) + 6O_2(g)} \quad \Delta H^\circ = +2803\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$

Thermodynamic & Molecular Breakdown

1. Enthalpy Change ($\Delta H^\circ \gg 0$)

Photosynthesis is strongly endothermic ($\Delta H^\circ = +2803\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$). Cleaving highly stable $\mathrm{C=O}$ double bonds in $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and $\text{O-H}$ bonds in water requires absorbing $2803\text{ kJ}$ of energy per mole of glucose synthesised. This energy is supplied by absorbing solar photons ($h\nu$) via chlorophyll pigments.

2. Entropy Change ($\Delta S^\circ < 0$)

Photosynthesis results in a significant decrease in system entropy ($\Delta S^\circ = -212\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$):
• Structural Ordering: 12 small fluid reactant molecules (6 gaseous $\mathrm{CO_2}$ + 6 liquid $\mathrm{H_2O}$) are organised into 1 large, highly structured aqueous glucose molecule ($\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}$) and 6 gas molecules.
• Restriction of Degrees of Freedom: Locking unbound carbon atoms into ring-structured hexose sugar chains severely reduces spatial and vibrational microstates ($\Delta S_{\text{system}}^\circ < 0$).

Stage 6 Energy Profile Diagram: Endothermic Photosynthesis Pathway

Why Photosynthesis is a Non-Equilibrium Steady-State System

With $\Delta H^\circ > 0$ and $\Delta S^\circ < 0$, standard Gibbs free energy change is positive ($\Delta G^\circ = +2866\text{ kJ/mol} > 0$), rendering photosynthesis thermodynamically non-spontaneous under isolated conditions. How does it occur continuously in plants without establishing equilibrium?

  1. Continuous Radiant Solar Coupling: Photosynthesis is driven by an external energy flux. Chlorophyll absorbs solar photons ($h\nu$), generating high-energy electrons that power ATP and NADPH synthesis, driving endergonic carbon fixation in the Calvin cycle.
  2. Biochemical Product Sinks: Synthesised glucose is immediately consumed or transformed. Plants rapidly polymerise glucose into insoluble starch (for storage) or cellulose (for cell walls), or consume it in cellular respiration ($\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \rightarrow 6CO_2 + 6H_2O}$). By continuously removing free aqueous glucose ($[\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}] \to 0$), the reaction is continually pulled forward, preventing reverse equilibrium.
  3. Multi-Step Enzymatic Control: Photosynthesis is mediated by biological catalysts (such as RuBisCO). These enzymes enforce unidirectional metabolic flux through regulated irreversible biochemical control steps.

5. Gibbs Free Energy ($\Delta G^\circ$) & Spontaneity Analysis

Mathematical Thermodynamics

The overall spontaneity of any chemical system at constant temperature ($T$, in Kelvin) and pressure is defined by the Gibbs Free Energy Equation:

$$\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$$

Essential Unit Conversion Warning!

In NESA examinations, $\Delta H^\circ$ is typically given in kilojoules per mole ($\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$), whereas $\Delta S^\circ$ is provided in joules per kelvin per mole ($\text{J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$).
You MUST convert $\Delta S^\circ$ to $\text{kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$ by dividing by 1000 before substituting into $\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$!
Additionally, temperature $T$ MUST be expressed in Kelvin: $T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15$.

Criteria for Reaction Spontaneity

  • $\Delta G^\circ < 0$ (Exergonic): The reaction is thermodynamically spontaneous in the forward direction under standard conditions.
  • $\Delta G^\circ > 0$ (Endergonic): The reaction is non-spontaneous in the forward direction. External energy input is required.
  • $\Delta G^\circ = 0$: The system is at dynamic equilibrium under standard state conditions.

The Four Thermodynamic Combinations Matrix

Case Enthalpy ($\Delta H^\circ$) Entropy ($\Delta S^\circ$) Gibbs Free Energy ($\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$) Spontaneity Condition Representative Example
1 $\Delta H^\circ < 0$ (Exothermic) $\Delta S^\circ > 0$ (Disorder increases) Always $\Delta G^\circ < 0$ (Negative) Spontaneous at all temperatures Combustion of fuels ($\mathrm{CH_4}, \mathrm{C_8H_{18}}$)
2 $\Delta H^\circ > 0$ (Endothermic) $\Delta S^\circ < 0$ (Disorder decreases) Always $\Delta G^\circ > 0$ (Positive) Non-spontaneous at all temperatures Photosynthesis (requires solar photon coupling)
3 $\Delta H^\circ < 0$ (Exothermic) $\Delta S^\circ < 0$ (Disorder decreases) $\Delta G^\circ < 0$ at low $T$; $\Delta G^\circ > 0$ at high $T$ Spontaneous at low temperatures Haber process forward step ($\mathrm{N_2 + 3H_2 \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3}$)
4 $\Delta H^\circ > 0$ (Endothermic) $\Delta S^\circ > 0$ (Disorder increases) $\Delta G^\circ > 0$ at low $T$; $\Delta G^\circ < 0$ at high $T$ Spontaneous at high temperatures Thermal decomposition of $\mathrm{CaCO_3(s) \rightarrow CaO(s) + CO_2(g)}$

Temperature Threshold for Spontaneity Switch ($\Delta G^\circ = 0$)

For systems where $\Delta H^\circ$ and $\Delta S^\circ$ share the same algebraic sign (Cases 3 and 4), setting $\Delta G^\circ = 0$ allows us to calculate the exact threshold temperature ($T_{\text{threshold}}$) above or below which spontaneity changes:

$$0 = \Delta H^\circ - T_{\text{threshold}}\Delta S^\circ \implies T_{\text{threshold}} = \frac{\Delta H^\circ}{\Delta S^\circ}$$

HSC Step-by-Step Solutions

Worked Example 1 (Easy): Classifying Thermodynamic Driving Forces

2 Marks

Classify the signs of enthalpy change ($\Delta H^\circ$) and entropy change ($\Delta S^\circ$) for the complete combustion of liquid ethanol: $$\mathrm{C_2H_5OH(l) + 3O_2(g) \rightarrow 2CO_2(g) + 3H_2O(g)}$$

Model Solution:

1. Enthalpy Change ($\Delta H^\circ < 0$): The reaction is highly exothermic because ethanol combustion releases significant thermal energy as strong product covalent bonds ($\text{C=O}$ and $\text{O-H}$) are formed.

2. Entropy Change ($\Delta S^\circ > 0$): The reaction experiences a net increase in entropy. The reactants contain $3\text{ moles}$ of gas and $1\text{ mole}$ of liquid ($3\text{ moles of gas total}$), while the products consist of $5\text{ moles}$ of high-temperature gas ($2\text{CO}_2 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$). Because $\Delta n_{\text{gas}} = +2$, spatial microstates increase ($\Delta S^\circ > 0$).

Worked Example 2 (Medium): Quantitative $\Delta G^\circ$ & Temperature Calculations

3 Marks

Propane gas undergoes complete combustion according to: $$\mathrm{C_3H_8(g) + 5O_2(g) \rightarrow 3CO_2(g) + 4H_2O(g)} \quad \Delta H^\circ = -2043\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$ Given that $\Delta S^\circ = +100.5\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$:
a) Calculate the standard Gibbs free energy change ($\Delta G^\circ$) at $298.15\text{ K}$.
b) State with justification whether this reaction is spontaneous at room temperature.

Model Solution:

a) Calculation:

Step 1: Convert entropy change from $\text{J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$ to $\text{kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$:

$$\Delta S^\circ = \frac{+100.5\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}}{1000} = +0.1005\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$$

Step 2: Substitute into Gibbs Free Energy equation:

$$\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$$ $$\Delta G^\circ = -2043\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} - (298.15\text{ K} \times +0.1005\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1})$$ $$\Delta G^\circ = -2043 - 29.96 = -2072.96\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$

b) Spontaneity Verdict:

Because $\Delta G^\circ = -2073\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} < 0$, the reaction is highly exergonic and thermodynamically spontaneous in the forward direction at $25^\circ\text{C}$.

Worked Example 3 (Hard): Photosynthesis Thermodynamic & Biochemical Non-Equilibrium Analysis

4 Marks

The overall equation for photosynthesis is: $$\mathrm{6CO_2(g) + 6H_2O(l) \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6(aq) + 6O_2(g)} \quad \Delta H^\circ = +2803\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}, \ \Delta S^\circ = -212\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$$ a) Calculate $\Delta G^\circ$ at $298\text{ K}$.
b) Explain why photosynthesis occurs continuously in living plants despite the sign of $\Delta G^\circ$, and state why it never reaches dynamic chemical equilibrium.

Model Solution:

a) Calculation:

$$\Delta S^\circ = \frac{-212}{1000} = -0.212\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$$ $$\Delta G^\circ = +2803\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} - [298\text{ K} \times (-0.212\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1})]$$ $$\Delta G^\circ = +2803 - (-63.176) = +2866.18\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$

b) Biological Non-Equilibrium Explanation:

Because $\Delta G^\circ = +2866\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} \gg 0$, photosynthesis is non-spontaneous in isolated systems. However, plants continuously couple the reaction to an external energy source by absorbing solar photons ($h\nu$) via chlorophyll pigments. Furthermore, photosynthesis fails to reach dynamic equilibrium because glucose product is rapidly polymerised into insoluble starch/cellulose or consumed in respiration ($[\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}] \to 0$), continually driving the reaction forward as a non-equilibrium steady state.

Worked Example 4 (HSC Extension Challenge): Calorimetry, Stoichiometry, and Thermodynamic Irreversibility

5 Marks

A student burnt $0.922\text{ g}$ of liquid ethanol ($\mathrm{C_2H_5OH(l)}$, $M = 46.07\text{ g mol}^{-1}$) in a spirit burner to heat $200.0\text{ g}$ of water in a copper calorimeter from $21.5^\circ\text{C}$ to $48.0^\circ\text{C}$. $$\mathrm{C_2H_5OH(l) + 3O_2(g) \rightarrow 2CO_2(g) + 3H_2O(g)}$$
a) Calculate the experimental enthalpy of combustion ($\Delta H^\circ$) in $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ (assuming no heat loss, $c_{\text{water}} = 4.18\text{ J g}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$).
b) Using the theoretical literature enthalpy of combustion ($\Delta H^\circ = -1367\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$) and theoretical entropy change ($\Delta S^\circ = +217.6\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$), calculate the standard Gibbs free energy change ($\Delta G^\circ$) at $298.15\text{ K}$.
c) Using collision theory, system boundaries, and activation energy concepts, explain why ethanol combustion in an open spirit burner is an irreversible non-equilibrium process.

Model Solution:

a) Experimental Calorimetry Calculation:

$$q = m c \Delta T = 200.0\text{ g} \times 4.18\text{ J g}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1} \times (48.0 - 21.5)^\circ\text{C} = 22154\text{ J} = 22.154\text{ kJ}$$ $$n(\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}) = \frac{m}{M} = \frac{0.922\text{ g}}{46.07\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.02001\text{ mol}$$ $$\Delta H^\circ = -\frac{q}{n} = -\frac{22.154\text{ kJ}}{0.02001\text{ mol}} = -1107\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$

b) Theoretical Gibbs Free Energy Calculation:

$$\Delta S^\circ = \frac{+217.6\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}}{1000} = +0.2176\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$$ $$\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ = -1367\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} - (298.15\text{ K} \times +0.2176\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1})$$ $$\Delta G^\circ = -1367 - 64.88 = -1431.88\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} \quad (\text{or } -1432\text{ kJ mol}^{-1})$$

c) Non-Equilibrium Explanation:

1. System Boundary: In an open spirit burner, gaseous products ($\mathrm{CO_2}$ and $\mathrm{H_2O}$ vapour) escape freely into the atmosphere ($\Delta m < 0$). Without product gases in the reaction zone, reverse collision frequency drops to zero ($r_{\text{reverse}} = 0$).
2. Activation Energy: Because combustion is intensely exothermic ($\Delta H^\circ = -1367\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$), the reverse activation energy barrier ($E_{a,\text{reverse}} = E_{a,\text{forward}} + \vert{}\Delta H^\circ\vert{}$) is insurmountable at ambient temperatures. No product molecules possess sufficient kinetic energy to overcome $E_{a,\text{reverse}}$, ensuring complete irreversibility.

Question 3 (Hard): Quantitative $\Delta G^\circ$ and Threshold Temperature Calculation

4 Marks

The decomposition of calcium carbonate occurs according to: $$\mathrm{CaCO_3(s) \rightleftharpoons CaO(s) + CO_2(g)}$$ Given $\Delta H^\circ = +178.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ and $\Delta S^\circ = +160.5\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$:
a) Calculate $\Delta G^\circ$ at $298.15\text{ K}$ and explain why calcium carbonate is stable at room temperature.
b) Calculate the minimum temperature (in $^\circ\text{C}$) required for the decomposition to become spontaneous ($\Delta G^\circ \le 0$).

Hint: Convert $\Delta S^\circ$ to $\text{kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$. Set $\Delta G^\circ = 0$ to find $T_{\text{threshold}} = \frac{\Delta H^\circ}{\Delta S^\circ}$, then subtract $273.15$ to get $^\circ\text{C}$.

Model Answer & Rubric

a) Calculation at 298.15 K:

$$\Delta S^\circ = \frac{+160.5\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}}{1000} = +0.1605\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$$ $$\Delta G^\circ = +178.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} - (298.15\text{ K} \times +0.1605\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1})$$ $$\Delta G^\circ = +178.3 - 47.85 = +130.45\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$$

Because $\Delta G^\circ = +130.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} > 0$, the reaction is endergonic and non-spontaneous at room temperature, making limestone stable.

b) Minimum Spontaneity Temperature:

$$\text{Set } \Delta G^\circ = 0 \implies \Delta H^\circ - T_{\text{threshold}}\Delta S^\circ = 0$$ $$T_{\text{threshold}} = \frac{\Delta H^\circ}{\Delta S^\circ} = \frac{178.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}{0.1605\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}} = 1110.9\text{ K}$$ $$T(^\circ\text{C}) = 1110.9\text{ K} - 273.15 = 837.75^\circ\text{C} \quad (\text{or } 838^\circ\text{C})$$

Hint: Structure your response into two distinct sections: (1) Combustion thermodynamics & open boundaries, and (2) Photosynthesis endergonic nature, photon coupling, and metabolic sinks.

Model Answer & Rubric

Model Answer:

Combustion and photosynthesis represent thermodynamic extremes of non-equilibrium systems:

1. Combustion Systems: Combustion is strongly exothermic ($\Delta H^\circ < 0$) and exhibits positive entropy ($\Delta S^\circ > 0$) due to gas production ($\Delta n_{\text{gas}} > 0$). Substituting into $\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$ yields an immensely negative $\Delta G^\circ \ll 0$ at all temperatures, making the reaction heavily exergonic and spontaneous. In natural open systems, escaping gaseous products drop reverse rates to zero ($r_{\text{reverse}} = 0$). Furthermore, the reverse activation energy barrier ($E_{a,\text{reverse}}$) is insurmountable at room temperature, driving the reaction irreversibly to 100% completion.

2. Photosynthesis Systems: Photosynthesis is endothermic ($\Delta H^\circ > 0$) and involves an entropy decrease ($\Delta S^\circ < 0$) as small gas/liquid molecules are structured into glucose. This yields $\Delta G^\circ \gg 0$, making it non-spontaneous in isolated systems. Photosynthesis operates as a non-equilibrium steady state because it is continually driven by absorbing solar light energy ($h\nu$). Additionally, plants continuously consume or polymerise glucose into starch and cellulose ($[\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}] \to 0$), removing products and preventing reverse equilibrium.
